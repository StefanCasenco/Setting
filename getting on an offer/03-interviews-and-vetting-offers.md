# 03 · Setter interviews, and how to tell a good offer from a bad one

Two jobs here: **win the interview**, and **don't end up on a dead offer.** A bad offer looks like this from the inside: almost no conversations per day despite plenty of leads, training calls that keep getting cancelled, a closer who doesn't show up to hot leads, and an "OTE" that's mathematically impossible. Ask the right questions *before* you join.

---

## Part 1: What they'll ask you, and strong answers

Keep answers short: 20–40 seconds. Give one example, one number, and stop. Same rule as a setting call: don't over-talk.

**"Tell me about yourself."**
> "I'm Stefan, from Moldova, based in Europe. I've spent the last [X] weeks training full-time to set, with [number] recorded roleplays, all reviewed and scored, coached by Scott Clancy, inside Jordan Davidson's Just Do Sales. I'm looking for an offer where I can dial hard, book qualified calls, and get better every week."

**"You don't have experience. Why should we hire you?"**
> "Fair. What I do have is [number] reviewed roleplays and a scorecard I run on every call. And when I found I was going in circles, I fixed it with my coach in two weeks. I'm coachable and I put in the volume. Give me your script and two weeks, and judge me on the numbers."

**"Your English, will prospects understand you?"**
> "I've had that on calls. *(smile)* Most of the time it's their signal, not my English. When someone asks where I'm from, I tell them, I make a joke about Moldovan wine, and we keep going. If anything, it makes me listen harder." *(See call 029 in `phone calls 2`.)*

**"What's the job of a setter?"**
> "Book qualified calls, and protect the closer's calendar from the ones that aren't. Get the prospect to say their situation, the impact, the goal and what's stopping them, in their own words, so the closer has everything before the call starts."

**"How do you handle 'how much is it?'"**
> "I don't quote price. 'It depends on what you need, and that's what [closer]'s call is for.' If they ask three times, I name it: 'I can tell this is really bugging you.' I give them a little, 'it's an investment, not a fifty-dollar course', and promise the closer will go through it straight."

**"How do you handle someone who says they need to talk to their partner?"**
> "I ask early: 'Is this a decision you'd make on your own, or with someone?' If it's with someone, they go on the closer's call together. I'd rather book both than book one and get a 'my wife said no' afterwards."

**"Walk me through how you'd qualify someone financially."**
> "After urgency, never before. For a capital offer: goal, current income, how much they're able to put aside each month, tight month or usual, total saved, and 'it takes money to make money' if they push back. For a program offer, I go through income or what they already spend, then 'is investing in yourself something you'd be in a position to do right now?'. Students, unemployed and low earners get the transparent line early: 'mid four-figure investment, is that viable for you right now, or completely out of the question?'"

**"When would you disqualify someone?"**
> "Fit: under 18, or outside the market. Financial: they can't invest without hurting themselves. Interest: a temp check of 5 or below with no reason, or 'I'm happy, I just clicked the ad.' And I tell them kindly, and give them something free, so they come back when they're ready."

**"What would you do on day one?"**
> "Watch the owner's content until I sound like them, read every script and objection doc, listen to the closer's best calls, and shadow the top setter's dials. Then dial."

**"Do a live roleplay with me."** *(Most setter interviews include this.)*
- Ask 10 seconds of context first: "Is this an inbound application, or an old lead? What's the offer?"
- Run the same seven stages as always. **Don't rush to the booking to look good.** They're testing discovery.
- If they play hard: stay calm, agree with what's true, ask the next question. If they say they're happy and don't need it, **disqualify them out loud.** That impresses hiring managers more than forcing a booking.

## Part 2: Questions YOU ask them (this is how you vet the offer)

Ask these near the end. A good offer answers easily. A bad one gets vague.

**Leads**
1. "How many new leads come in per day, and where from: ads, organic, webinars?"
2. "How many conversations does a setter have on a normal day?" *(If it's something like 38 leads a day and only 3 real conversations, that's a red flag.)*
3. "What's the show rate and close rate on booked calls right now?"

**Money**
4. "What's the commission structure, and is there a base?"
5. "What does your top setter make a month, and what does a new setter make in month one?" *(Do the maths: OTE ÷ 12 ÷ your commission % = the monthly sales you'd need. If that's far above what the whole team closes, walk away.)*
6. "When does commission pay: on booked, on showed, or on closed?"

**Support**
7. "Who trains me, and how often? Are there call reviews?"
8. "Who are the closers, and how many?" *(One closer who no-shows is your income disappearing.)*

**Fit**
9. "What time zone are the leads, and what hours do you need me?"
10. "Why did the last setter leave?"

**Green flags:** they answer with numbers. They have a script and an objection doc. They do call reviews. The closers have a calendar that fills.
**Red flags:** "it's uncapped!" with no numbers, commission-only with low lead flow, "we're just getting started with ads", you have to buy their course to join, no closer review process, or they dodge "why did the last setter leave?".

## Part 3: Where to find offers

- **Setter Syndicate job board,** and any job board inside the communities you're in. Scott mentioned it.
- **Jordan's network and JDS:** ask in the community who's hiring. Post your Loom there.
- **Offer owners' content:** find coaches in niches you know (trading, ecom, fitness, high-ticket) with ads running. If they're running ads, they need setters. DM them with a one-line hook and your Loom.
- **Your roleplay partners who are on offers** (George, Kevin): ask for an intro to their manager.

**DM template to an offer owner** (short, no paragraphs):
> "Hey [name], love your content on [specific thing]. I'm a phone setter, [number] reviewed roleplays, coached by Scott Clancy. 90-second Loom here: [link]. Open to setters?"

Follow up once after 3 days: *"Hey [name], just bumping this. Even a 'not right now' helps."*
