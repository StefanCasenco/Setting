# 02 · Your Loom: scripts that get watched past 30 seconds

Built from Scott's Loom review on the Syndicate call (4 Nov): Steyn's 4-minute Loom, and the Loom Scott hired Kevin from. Atefur's slideshow advice (#5) and your own draft from that call are in here too.

---

## Scott's rules (from the call)

1. **60–120 seconds**, unless the job post asks for longer. *"If I'm not engaged in the first 30 seconds, I'm not watching the rest."* He's hired over 100 reps.
2. **Hook first.** Your strongest thing goes in the first 30 seconds. Steyn buried **$200K cash collected** at 3:30, so nobody would ever hear it.
3. **It's a sales pitch, not a CV.** *"Don't list everything you've done since you came out of the womb."* Skip the jobs that don't connect to the offer.
4. **No experience? Own it, then show effort and character.** *"I've invested X into training, done X roleplays, here's what I understand, here's who I am."*
5. **Conviction beats polish.** About Kevin's Loom: *"It's not a 10 out of 10, he's not the most natural, but he has so much conviction… that he's going to be the hardest working person on the team."*
6. **Tie it to their offer.** Name the offer, and say why *this* one.
7. **Assume the interview.** End with a clear next step.
8. **Three takes, max. Send the best one.** *"If you spend 18 takes on one Loom, you could've sent 18 applications."* It's a volume game. Sending 20 Looms that are 30% worse beats sending one perfect one.
9. **Title it yourself,** with a hook, not "Loom recording 4 Nov". For example: *"Why I'll be your hardest-working setter, Stefan"*.
10. **Energy.** Steyn looked exhausted at 3:30am on take 18. Record when you're fresh. Sit still (Pippa noticed his chair moving).

**Atefur's add (#5):** *"Every kid and their mom sits in front of the camera talking. Imagine a Loom where someone shows you what they've done, with proof."* So use a **slideshow behind you**: your Fathom roleplay list, your community, your mentors, your scorecard. 85% of applicants won't bother, so it makes you stand out. But still talk to camera with conviction. Don't read the slides.

## Your draft from Atefur #5 (what to keep, what to cut)

> *"In this video, I'll cover two things. Who I am and why I'll be the best setter. Before we dive in, I come from JDS, run by the man himself, Jordan Davidson."*

- **"I'll cover two things"** wastes 5 seconds. Cut it. Just start.
- **JDS / Jordan** is good social proof. Keep it, but put it after the hook, not as the hook.

> *"My goal is to eventually get into closing, but first I want to prove myself as a setter."*

- **Cut it** (Atefur: *"Don't say that"*). A hiring manager hears: "he'll leave the setter seat as soon as he can."

> *"Over the last few weeks I've done 40 roleplays on trading-specific prospects. Early on every call was all over the place… I reviewed all my calls and the problem wasn't the prospect, it was me. I had no structure. I took that to Scott… since then every call has had the same structure."*

- **This is your hook.** It's a real story: a problem, you owning it, fixing it with a mentor, and the result. That's exactly what Scott means by showing character instead of listing jobs. **Move it to the first 20 seconds**, and make it shorter.

## Script A: no experience yet, general (≈ 90 seconds)

> **[0–20s, hook]**
> "Hey [owner's name], Stefan here. In the last [X] weeks I've done over [number] recorded setting roleplays, and I've reviewed every single one. Here they are."
> *(slide: a screenshot of your Fathom list, all the roleplay titles)*
>
> **[20–45s, the story]**
> "Early on, every call went in circles. Different prospect, same problem. When I reviewed them, the problem wasn't the prospects, it was me: I had no structure. So I took it to my coach, Scott Clancy, and rebuilt how I run every call. Now every call follows the same seven stages, and I score every one of them."
> *(slide: your scorecard from 01-your-calls-review.md)*
>
> **[45–70s, who's behind you, and who you are]**
> "I'm part of Jordan Davidson's Just Do Sales, I train with Scott, and I do roleplays daily with setters who are already on offers. English is my second language, I'm from Moldova, so I've learned to listen harder than most people talk. *(small smile)* And I'm based in Europe, so US evenings are my afternoons."
> *(slide: JDS and Scott's Instagram, with logos)*
>
> **[70–90s, why you, and the next step]**
> "I'm not here to fill a seat. I'm here to put qualified calls on [closer/owner]'s calendar and protect it from the ones that aren't. I'd love to jump on a call and show you how I'd handle your leads. My details are below. Looking forward to it."

**Notes:**
- Replace every [bracket] with a real number. "Over 40" beats "a lot".
- The **"listen harder than most people talk"** line turns your second language into a strength. Say it with a smile. Only keep it if it feels true to you.
- **"Protect it from the ones that aren't"** shows you understand disqualifying (Scott). Most applicants only talk about booking.

## Script B: a trading offer (≈ 75 seconds)

> **[hook]** "Hey [name], Stefan here. I've done [number] trading-specific setting roleplays: blown prop accounts, revenge traders, guys burnt by signal groups, people who 'just saw the ad.' Here's the list."
> *(slide: Fathom list, trading ones highlighted)*
>
> **[proof of understanding]** "What I've learned is that most trading leads don't have a strategy problem, they have a discipline problem. They know the setups and break their own rules on a Friday. My job is to find that, in their words, before the call ever reaches you."
>
> **[why you, and why this offer]** "I've been watching [owner]'s content for [time]. [One specific thing you like, e.g. 'the way you teach risk first']. That's the kind of offer I can set with a straight face."
>
> **[close]** "I'm in Europe, so US afternoons and evenings are my working hours. Let's jump on a call. I'll show you how I'd run one of your leads live."

## Script C: a program offer (high-ticket sales, AI agency, fitness, coaching) (≈ 75 seconds)

> **[hook]** "Hey [name], Stefan here. I've run [number] recorded setting roleplays. And the thing I'm proudest of isn't the ones I booked, it's the ones I *didn't*: the 17-year-old, the guy with $300, the guy who 'just clicked the ad.' Your closer's calendar should only have people who can actually buy."
>
> **[process]** "Every call I run goes through the same seven stages. Situation, impact, goal, roadblock, urgency, money, booking. And the money questions come only after they've told me why they need to change, so they don't feel like a credit check."
>
> **[why this offer]** "[One specific line about their offer or content.] I want to set for something I believe in, and this is it."
>
> **[close]** "I'd love to show you on a call. My details are below."

## Slide deck for the Loom (5 slides, keep it simple)

1. **Title:** "Stefan Cașenco: why I'll be your hardest-working setter"
2. **Proof:** a screenshot of your Fathom list (scroll it, or make a collage). Caption: "[number] recorded roleplays in [weeks]"
3. **Process:** the 7 stages as one line each, and your scorecard
4. **Who's behind me:** JDS (Jordan Davidson), Scott Clancy, the roleplay partners currently on offers
5. **Next step:** your email, phone, time zone and hours available ("US hours: 3pm–2am my time")

Use Canva or Google Slides with one font and no animations. The slides are proof, not a presentation. You still talk to camera.

## Before you hit record

- Fresh, not at 3am. Light in front of you, not behind.
- Camera at eye level. Sit still.
- Read your script out loud five times, then **don't read it while recording**. Glance at bullet points if you need to.
- **Three takes. Send the best.** Move on to the next application.
- Title the Loom yourself. Paste the link into the application with one line: *"90-second Loom here, the first 20 seconds will tell you if I'm worth a call."*

## Volume target

**5 applications a day, every weekday, for two weeks.** That's 50. Scott: it's a volume game. Track them in a simple sheet: offer, owner, date sent, Loom link, reply (yes/no), interview date.
