## The 10 habits to fix (most important first)

### 1. You answer for them: "So you're more after X, right?"
- K1 14:10: *"So you're more after the time freedom, right?"*
- K1 15:05: *"So you're more after the guidance?"*
- K2 14:38: *"Okay, so you're more after the right guidance, right?"*
- K4 19:04: *"And with trading, you'd be able to have that freedom and be your own boss, right?"*

**Why it costs you:** Scott: *"You don't get points for knowing what they're thinking. Force them to say it."* When you say it, they just say "yeah". When they say it, they hear themselves, and you can use their words in the booking.

**Instead:**
- *"What do you think it'd take for you to actually get there?"* (they'll say guidance in their own words)
- *"So what does that give you that you don't have right now?"* (they'll say freedom)
- If you want to confirm, repeat their own words with no new label: *"So, 'not knowing what to do.' Is that the main thing?"*

**Drill:** Take five of your old lines that start with "So you're more after…". Rewrite each as an open question. Say them out loud, ten times each.

### 2. You give them the answers to pick from
- K5 20:37: *"Don't you understand it, or do you say there's a mindset problem, or what's happening?"*
- K5 22:28: *"The inconsistency with the mindset, or what's the problem?"*
- K6 17:17: *"Do you like it? Do you hate it?"*
- K8 19:51: *"What's the goal? Quit the job? Do it on the side?"*

**Why it costs you:** they pick one of your options instead of thinking. You get the answer you suggested, not the real one.

**Instead:** stop after the first question. *"Tell me more about that structure. What's happening?"* … silence. *"How do you feel about the job?"* … silence. *"And once you hit 5k, what's the plan?"* … silence.

**Drill:** Record yourself asking ten open questions. Every time you add "or…", stop, delete it, and say the question again.

### 3. You skip the moment when they show you real pain
- K4 19:02: Kevin says he **missed his best friend's wedding abroad** and *"I created a business that can't function without me."* You went straight to *"With trading, you'd have that freedom, right?"* Kevin's feedback: *"You could have dug a bit deeper. Oh, you missed your friend's wedding?"*
- K2 13:52: *"I'm not happy, but what else am I meant to do, I gotta provide."* You said "for sure" and moved to timeframe.
- K4 24:43: *"It made me realise I'm my own slave."* That line was worth a whole call.

**Why it costs you:** Scott: *one* real pain is enough, the North Star. You found it in K4 and walked past it.

**Instead:** when they say something heavy, slow down. Repeat it back, then ask one impact question:
- *"You missed your best friend's wedding. How did that feel?"*
- *"'What else am I meant to do.' How long have you felt like that?"*
- *"Your own slave. What do you mean?"*
Then **stop and let it sit.** Atefur: *"Not good enough? What do you mean?"*

**Drill:** Ask Kevin to drop one heavy line in a roleplay without warning. Your only job is to catch it and ask two follow-ups before you move on.

### 4. Money comes too early, before you know their situation
- K4 17:15: you asked *"how much have you made in the last two, three months… how much do you have set aside to invest?"* before you knew anything about his life. Kevin: *"A bit aggressive… you could have probed into asking me how much I make first."*
- K5 22:55: capital came straight after the problem, before goal, why-now or urgency.

**Why it costs you:** money questions with no pain behind them feel like a credit check. Atefur: *"If a guy's fighting you on money, you've done something wrong earlier in the call."*

**Instead:** current situation (and impact) → goal → roadblock → why now → urgency → **then** money → pitch. And for a business owner, start the money questions with income: *"What's the business bringing in for you these days?"* before *"how much have you set aside?"*

### 5. You pitch the product, and nearly quote prices
- K5 22:55: *"What we do is some bigger swing trades, weeks to months, and you need capital for that."*
- K6 26:18–26:56: *"How much would you have set aside for the mentorship?"* → Kevin: *"How much does it usually cost?"* → you: *"Anywhere from three to seven grand."*

**Why it costs you:** Atefur (#5): *"Steer away from the fact that they'll have to get their card out. If they believe the call is to sell them a course, what's the likelihood they show up?"* The moment you give a range, the closer's call becomes a price negotiation.

**Instead:**
- Never describe strategies or features. *"That's exactly what [closer] goes through."*
- Never give a number. *"It depends on what you need, and I don't know that yet. That's what [closer]'s call is for."*
- For program offers, check they can invest without mentioning price: *"If [closer] finds the right fit, is investing in yourself something you'd be in a position to do right now?"*

### 6. Your pitch is generic
- K1 16:28: *"He can get a bit more in depth, help you build a roadmap on how he can get you to your goal."*
- K2 17:19: *"…because I have another call after this."* Kevin: *"The pitch, you need to improve that."*

**Instead:** Scott's four parts:
1. **Recap in their words:** situation, impact, goal, roadblock.
2. **Bridge:** "That's exactly what [owner]'s team helps with."
3. **Value:** "He'll look at [their specific thing] and map out a plan."
4. **Two times, assumptive:** "Tomorrow afternoon or Thursday?"

Example rebuilt from K4: *"So you're a wedding photographer, the business has dropped from five or six K to two to four, and you missed your best friend's wedding abroad because you'd built a business that can't run without you. You called yourself your own slave. You want five or six K from trading so photography can go back to being a hobby. And what you said you need is to know the right steps so you don't lose money. Next step is a Zoom with Jack, one of our head coaches. He'll map out a plan for your situation. Does tomorrow afternoon work, or Thursday?"*

### 7. "Like… I guess… like" before almost every question
- K8 18:07: *"So tell me, man, like, what, I guess, like, have you interested in starting to, wanting to do e-com in the first place?"*
- K8 18:35: *"And I guess, like, what would you say you do at the moment, like, worldwide?"* (you meant work-wise)
- K8 20:52: a 60-word speech about money being a tool before the question.

**Why it costs you:** it makes you sound unsure, and in your second language it makes the question harder to follow. Scott: *over-talking takes the edge off the question.*

**Instead:** learn **five questions by heart, word for word**, so they come out clean under pressure:
1. *"What got you interested in [X] in the first place?"*
2. *"What do you do for work at the moment?"*
3. *"How's that been affecting you?"*
4. *"What do you think it'd take for you to get there?"*
5. *"On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is this for you?"*

**Drill:** record yourself asking those five. Listen back and count every "like" and "I guess". Do it again until it's zero.

### 8. You accept "I just saw the ad" and move on
- K6 21:35: *"What changed now?"* "Just a good ad." → you moved to roadblock.
- K8 21:54: *"Why now?"* "I just saw the ad." → you moved on.
- (And in the Syndicate call you *played* this prospect, and watched George get stuck on it.)

**Instead (Scott and Atefur):**
- *"Fair. And how long have you felt [their word] about the job?"* → *"So what's different now, compared to [that long] ago?"*
- Atefur #5: *"When's 'just now'? … How long ago was that? … What shifted for you when you saw it?"*
- Then urgency: *"On a scale of 1 to 10…"*. A 4 with "just saw an ad" is an interest DQ, and that's a fine outcome.

### 9. You almost never run the temp check
In none of the Kevin calls did you ask the 1–10 question or "what happens if nothing changes in six months?". That's Scott's step 6, and it's where the urgency comes from.

**Instead:** after why-now, every time:
- *"On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it for you to [their goal]?"*
- *"What makes it a [number]?"* (they sell themselves)
- *"What's holding it back from a 9 or 10?"* (the hidden objection, to flag for the closer)
- 6 or below: *"How come?"* → binary challenge: *"Is this something you want to change now, or something you'll keep kicking down the road?"*

### 10. You book prospects who should be disqualified
- K8: Kevin played a retail manager who *loves his job*, has flexible hours, "not really big into materialistic stuff", "just saw the ad", two months interested. That's a textbook interest DQ, and you went to money and booked.
- K7 (trading): the guy at work who "downloaded stuff half-interested", "might" use free apps. You were right to stop ("I don't want to pitch"). Say it out loud to the prospect next time, kindly.

**Instead:** Scott: *your job isn't to book as many as possible, it's to book qualified ones.* Script the exit:
*"Honestly, from what you've told me, you like your job, there's no rush, and you're just having a look. That's a great spot to be in. I don't think booking you in makes sense right now. If that changes, give me a shout."*
