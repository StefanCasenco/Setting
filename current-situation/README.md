# CURRENT SITUATION: the playbook

The only freestyle part of the call. Everything else is fixed (see `../SETTING-SCRIPT.md`).

## 1. What CURRENT is for

You leave with their pain **in their words**, **one feeling**, **one cost**, ideally **one scene** from their week.

- **Tactical** = "I keep blowing prop accounts." A problem with the method.
- **Deep** = what's under it: time, freedom, family, feeling behind.
- You are not collecting job facts. Job, hours, money come up *inside* their story, casually.

## 2. The layers (one imagined prospect: Dan, 34, warehouse, wants "more money")

| Layer | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Surface | "I just want more money." |
| L2 | What it means | "What do you mean, more?" → "I'm tired of living paycheck to paycheck." |
| L3 | A scene | "Walk me through a normal week." → "Six days at the warehouse, Sunday I just sleep." |
| L4 | Personal impact | "What's the hardest part of that?" → "My daughter asks me to play and I'm too wrecked." |
| L5 | Cost of staying | "Where does that go if nothing changes?" → "She grows up and I missed it." |

Aim for L3-L4. Stop earlier if they don't open up.

## 3. Twelve techniques

**1. Echo a word.** *When:* they use a vivid word. *Why:* shows you heard; they expand. *Lines:* "Stuck?" · "A cycle, huh." · "Behind." *Failure:* echoing every sentence; it turns into a parrot.

**2. "What do you mean?"** *When:* any vague or loaded answer. *Why:* open, no judgement; "give me an example" gets a logical answer. *Lines:* "What do you mean by stuck?" · "How do you mean, more?" · "What do you mean, the routine?" *Failure:* asking it three times in a row on the same word.

**3. Scene.** *When:* L2 is clear but abstract. *Why:* scenes carry feeling. *Lines:* "Walk me through a normal week." · "What does a Sunday look like?" · "Take yesterday, how did it go?" *Failure:* asking for a scene before there is any thread.

**4. The motivation question.** *When:* tactical only, or they like their job. *Why:* gets to the real reason. *Lines:* "I know you want to get better at trading, but what's the real motivation for even trying it?" · "Sure, most people want more money. What's the real reason for you?" *Failure:* asking it as the very first question after INTENT when they already gave a feeling.

**5. Guess out loud.** *When:* one-liners. *Why:* easier to correct a guess than to produce an answer. *Lines:* "Sounds like time matters more than the money?" · "Kind of feels like you're running in place?" *Failure:* guessing something loaded they didn't hint at.

**6. Two-option question.** *When:* closed or guarded. *Why:* low effort to answer. *Lines:* "More about the money, or more about the time?" · "Is it the job, or what the job takes from you?" *Failure:* options that are both wrong. Leave room for "neither".

**7. "What's the hardest part of that?"** *When:* they show emotion or a cost. *Why:* human, not therapy-speak. *Lines:* "What's the hardest part of that?" · "What's the worst part of it?" *Failure:* firing it after every answer.

**8. The reason line.** *When:* "why are you asking?" *Lines:* "I'm not asking just for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, like a personal trainer would." *Failure:* sounding defensive; say it flat and keep going.

**9. Tactical to emotional analogy.** *When:* they think the problem is only the method. *Why:* pain drives change; someone with a health scare doesn't care about diet tips, they care about their kids. *Lines:* "Sure the strategy matters, but what's it all for?" *Failure:* lecturing.

**10. Silence.** *When:* after something heavy or true. *Why:* people fill it with the real thing. *Lines:* (say nothing, three seconds). *Failure:* filling it with "I hear you".

**11. Softened "as you want to be".** *When:* big or status goals. *Lines:* "What makes you feel like you're not as free as you want to be right now?" *Why:* uses their word, not defensive. *Failure:* swapping in "why aren't you".

**12. Stop and move on.** *When:* two tries, still surface. *Why:* forcing it breaks trust; the rest of the call fills the gap. *Lines:* "Got it man." then RECAP with what you have. *Failure:* digging a third time.

## 4. Exit test

You can say their pain in their words with one feeling and one cost. Example: "stuck in the cycle, drained, no time with my daughter." Then go to RECAP. Don't dig after this.

## 5. Don'ts

- No "why aren't you / why haven't you" here.
- Don't assume the job is the problem.
- No "what would that look like" before the pain has landed.
- Don't re-dig once you have the pain.
- Don't ask why they're *still* stuck (closer's job).
- Don't say "can you give me an example". Say "what do you mean?".
- Don't copy anyone's lines. Keep the principle, say it your way.
- No "how does that make you feel".

## 6. If you only remember five things

1. Echo their word, then ask "what do you mean?"
2. One question per turn, two short sentences max.
3. Go from fact to scene to what it does to them.
4. Two tries at the surface, then move on.
5. Recap the deep goal, not the tactical one.
