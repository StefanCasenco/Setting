# 01 · Openers: the first 60 seconds

The opener has one job: get them talking about **themselves** without their guard going up.
Three things kill a call in the first minute:

1. **Sounding like you're reading.** Fix: short lines, their name, a pause.
2. **A yes/no question they can say "no" to.** "Are you free for a chat?" → "Nah, I'm busy." Fix: say what the call is, then "sound fair?"
3. **A big question too early.** "What's going on in your life that made you look for support?" in the first ten seconds feels like therapy. Fix: one easy question first, then the deeper one.

---

## Your lines, checked

### Inbound (they messaged you / reached out)

> hey [prospect] thanks for reaching out - so are you looking to start [X] or already doing it?

**Keep it.** This is a great first question. It's easy (two options), it's about them, and the answer tells you which way the call goes.

> what's going on right now for you to look for support with [X]?

**Good question, wrong moment if it's second.** It's heavy. Ask it *after* they've told you where they're at, and hang it on their answer:
> "Okay so you've been at it a few months. And what's going on right now that made you want some help with it, rather than keep going on your own?"

> whats got you interested in [X]?

**Fine, but pick this OR the one above, not both.** If they're starting → "what's got you interested". If they're already doing it → "what's going on that you want support".

**Put together:**
> "Heyy [name], thanks for reaching out, man. So just so I know where you're at, are you looking to start [X], or are you already doing it?"
> → *Starting:* "Okay cool. And what's got you looking at [X] in the first place? Everyone's got a different reason."
> → *Already doing it:* "Oh nice, how long you been at it? … And what's going on right now that you want some help with it, rather than figure it out solo?"

### Outbound (you're messaging them after a follow, comment, story reply)

> hey [prospect] appreciate the follow / just saw you commented on my post / saw you engaged with my story

**Good. Specific beats generic.** If you can, name the thing:
> "Hey [name], appreciate the follow man. Saw you liked the one about [topic of the post]."

> are you looking to start [X] or already doing it?

**Keep it, same as inbound.**

> what drew you to my page?

**Careful with this one.** It's about you, not them, and most people honestly don't know ("the algorithm?"). Better:
> "What's got you looking at [X] content lately?"

That's about *their* situation and it gets the same info.

**Outbound is a DM, not a call.** Don't ask for the call in message one. Get two or three messages back first, find one thing they want, then:
> "Honestly it'd be easier to just hop on a quick call, I type like a grandma. 10 minutes, I'll see where you're at and if we can help. If not, no stress. When are you usually free, mornings or evenings?"

### Dialling (calling someone who opted in)

> hey [prospect] this is [Name] from [Business Owner's] team. Saw you filled out an application about... / signed up for one of our webinars about... / clicked on one of our ads about...

**Good. Keep the "[Owner]'s team".** People know the person, not the company name.

> are you free for a quick 5/10 minute chat?

**Change this.** Asking "are you free?" hands them an easy "no". Kevin does it better: tell them what the call is, then ask "sound fair?"
> "Does that ring a bell? … Cool. So just so you know, this is a quick 5–10 minute chat, just to see where you're at and if there's any way we can actually help. If there is, great, I'll point you in the right direction. If not, no stress at all. Sound fair?"

If you want to check timing, check it *after* they've said yes to the call being about them:
> "Did I catch you at an okay time, or are you driving or something?"

That's softer than "are you free?" because it's about not bothering them, not about permission.

> what made you fill out the form?

**Soften it.** On its own it can sound like "why did you do that?" Give it context:
> "And I always ask this cause everyone fills these out for different reasons. What was going on that made you fill it in?"

> what got you potentially interested in [xyz]?

**Drop "potentially".** It sounds like you doubt them. And it's a hard word to say fast in a second language anyway.
> "What got you interested in [xyz] in the first place?"

## Full opener templates (read out loud until they feel like yours)

### Dial · application / form
> **S:** Heyy… [name]?
> **P:** Yeah, who's this?
> **S:** Hey [name], it's Stefan from [Owner]'s team. You filled out an application a little while ago about [getting into trading / starting a store / …]. Does that ring a bell?
> **P:** Oh yeah, yeah.
> **S:** Perfect. So just so you know, it's a quick 5–10 minute chat. Just to see where you're at, where you want to be, and if we can actually help. If we can, great, I'll walk you through the next step. If not, no stress at all. Sound fair?
> **P:** Yeah, sure.
> **S:** Cool. So tell me, man, what had you interested in [X] in the first place?

### Dial · webinar
> **S:** Hey [name], it's Stefan from [Owner]'s team. You were on [Owner]'s webinar on [day], about [topic]. Does that ring a bell?
> **P:** Yeah, I watched some of it.
> **S:** Ha, some of it, that's honest. Most people catch the middle bit. Did anything stand out to you?

*(The webinar is your free context. "What stood out?" is a much easier first question than "what got you interested?" because they just watched something.)*

### Dial · ad / lead magnet / free guide (colder)
> **S:** Hey [name], it's Stefan from [Owner]'s team. A few days ago you grabbed one of our free guides on [topic]. Does that ring a bell?
> **P:** Uh, maybe? I download a lot of stuff.
> **S:** Ha, fair, you're not the only one. Just so I don't waste your time, is [X] something you're actually looking to get into, or are you more just having a look around for now?

*(You asked this in Kevin #8 and Atefur's "#5". It's the right move on a cold lead. It lets them say "just looking" without you sounding offended, and if they say "I want to get into it", they've just sold themselves.)*

### Inbound · they DM'd or booked from a post
> **S:** Heyy [name], thanks for reaching out, man. I saw your message about [thing they said]. So just so I know where you're at, are you looking to start [X], or already doing it?

### Outbound · after a DM conversation turned into a call
> **S:** Heyy [name], good to finally put a voice to the messages. So you were saying [one thing from the DMs, in their words]. Tell me a bit more about that.

*(Starting from what they already told you is the best context there is. They feel heard in the first ten seconds.)*

## When the opener goes sideways

**"Who's this?" / "How'd you get my number?"**
> "Ha, fair question. It's Stefan from [Owner]'s team. You put your number in when you [filled the form / signed up for the webinar] about [topic]. I'm just the guy who follows up."

**"I don't remember filling that in."**
> "No worries, people sign up for loads of stuff. It was about [topic], around [day]. Is that something you've been looking into at all?"
> *If no:* "All good, man, I'll take you off the list. Have a good one." *(Don't fight it.)*

**"I'm at work / driving / busy."**
> "Ah, no worries at all. When's better, later today or tomorrow? Morning or evening?"
> *(Give two options. "When's good for you?" gets "I'll call you back", and they won't.)*

**"Is this a sales call?"**
> "Ha, kind of, I'm not gonna lie to you. But I'm not selling you anything on this call. I just want to see where you're at, and if it makes sense, I'll book you in with someone who can go through it properly. If not, I'll tell you. Fair?"

**"How much is it?" (in the first minute)**
> "Honestly, depends on what you need, and I don't know that yet. That's literally why I'm calling. Give me five minutes to see where you're at, and if it makes sense, the next call goes through all of that. Fair?"

**"Can you just send me some info?"**
> "I can, but honestly, info without knowing where you're at is just a brochure. Give me five minutes and I'll know if it's even worth sending. Sound fair?"

**They sound flat / tired / annoyed.** *(Kevin #8: "Are you always this excited?" got "You rang me, I'm at work.")*
> "Sounds like I caught you at a busy one, my bad. You still good for 5 minutes, or should I give you a ring after?"
> *(Name it kindly. Never be sarcastic about their mood.)*

## The frame line: three ways to say it

Same meaning, pick the one that's easiest in your mouth:

1. "My goal here is just to see where you're at, where you want to be, and if we can actually help. If we can, I'll walk you through the next step. If not, no stress."
2. "Just a quick chat to see if there's any alignment with what we do. If there is, great. If not, no worries at all. Sound fair?" *(Kevin's)*
3. "I'm just gonna ask you a few questions, get the bigger picture, and see if it even makes sense to go further. Cool?"

**Always end with "sound fair?" / "cool?"** A small yes at the start makes the next answers easier.
