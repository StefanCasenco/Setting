# 05 · Tech sales (career change) · Dial · Clicked an ad

**Prospect:** Daniel, 27, assistant store manager at a sports retail chain, US · **Lead:** clicked an ad, "break into tech sales in 90 days", opted in yesterday · **Real length:** about 13 minutes · **Ends:** booked

> **What's different in tech sales.** The prospect wants a *job*, not a business. So the money pushback is different: "why would I pay to get a job?". Have the answer ready (it's below). The goal is usually a salary or OTE number, and the "what have you tried" is almost always "applied to loads of jobs, heard nothing".

## The call

*— INTRO —*

**[SETTER]:** Heyy… Daniel?

**[DANIEL]:** Yeah, who's calling?

**[SETTER]:** Hey Daniel, it's Stefan from Chris's team. Yesterday you clicked on one of our ads about getting into tech sales, and you left your number. Does that ring a bell?

**[DANIEL]:** Uh… yeah, I think so. The 90 days thing?

**[SETTER]:** That's the one. So just so I don't waste your time, is tech sales something you're actually looking to get into, or are you more just having a look around for now?

**[DANIEL]:** No, I'm looking. I'm serious about it.

**[SETTER]:** Love that. So it's a quick 10-minute chat. I want to see where you're at, where you want to be, and if we can actually help. If we can, I'll walk you through the next step. If not, no stress at all. Sound fair?

**[DANIEL]:** Yeah, go ahead.

*— INTENT —*

**[SETTER]:** So out of all the career changes people make, what had you looking at tech sales?

**[DANIEL]:** My buddy from college did it. He was a waiter, and now he's an AE at some software company. He makes like 120. He works from home. And he's not smarter than me. *(laughs)*

**[SETTER]:** *(laughs)* He's not smarter than you. So when you see him now, what's the thing that gets you the most?

**[DANIEL]:** Honestly? He's home. He picked his kid up from school on a Tuesday. I haven't had a Tuesday off since March.

*≈ 1:30*

*— CURRENT SITUATION —*

**[SETTER]:** Since March. What's keeping you that busy?

**[DANIEL]:** I'm assistant manager at a sports store. Retail. We're short-staffed, so I'm covering shifts all the time. Weekends, holidays. Black Friday's coming up, so… *(laughs)*

**[SETTER]:** Oh man, Black Friday in a sports store. What does a normal week look like for you?

**[DANIEL]:** Fifty, fifty-five hours. Five, sometimes six days. And it's never the same days, so I can't plan anything.

**[SETTER]:** And how long have you been there?

**[DANIEL]:** Five years. Started as a sales associate.

**[SETTER]:** Five years and you made assistant manager, that's not nothing. Is it something you still enjoy, or more just what you know at this point?

**[DANIEL]:** I liked it at first. I'm good with people, I like selling. But the pay hasn't really moved. I got promoted and it was like a dollar fifty more an hour. And now I'm doing two jobs.

**[SETTER]:** A dollar fifty for doing two jobs.

**[DANIEL]:** Yeah. *(pause)* And my girlfriend and I want to get married next year. And I'm looking at the numbers and I'm like, how?

**[SETTER]:** What do you mean, "how"?

**[DANIEL]:** Like, the wedding, a place of our own. We're in a one-bed with her sister right now. I can't even ask her to marry me properly, I don't have the ring money.

**[SETTER]:** Yeah. That's a lot to carry.

**[DANIEL]:** She doesn't push it. But I know she's waiting.

*≈ 4:30*

**[SETTER]:** So basically, it's not the job itself. It's that you're good at selling, but the job isn't letting you build the life you want with her. Is that fair?

**[DANIEL]:** Yeah, man. That's it.

*— TIMEFRAME —*

**[SETTER]:** Got it. Give me the bigger picture. How long have you been looking at tech sales?

**[DANIEL]:** Since the summer. When my buddy got promoted.

**[SETTER]:** And since the summer, have you actually applied anywhere, or has it been more research so far?

**[DANIEL]:** I applied. Like, 40 jobs? SDR roles. On LinkedIn.

**[SETTER]:** And how did that go?

**[DANIEL]:** Two auto-rejections. The rest, nothing. Not even a no.

**[SETTER]:** Yeah, that's really common with LinkedIn applications right now. It's not just you.

*— WHAT GOT IN THE WAY —*

**[SETTER]:** And if you had to point at the one thing that's in the way, would you say it's the resume, not knowing the software side, not knowing anyone, or something else?

**[DANIEL]:** Honestly, I think it's the resume. It says "retail" and they just skip it. My buddy said he got in through a referral. I don't know anyone.

*— WHY NOW —*

**[SETTER]:** And you've been on this since summer. What made you click the ad yesterday, specifically?

**[DANIEL]:** They posted the holiday schedule. I'm on Thanksgiving, Black Friday and the 26th. And my girlfriend's family does a big thing on Thanksgiving. Second year in a row I'm missing it.

**[SETTER]:** Second year in a row. Yeah, I get it.

*≈ 7:30*

*— FINANCIAL QUALIFICATION —*

**[SETTER]:** Okay, let me ask you a couple of quick ones on the money side, just so I know where you're at. Cool?

**[DANIEL]:** Sure.

**[SETTER]:** So with tech sales, the first role is usually an SDR. If you landed one, what would it need to pay for it to be worth leaving the store?

**[DANIEL]:** Honestly, if I could get to like 65 base, I'd jump.

**[SETTER]:** 65 base. And just so I see the gap, where are you at right now, roughly?

**[DANIEL]:** About 44 a year.

**[SETTER]:** And with how expensive everything is now, and splitting a place, how much would you say you're actually able to put aside each month?

**[DANIEL]:** Like 200, 250.

**[SETTER]:** Is that usual, or a good month?

**[DANIEL]:** Usual. Overtime months are better.

**[SETTER]:** And in total, how much have you been able to save so far?

**[DANIEL]:** Why does that matter? It's a job. I'm trying to get *paid*, not pay.

**[SETTER]:** *(laughs)* Totally fair question, a lot of people ask that. Honestly, it's because a program like this is an investment. And you've already tried the free way, 40 applications, and heard nothing back. The thing that usually gets people in is the right help with the resume, practice for the interviews, and people who can intro you. That's what costs money. And I don't want to book you into something that doesn't make sense for where you're at. Fair?

**[DANIEL]:** Hm. Yeah, okay. That's fair.

**[SETTER]:** So roughly, what have you got saved?

**[DANIEL]:** About four grand. That's kind of the ring money. *(laughs)* Slowly.

**[SETTER]:** *(laughs)* The ring money. Okay. And would you want to keep the ring money as the ring money, or are you open to putting some of it toward this if it gets you the job that pays for the ring?

**[DANIEL]:** *(pause)* I mean… if it actually gets me the job, the ring's easy after that.

**[SETTER]:** Fair. And is credit something you've got available, if spreading it out made more sense?

**[DANIEL]:** I've got a card, yeah. I'd rather not, but yeah.

**[SETTER]:** Okay, good to know. And if we found a way that fits, would you be open to investing in yourself, time, energy, resources, to make the switch?

**[DANIEL]:** Yeah. I'm done covering shifts.

**[DANIEL]:** *(gives it)*

**[SETTER]:** And is this number good for a reminder text?

## What made it flow

- **"Is tech sales something you're actually looking to get into, or more just having a look around?"** On a cold ad lead, this comes before the frame. He said "I'm serious", so he sold himself before you asked anything.
- **"Out of all the career changes people make"** turned the intent question into a choice.
- **The buddy was the way in.** "When you see him now, what's the thing that gets you the most?" moved from the friend to Daniel's own life in one step.
- **"Five years and you made assistant manager, that's not nothing."** A small compliment before the job-feeling question. It makes "is it something you still enjoy?" feel safe to answer honestly.
- **"What do you mean, 'how'?"** He said one word. The setter asked about that word, and it opened the girlfriend and the ring.
- **"It's really common with LinkedIn right now, it's not just you."** Normalising the 40 rejections.
- **The job-not-business pushback** ("I'm trying to get paid, not pay") was answered with his own 40 applications. The free way already didn't work.
- **"Would you keep the ring money as the ring money?"** Using his words for his savings, and giving him the choice.

## Setter, not closer

- **The ring was the pain.** One feeling (she's waiting), one cost (can't propose). The setter didn't push on it to build pressure.
- **The ring money wasn't pushed.** It was offered as a choice, and he answered it himself. Making the case for spending it is the closer's job, if it's even the right thing.
- **No promise of a job or salary.** The pitch promised a plan to get *a* role, and a coach who understands his background.
