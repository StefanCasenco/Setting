## What made it flow

- **The intro didn't ask "are you free?".** It confirmed the application, said what the call is, then "sound fair?". The timing check ("sounds like you're outside") came after the yes, and it was about him, not about permission.
- **"Out of all the ways to make money online"** made the intent question feel like a choice he made, not a test.
- **"Seven at night"** was his throwaway line. The setter grabbed it. That one grab gave the job, the hours and the body, without asking "what do you do?" or "how do you feel about the job?".
- **"Being self-employed, I know it's never really 9 to 5"** is a context line (real life). It makes "how many hours?" sound like interest, not a form.
- **Options on "what have you tried":** "put money on a trade, or more watching and paper trading?" He picked one and told the £500 story by himself.
- **The money pushback was answered with his own story.** "You already lost £500 on your own, I don't want you putting in money you can't afford to lose." That's the strongest reason line there is, because it's about him.
- **Pitch used his words:** "first two grand, so you can cut down to four days." Not "your goals".

## Setter, not closer

- **The back injury was enough pain.** One feeling (stressed, felt like crap), one cost (two weeks no money, wife picking up shifts). The setter didn't dig five more layers. That's the closer's job.
- **"It's our emergency money" wasn't fought.** No reframe, no "what's the cost of staying the same?". The setter just noted it and brought the wife onto the call, so the closer can handle it properly.
- **No promise about trading results.** The pitch promised a *plan*, not money.

## Lines to steal

> "Being self-employed, I know it's never really 9 to 5. What does a normal week look like?"

> "Since the savings are joint, it'd be worth having your [wife / partner] on the call too."
