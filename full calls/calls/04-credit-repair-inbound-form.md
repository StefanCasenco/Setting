# 04 · Credit repair · Inbound · Filled out a form

**Prospect:** Tasha, 38, US, medical billing clerk, single mum of one · **Lead:** filled out "fix my credit" form from a Facebook ad, this morning · **Real length:** about 12 minutes · **Ends:** booked

> **Read this first: credit repair is different.**
> The prospect isn't starting a business. They have a problem *now* (bad credit) and usually a deadline (a house, a car, an apartment). And it's a regulated space in the US. **Never promise a score, a number of points, or that something "will be removed".** Never say "we can get that deleted". You don't know that, and saying it can get the business in trouble. The setter's job is the same: find the pain, the deadline, and whether they can afford help, then book.
> *(If your credit repair offer is the bizopp version, "start your own credit repair business", run it like the AI agency call instead.)*

## What made it flow

- **"Some people want a car, some an apartment, some just want the calls to stop. What's the main thing for you?"** Options in the intent question. In credit repair, people feel shame about it, so options make it easy to answer.
- **"Is that a someday thing, or is there something pushing it right now?"** It finds the deadline (May) in the second question. In credit repair the deadline is your "why now".
- **"No judgement at all, most people I speak to have a story behind the number."** This is the most important context line in this niche. It turns "why is your credit bad?" (shame) into "tell me your story" (relief).
- **"So some of it wasn't even yours."** Picking up on the unfair part makes her feel understood, not judged.
- **"Disputing it yourself, worked with anyone, or just paying what you can?"** Options on what she tried.
- **The down payment wasn't touched.** "That's the house money, so I'm guessing you want that to stay the house money." Respecting it built trust, and the money question moved to monthly instead.

## Setter, not closer, and no promises

- **The dealership story was the pain.** "In front of my daughter." One moment, one feeling (embarrassed), one cost (no car, a question she couldn't answer). The setter stopped there.
- **"My score went down when I paid it off"** wasn't explained. The setter said "that one confuses a lot of people" and moved on. Explaining credit is the specialist's job, and getting it wrong is risky.
- **The pitch promised honesty, not points.** "Tell you honestly what can be done and how long it would realistically take." Nothing about deleting anything or hitting a score.
