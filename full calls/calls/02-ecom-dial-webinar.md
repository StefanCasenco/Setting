# 02 · Ecom · Dial · Watched the webinar

**Prospect:** Priya, 34, nurse on night shifts · **Lead:** attended the webinar on Tuesday · **Real length:** about 14 minutes · **Ends:** booked, with her husband on the call

---

## The call

*— INTRO —*

**[SETTER]:** Heyy… Priya?

**[PRIYA]:** Yes?

**[SETTER]:** Hey Priya, it's Stefan from Leo's team. You were on Leo's webinar on Tuesday, about starting an online store. Does that ring a bell?

**[PRIYA]:** Oh, yes. I watched most of it. I had to leave near the end, sorry.

**[SETTER]:** Ha, no need to be sorry, most people catch the middle bit. So just so you know, it's a quick 10-minute chat. Just to see where you're at and if there's any way we can actually help. If there is, great. If not, no stress at all. Sound fair?

**[PRIYA]:** Yes, okay.

*— INTENT —*

**[SETTER]:** So what stood out to you from the webinar?

**[PRIYA]:** The bit where he showed the woman who did it from her kitchen. With the candles. I thought, okay, maybe a normal person can do this.

**[SETTER]:** A normal person. What do you mean by that?

**[PRIYA]:** Like, not some 22-year-old guy in Dubai. *(laughs)* She had kids, she had a job. She looked tired, like me.

**[SETTER]:** Ha. She looked tired like you. Are you tired at the moment?

**[PRIYA]:** I'm a nurse, I work nights. I'm always tired. *(laughs)*

*≈ 1:30*

*— CURRENT SITUATION —*

**[SETTER]:** Nights. How long have you been on nights?

**[PRIYA]:** Four years now. Three twelve-hour nights a week, sometimes four.

**[SETTER]:** Four years of nights, that's a lot. Is nights something you chose, or more something that happened?

**[PRIYA]:** It happened. The pay is better, and when my son was small it made sense, my husband did the evenings. But now it's just… the way it is.

**[SETTER]:** And what does a normal day look like for you, on a night-shift week?

**[PRIYA]:** I get home at 8:30 in the morning. I sleep till two, maybe. Then I get up, school pickup, dinner, and I'm back at work at 7:30. On my days off, I'm trying to fix my sleep, so I'm a zombie.

**[SETTER]:** So on the days you're working, you basically see your son for like, a few hours in the afternoon.

**[PRIYA]:** Yes. And I'm half asleep for it.

**[SETTER]:** How old is he?

**[PRIYA]:** Nine.

**[SETTER]:** And how's that sitting with you, honestly?

**[PRIYA]:** *(pause)* Not good. Last month he had a school play and I missed it, because I couldn't swap the shift. He said it was fine. But I know.

**[SETTER]:** Yeah. He said it was fine.

**[PRIYA]:** He's a good boy. That's the worst part, he doesn't complain.

**[SETTER]:** Yeah, that's heavy.

*≈ 4:00*

**[SETTER]:** So basically, it's less about getting rich, and more about not missing those things. Having something that lets you come off nights. Is that fair?

**[PRIYA]:** Yes. Exactly that. Even just off nights would be enough.

*— TIMEFRAME —*

**[SETTER]:** Got it. Give me the bigger picture then. How long has ecom been on your radar?

**[PRIYA]:** Maybe a year? I started a store last year actually.

**[SETTER]:** Oh, nice. What was the store?

**[PRIYA]:** Baby stuff. Muslin blankets. I set it up on Shopify, ordered some samples.

**[SETTER]:** And how did it go?

**[PRIYA]:** I got three sales. Two were my friends. *(laughs)*

**[SETTER]:** Ha. Three sales is three more than most people get. And what happened after that?

**[PRIYA]:** I ran some ads, I spent maybe £400 on Facebook, and nothing happened. Then I had a month of extra shifts and I just… stopped. It's still there, actually. I'm still paying the Shopify fee.

*— WHAT GOT IN THE WAY —*

**[SETTER]:** And looking back, if you had to point at one thing that held it back, what would you say it was? The product, the ads, the time, or something else?

**[PRIYA]:** Honestly, I didn't know what I was doing with the ads. I watched videos, but every video says something different. And I don't have time to test like they say.

**[SETTER]:** So it's less about the effort, and more about not knowing which thing to do with the little time you've got.

**[PRIYA]:** Yes. Exactly.

*— WHY NOW —*

**[SETTER]:** And you've had the store sitting there for a while. What made you sign up for the webinar this week?

**[PRIYA]:** The rota came out for December. I'm on Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve. And I just thought, I can't keep doing this.

**[SETTER]:** Yeah. That makes sense.

*≈ 7:30*

*— FINANCIAL QUALIFICATION —*

**[SETTER]:** Okay, let me ask you a couple of quick questions on the money side, just so I know where you're at. Cool?

**[PRIYA]:** Yes.

**[SETTER]:** So you said even just off nights would be enough. If you came off nights, how much would you be losing a month, roughly?

**[PRIYA]:** The night pay is about £600 more a month.

**[SETTER]:** Okay. So the first goal is the store making at least £600 a month, so you can go to days. And then the bigger goal?

**[PRIYA]:** Bigger goal, honestly, go part-time. Three days. So maybe £1,500, £2,000 from the store.

**[SETTER]:** Love that. And just so I understand where you're at, what's coming in for you each month right now, roughly?

**[PRIYA]:** About £2,400 after tax.

**[SETTER]:** And you know how expenses are nowadays, everything going up, with a nine-year-old… how much would you say you're actually able to put aside from that each month?

**[PRIYA]:** Maybe £200. It depends on the month.

**[SETTER]:** Is that on a good month or your usual?

**[PRIYA]:** Usual. Some months nothing, like when the car broke.

**[SETTER]:** Yeah, the car always breaks at the worst time. And in total, how much have you been able to save up so far?

**[PRIYA]:** Me personally, maybe £3,000. We have more together, but that's for the house.

**[SETTER]:** Okay. And when it comes to something like this, investing in the store, is that a decision you'd make on your own, or something you'd talk through with your husband?

**[PRIYA]:** I'd talk to him. He's the money person. He's a bit sceptical, honestly. He saw me lose the £400.

**[SETTER]:** Ha, that's fair from him. And if we found a way that actually fits you, with the hours you've got, would you be open to investing in yourself, time, energy and resources, to make it work?

**[PRIYA]:** Yes. If it actually works this time.

## What made it flow

- **"What stood out to you from the webinar?"** is the easiest first question on a webinar lead. She just watched something, so she has an answer ready. It led straight to "a normal person", which was the real story.
- **"Is nights something you chose, or more something that happened?"** is the job-feeling question with options. It's much softer than "how do you feel about the job?" and her answer explained the whole history.
- **"What does a normal day look like on a night-shift week?"** made the hours question a story, not a number.
- **"Three sales is three more than most people get"** is a context line that normalises a failure. After that she told the £400 story freely.
- **"The product, the ads, the time, or something else?"** gave her options on what got in the way.
- **The goal number came from her own words** ("even just off nights would be enough"), so the setter asked "how much would you lose coming off nights?". That's a goal question that doesn't sound like a goal question.
- **"The car always breaks at the worst time"** is a small real-life line. It keeps the money section from feeling like a form.

## Setter, not closer

- **The school play was the pain.** One moment, one feeling ("I know"), one cost (she missed it). The setter said "yeah, that's heavy" and stopped. He didn't ask "and how does that make you feel as a mother?". That's too much for a setter.
- **"He's a bit sceptical" wasn't handled.** The setter just made it a reason to put the husband on the call. Handling a sceptical spouse is the closer's job, and it's much easier with the spouse actually there.
- **"If it actually works this time"** wasn't answered with a promise. The pitch promised a plan around nights, not results.

## Lines to steal

> "So what stood out to you from the webinar?"
> "Is [the job / nights / the commute] something you chose, or more something that happened?"
> "Three sales is three more than most people get."
> "If you came off [nights / the second job], how much would you be losing a month?"
> "Since he's the money person, it'd be worth having him on the call too, so he can ask all his questions directly."
