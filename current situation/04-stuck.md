# 04 · When it isn't going smoothly

Real prospects are harder than the ones in the roleplays. Atefur said that outright after the first call: "no prospect ever says any of this." (#1) Expect short answers, deflecting, jokes and jargon.

## They give one word or "idk"
Two tries at most. Make it easy to answer:
- **Guess out loud:** "Sounds like money's been tight lately?"
- **Two options:** "Is it more the money, or more the time?"

Still surface after two? **Stop digging.** Say "Got it man." and move on. The rest of the call fills it in. (#2: surface answers are normal.)

## "Why are you asking me this?"
Give the reason line, flat, and keep going:
> "I'm not asking just for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, like a personal trainer would." (#3)

Atefur's version of the same idea: "Can we help you if we don't know your story?" (#1)

If they won't go there: "No pressure. Even a short answer helps."

## They talk a lot
Find the one real sentence.
- "Hold on, back up. The part about your dad, what happened there?"
- "You said something there: 'I'm done.' Done with what?"
- "Out of all that, which part sits worst?"

## Only tactical
Don't follow the method talk. Use the motivation question (#3):
> "I know you want to get better at trading, but what's the real motivation for even trying it?"

If they say money: "No one comes to us to make less money. What's the real reason?" (#3)
If they say time or freedom: "Time? Freedom? How do you mean?" (#3)

## They like their job
Don't dig the job. Ask the motivation question (#3).

## Vague feeling word ("mental aspect", "comfortable", "fulfilled")
> "What do you mean about the mental aspect?" (#2)

Not "can you give me an example". (#2)

## They get emotional
Slow down. Don't fill the silence. One human sentence, then wait.
- "What's the hardest part of that?"

If it's heavy (loss, crisis), stop the framework altogether. The booking can wait.

## They push back with an ego or "I know all this"
Don't argue and don't prove anything. Ask what's still not where they want it:
- "What's still not where you want it?"
- "What does that cost you?"

## "Is this a scam?"
> "Fair worry. This call is just to see where you're at, nothing more. [Closer] answers the detailed stuff." Then: "What made you ask?"

## "How much is it?"
They tend to ask early. Atefur's taster, once:
> "It's around a four-figure investment, but we can't even know if we can help you just yet." (#3)

Then back to them. Most of the time, don't say it at all. Never say it's free and never deny there's a cost.

## You went too deep, or too shallow
- Too shallow: ask for the next layer: "How so?"
- Too deep for the moment: you can overshoot. Better than always shallow. (#2) Notice where they got uncomfortable and ease back.
- If you notice you're acting like a closer (challenging, "why aren't you"), you've left your lane. Go back to the word they gave you. (#1)
