# 01 · The method

Source tags: **#1–#4** are the four calls with Atefur. **[F]** is Flavius. Details in `06-sources.md`.

## What CURRENT is for
- **Pain, not facts.** You're collecting how it feels and what it costs, not a CV. (#2, #3)
- **Tactical vs deep.** "I keep blowing accounts" is tactical. Under it is the real thing: time, freedom, family, feeling behind. Get to the real thing. (#3)
- **Pain drives change.** People change when it hurts, not when they know what to do. (#3)
- **Current is pain, desire is goals.** Stay in pain until it lands. Don't go to "what would that look like" early. (#2, #3)
- **You sell the meeting, not the product.** You don't need the full closer-level dig. You need enough pain to book. (#1)
- **Length.** About 5–7 minutes; it's the most important part of the call. (#2)

## The loop (every single turn)

1. **Listen** for the word that carries the weight: a feeling word, a vague word, or a thing they keep returning to ("stuck", "no time", "comfortable", "cycle", "behind").
2. **Ask about that word**, in their words. Not about your next topic.
3. **One question.** Go straight at it.
4. **Listen to the answer.** It hands you the next word.
5. **Check:** do I have a feeling and a cost yet? If not, go one layer deeper. If yes, go to the exit test.

### Straight in, no run-up
You wanted this one most, so it's first:

> Don't ask four questions to get to the one you want. Ask it. (#4: "just ask him straight away.")

Example from #4: instead of asking how much time they have, then how much they earn, ask **"What makes you feel like you don't have enough time right now?"** and let them tell you.

Test for any question before you ask it: **will the answer move the call toward their pain?** If you'd only be noting it down, skip it. (#4)

## The layers
| | |
|---|---|
| L1 | Surface: "stuck", "need money", "more time" |
| L2 | What it means to them. Ask **"what do you mean?"** |
| L3 | A scene from their week |
| L4 | The personal impact: how it hits them and the people around them |
| L5 | What staying put costs them |

Aim for **L3–L4**. Stop earlier if they honestly don't open up. Surface answers are normal. Some calls go deeper than needed, and that's fine when you're building the skill. (#2)

The shape of Atefur's own demo (#1): *more money → quit my job → it's time-consuming → no time for family and friends → what's the negative impact.* Peeling an onion.

## Atefur's rules

1. **Link the problem to the person.** Tactical problem → real-life emotion. "What are you trying to get out of trading?" (#2)
2. **Echo their word, then ask what it means.** Repeat the same word; "it can be as simple as that." (#1)
3. **"What do you mean?" beats "can you give me an example?"** The example gets a logical answer; "what do you mean" gets one from the heart. (#2)
4. **Use their words back, softened.** "What makes you feel like you're not as ___ as you want to be right now?" (#2)
5. **The "magic question"** after a surface goal: "What makes you feel like you're not achieving your potential right now?" (#2)
6. **Ask for the real motivation** when it's only tactical: "I know you want to get better at trading, but what's the real motivation for even trying it?" (#3)
7. **Ask what the vivid word looks like.** "When you say lonely, what does that actually look like?" / "What makes you use the word 'cycle'?" (#4, #1)
8. **Ask for the knock-on effects.** "What kind of knock-on effects has that been having on you?" / "What's the more negative impact?" (#3, #1)
9. **Go human to human.** "Forget sales. What's more painful about this, as a person?" (#2)
10. **Read between the lines of what they said.** If they say "10 grand a month", what does that tell you they don't have now? If they say "fulfilling life", what does that say about now? (#2)
11. **Use the reason line** when they push back: "I'm not asking just for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, like a personal trainer would." (#3)
12. **Don't re-dig.** Once you have the pain, stop. (#2)
13. **Better too deep than shallow.** You learn where the middle is by overshooting. (#2)
14. **Stay in your process.** If you catch yourself jumping to the future, go back. (#3)

## Don'ts
- **Don't ask "why aren't you / why haven't you".** You get a defensive or self-blaming answer. (#2)
- **Don't ask why they're *still* stuck.** That's the closer's job: the problem and the limiting belief are two different things. (#2, #1)
- **Don't hold them accountable.** "What stopped you in the last three months?" is the closer's job. (#1)
- **Don't assume the job is the problem.** Some people like their job and want this for another reason. (#3)
- **Don't ask "can you give me an example."** (#2)
- **Don't ask about the desire early.** No "what would that look like" before the pain has landed. (#3)
- **Don't copy lines word for word.** Keep the principle, use your own words. (#2)
- **Don't ask a question just to build rapport or fill time.** Every question moves toward the pain. (#4)
- **No therapy voice.** Not "how does that make you feel."

## Exit test
You can say it: **their pain in their words, one feeling, one cost.** Then:

1. Say something short like **"Got it man."**
2. Move on. The framework picks it up at the recap of their deeper goal.
3. Don't go back in.

Atefur's own stop signals: "a named problem with an emotion" is enough to transition (#2); "I think I got to a deep enough depth of the problem to close it" (#4).

## Delivery
- **Warm and a bit upbeat, not hyped.** You sounded robotic on the first call; by the third your tone had improved a lot. (#1, #3)
- **Short.** One question, two short sentences at most.
- **Sound like you're asking because you want to know.** (#1)
- **Silence is allowed.** After something real, wait.
