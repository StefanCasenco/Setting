# 05 · Practice

Atefur's point: **structure like iron, freestyle where it counts.** Your freestyle only works if you've drilled the listening, so these drills are about listening.

## 1. The pause drill (#3)
Review a recording of your own call. After **every** prospect sentence, hit pause and ask:

> "How could I have gotten deeper?"

Write one line each time: the better question. Next sentence, pause again. Do it until the CURRENT section ends.

Score it:
- Layers reached (L1–L5)
- Times you echoed their word
- Times you asked "what do you mean?"
- Times you asked a question that wasn't about what they just said
- Minutes in CURRENT
- Did you hit the exit test?

## 2. Play the prospect against yourself (#2)
Say both sides out loud. Play an easy prospect first: the setter says "what makes you feel like you don't have X right now?" and you answer, then go through the layers yourself. An AI works too, but **say it out loud**, don't type it. (#2)

## 3. The thread tracker (use on every call)
Keep three lines on paper during the call:
1. **Their words** (the exact words that carried weight)
2. **The feeling** (one)
3. **The cost** (one)

If you get to minute three and you can't fill line 1 with their words, you're asking your questions, not following theirs.

## 4. The "one question" check
Take any call you've recorded and count how many questions it took from the first money answer to the first feeling. Aim for three or fewer. If it took six, find the questions that only collected facts.

## 5. Get on an offer
Roleplays plateau. Atefur's advice in #3: you need live calls, with real prospects, and real feedback. About 50 live setting calls.

## Weekly
Pick the single habit that shows up most in your pause drill. Write its fix on a card and read it before each call. One habit per week.
