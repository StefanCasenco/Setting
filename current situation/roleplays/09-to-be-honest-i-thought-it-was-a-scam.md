# 09 · "To be honest, I thought this was a scam."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 5½ minutes · **Ends:** qualified, reluctantly

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: To be honest? I only booked this because a mate told me to. I thought it was a scam. I did a course last year, a trading course, and I lost a grand and got nothing out of it. So I'm a bit, you know.

[SETTER]: Fair worry. This call is just to see where you're at, nothing more. The advisor answers the detailed stuff. What was the course?

[PROSPECT]: Some guy on Instagram. The usual. Lambo, "financial freedom." A bunch of videos and a Discord that went quiet after two weeks.

[SETTER]: Ha. Yeah. Those are everywhere. What made you want to look again, though?

[PROSPECT]: Because I still want it. That's the annoying bit. I thought, I'll never do that again, and then three months later I'm back looking.

[SETTER]: What are you looking for?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly, a way out of the help desk. I'm in IT support, Birmingham. Good job on paper. Thirty-eight grand, decent company. But they've got me on call, so I'll get a ping at three in the morning. A printer in Leeds.

[SETTER]: A printer in Leeds. At three in the morning.

[PROSPECT]: *(laughs)* It's not a printer, it's a server. But it might as well be. I'm up for an hour and I can't go back to sleep and I'm useless the next day.

*≈ 1:45*

[SETTER]: Is that every week?

[PROSPECT]: Every third week. But I'm dreading it the whole other two. It's always there.

[SETTER]: How's that with your girlfriend?

[PROSPECT]: She's good about it. She's a nurse, she does nights, she gets it. She's actually the one who said, "maybe try something that's yours." She's not pushing. She's just said it a few times.

[SETTER]: And what do you think when she says it?

[PROSPECT]: I think she's right. Then I think about the grand I lost and I go quiet.

[SETTER]: Is that the hard part?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. It's not the money. It's that I felt stupid. I don't love feeling stupid.

[SETTER]: Who knew about it?

[PROSPECT]: Just her. And my brother, who thinks I'm an idiot, lovingly. He sends me "ZERO GAINS" memes.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* Nice.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. It's fine. But I do hear it when I'm thinking about doing something. A little voice that says "remember the Discord."

[SETTER]: The Discord.

[PROSPECT]: That's what we call it. "Remember the Discord." *(laughs)*

*≈ 3:15*

[SETTER]: So that was fourteen months ago, the course. Have you done anything since?

[PROSPECT]: No. A bit of free YouTube. I don't trust any of it.

[SETTER]: Mm. So what's making you open to it now?

[PROSPECT]: I don't know, nothing big. I did a three-week on-call stretch in September because two people were off, and by the end of it I was a mess. And I thought, I can't do this for twenty years.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Alright, one more bit before I wrap up. A few questions on the money side, that okay?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. But I'm not paying for another course, just so you know.

[SETTER]: Understood. I'm not selling you anything on this call. It's just to see if it makes sense to book you in with the advisor.

[PROSPECT]: Okay.

[SETTER]: What would you want trading to be adding every month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Two grand a month. I'd drop to part-time.

[SETTER]: And what are you on now, take-home?

[PROSPECT]: About twenty-four hundred.

[SETTER]: Right. And how much do you put away each month?

[PROSPECT]: Five hundred, usually. She's good with money, we split rent.

[SETTER]: And in total, how much have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why's that your business?

[SETTER]: Yeah man, but I'm sure you know, trading takes money to make money, right? So how much have you saved?

[PROSPECT]: *(sigh)* About seven grand. Sat in a savings account doing nothing.

[SETTER]: Is any of it spoken for?

[PROSPECT]: Most of it. We've got a separate pot for the wedding. This one's mine.

[SETTER]: Okay. That helps. And since you said it earlier, are you wondering what this costs?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, honestly. How much is it?

[SETTER]: It's a four-figure investment, depending on the program. But we can't even know if we can help you yet. That's what the advisor call is for.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. That's actually fair. I wanted someone to just say it's not free.

[SETTER]: It's not free. No.

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** gave me suspicion, not a goal. I used the "fair worry" line once and moved on.
- **The real reason ("a way out of the help desk") came as a follow-up,** after the suspicion was acknowledged.
- **Why now** was asked at the end of the current section, softly: "what's making you open to it now?"
- **The money part started with his boundary** ("not paying for another course"). I answered it honestly, then went through the ladder.
- **I gave the price taster once**, when he was clearly wondering.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"I only booked this because a mate told me to."** He doesn't trust the call, and he's telling me up front.
- **"I still want it. That's the annoying bit."** He's drawn to it and afraid. That's a good place to be.
- **"A printer in Leeds."** A joke that's really about the 3 a.m. pings. Humour is a tell.
- **"I'm dreading it the whole other two."** The weight isn't the hour of lost sleep. It's that it's always there.
- **"It's not the money. It's that I felt stupid."** The actual wound, and it has nothing to do with the grand.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"What made you want to look again?"** pulled the real reason from under the suspicion.
- **"And what do you think when she says it?"** moved from what she says to what he feels, with no feelings word.
- **"Is that the hard part?"** a gentle guess that was right.
- **I never tried to talk him out of his suspicion.** The advisor handles that.
