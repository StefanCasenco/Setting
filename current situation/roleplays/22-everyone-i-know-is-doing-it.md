# 22 · "Yeah man, I've seen loads of people make money from trading, so I thought, why not."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 4½ minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Hey, so what had you interested in trading in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, man, I've seen loads of people online making money from trading, so I thought, why not. Everyone's doing it now. I figured I'd see what it is.

[SETTER]: Everyone's doing it. Is that everyone online, or people you actually know?

[PROSPECT]: Both. Well. One guy. Kuba. We were at school together. He quit his job in the spring. He sits at home and trades. Or that's what he says.

[SETTER]: Hm. Do you believe him?

[PROSPECT]: I don't know. *(laughs)* He bought a new laptop. And he's always got time to go to the gym at like eleven in the morning.

[SETTER]: How do you feel about that?

[PROSPECT]: I don't care. It's fine. I mean. We used to be on the same level, you know? Same job basically. And now he's, I don't know. Free.

[SETTER]: Free.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Sorry, I'm on the bus. It's loud. Hold on, I'll go to the back.

[SETTER]: No stress.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Better. Sorry. What was I saying.

[SETTER]: Kuba's free. And you're on the same level.

*≈ 1:30*

[PROSPECT]: Were. I'm a software tester. Warsaw. It's a good job, the money's fine, I get paid on time. But I sit in the same open space with the same people and I test the same app for two years now. Same bugs. I know the app better than the developers.

[SETTER]: Two years on the same app.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. And my boss says I'm doing great. And then, I don't know. I got the same raise as the new guy. Three percent.

[SETTER]: Did that get to you?

[PROSPECT]: A bit. I didn't say anything. I'm not that guy. But I did the math on the bus home. Three percent of nothing.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* Three percent of nothing.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I'm twenty-nine and I'm going to be testing this app when I'm forty. That's what I thought.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: Have you looked into trading yourself?

[PROSPECT]: A bit. I've got an account. I put in some money last year. I did okay for a few weeks and then I lost most of it in a day. I got cocky.

[SETTER]: What happened in the day?

[PROSPECT]: I doubled up because I was up. Classic. Then I thought, I'm not doing this alone.

[SETTER]: Mm. And you reached out now rather than last year because of...?

[PROSPECT]: Kuba, probably. He sent me a photo of his desk. Three monitors. And I was at my desk, with my one monitor and a bug report. I looked at it for a minute and thought, okay. Enough.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Okay. Let me ask you about the money. What are we looking to make from trading each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly, three thousand euro. That would make me independent.

[SETTER]: And where are you at now?

[PROSPECT]: I'd rather not say exactly. It's decent.

[SETTER]: No problem. Is it more like two and a half or more like four, take-home?

[PROSPECT]: Closer to three. Three-two, something.

[SETTER]: Right. And how much can you put away from that?

[PROSPECT]: Quite a lot, actually. I live alone, but it's cheap. Twelve hundred a month, maybe.

[SETTER]: Nice. And in total, how much have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: That's personal.

[SETTER]: Yeah, I get that. It's just so I know whether it makes sense to book you in. Ballpark's fine.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. About nine thousand euro. Sitting there.

[SETTER]: Is it free to use?

[PROSPECT]: Mostly. I'd keep a few thousand as a buffer.

[SETTER]: Good, that's smart. That's helpful, thanks.

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** returned "everyone's doing it," which is a vague answer. I asked "online or people you actually know?" and got the real source: Kuba.
- **"How do you feel about that?"** was a risk, because it's a feeling question. He deflected ("I don't care, it's fine"), and the next sentence was the real one ("we used to be on the same level").
- **The job came from the story.** He volunteered it after I repeated "free."
- **No recap, no formal why-now.** "What happened in the day?" and "you reached out now rather than last year because of...?" covered it.
- **He wouldn't give an income** ("I'd rather not say exactly"). I offered a range, and he gave a number.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"One guy. Kuba."** Everyone online turned out to be one person he knows. That's where the feeling is.
- **"He bought a new laptop."** and **"he's got time to go to the gym at eleven."** He's watching Kuba closely. That's envy, said with a laugh.
- **"We used to be on the same level."** The real pain: being left behind by someone close.
- **"Three percent of nothing."** A joke about the raise. He's angrier about work than he lets on.
- **The photo of Kuba's desk.** The trigger. One monitor against three.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"Is that everyone online, or people you actually know?"** broke "everyone" down to one name.
- **"Do you believe him?"** is a gentle, curious question that got the laptop and the gym.
- **"Free."** I repeated his word and said nothing else.
- **"Did that get to you?"** about the raise. He admitted it a little, and I laughed with him.
- **I never asked whether Kuba really makes money.** It doesn't matter for the call.
