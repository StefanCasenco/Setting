# 02 · "I've been on YouTube for like two years. I'm just tired of doing nothing with it."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 5½ minutes · **Ends:** not booked (kind exit)

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so tell me, what had you interested in trading in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Uh, I don't know, it's kind of embarrassing. I've been on YouTube for like two years, watching all the guys, and I'm just tired of doing nothing with it. Like I know a lot of stuff. I just don't do anything.

[SETTER]: Embarrassing how?

[PROSPECT]: Because I'm twenty-six and I live with my parents and I make coffee for a living. So I'm not exactly the poster boy.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* Okay. How long have you been making coffee?

[PROSPECT]: Four years. It was supposed to be temporary. I started in college, I was gonna quit, and then I just never did.

[SETTER]: Right. And the living with your parents. Is that a money thing, or are you just comfortable there?

[PROSPECT]: Money. Well, kind of. I make eleven-fifty after tax, plus tips. I could technically afford a place, but I'd have nothing left. I save maybe four hundred a month like this. So I stay.

[SETTER]: Mm. What's it like being there?

[PROSPECT]: It's fine. They're cool. My mum still does my laundry sometimes, which is mortifying. My girlfriend came over once and my mum had folded my boxers. Like, folded them. Anyway.

*≈ 1:30*

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* And how long have you two been together?

[PROSPECT]: Two years. She's great. She's saving for a deposit, actually, and she's, well. I'm meant to be saving with her. I'm not putting in as much as I'd like.

[SETTER]: Mm.

[PROSPECT]: So she's putting in more than I am. And I don't love that.

[SETTER]: Does she say anything?

[PROSPECT]: No, never. She wouldn't. But the other week she goes, "are we ever going to move, or is this just it?" Not in a mean way. She was half joking. But it landed.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That'd land.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. And then my mate Dec buys a flat in Dublin. Dec, who I went to school with. I'm not jealous. I just look at it and I'm like, what did I do for four years.

[SETTER]: Do you say any of that to her?

[PROSPECT]: God, no. I make jokes. She thinks I'm fine. I do the "ah, we'll get there" voice.

[SETTER]: The "we'll get there" voice.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I do it a lot lately. I can hear myself doing it.

[SETTER]: What's it like, hearing yourself?

[PROSPECT]: Shit, honestly. Like I'm my dad. He does the same voice about the roof.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: Have you actually put money into trading, or has it been mostly watching?

[PROSPECT]: Mostly watching. I did a demo for a few months. And I put like three hundred euro in a real account last year and lost most of it in a week.

[SETTER]: What happened?

[PROSPECT]: I got scared and closed it. Then I thought, I don't know what I'm doing, I'll lose more. So I stopped.

[SETTER]: Yeah, that's pretty normal.

[PROSPECT]: Is it?

[SETTER]: Yeah. Most people don't have anyone to ask when it goes wrong.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, that's true. I didn't.

[SETTER]: Okay man, I know it's been two years, life goes on. But why now? What's making this the time you want to do something about it?

[PROSPECT]: The girlfriend thing, I think. And Dec's flat. It all kind of came at once.

*≈ 4:15*

[SETTER]: Okay. Let me ask you a bit about the money side, so I know where you're at. You mentioned you're saving about four hundred?

[PROSPECT]: Three-fifty, honestly. Four is a good month.

[SETTER]: Three-fifty. And in total, how much have you got put away?

[PROSPECT]: That's, it's personal, but about six hundred. That's it. I had more but I spent it on the account.

[SETTER]: Okay. Appreciate you being straight.

[PROSPECT]: I could borrow some off my brother, if it was for something good. He's said he'd lend me up to two grand.

[SETTER]: Hm. I wouldn't do that, man. I'd never want you borrowing for this.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. No. I know. I just, yeah.

[SETTER]: So here's where I'm at. Right now it wouldn't make sense to book you. I'd rather be straight with you than waste your time. I'll send you some resources, and let's pick it up in a month or two once you've got a bit more behind you. How's that sound?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. That's fair, actually. Thanks for being honest.

[SETTER]: Of course. You're doing the right thing by looking. Don't rush it.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** got a confession, not a goal. The "why" came out of "embarrassing."
- **Current** was most of the call, and the best material came sideways (the laundry, the flat in Dublin).
- **He answered part of the money ladder inside the story** ("eleven-fifty, I save four hundred"). So the money section at the end was only the total, plus a pushback.
- **Why now** used your wording because he'd given a vague "all at once" and the setter wanted the trigger. It got a half-answer. The setter didn't push for a better one.
- **The result** was a DQ at the savings number, said plainly.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"It's kind of embarrassing."** The opening line and the real subject.
- **"What did I do for four years."** His own cost, in one sentence.
- **"She's putting in more than I am."** The real pressure is his girlfriend's deposit.
- **"I could borrow some off my brother."** The point where the call changes: from sales to responsibility.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"Embarrassing how?"** went to his word and got "I make coffee for a living," which gave me the job without asking.
- **"Money thing, or comfortable?"** is a two-option. It got the real reason and a bit of his income, free.
- **"Does she say anything?"** pointed at the one person who matters. He told me the "are we ever going to move" line.
- **The DQ** said the truth (not the right time), made no suggestion about money, and left the door open.
