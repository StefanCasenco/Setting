# 08 · "Money."

**Offer:** trading · **Where it ended:** stopped at L2 on purpose

## The call

[SETTER]: What was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Money.

[SETTER]: Sure, everyone wants more money. But money's just a tool, right? What's the real reason behind it for you?

[PROSPECT]: Dunno. Stuff.

[SETTER]: Sounds like money's been tight lately?

[PROSPECT]: Kinda.

[SETTER]: Is it more that it runs out before the month does, or more that nothing's left after the bills?

[PROSPECT]: Nothing left after.

[SETTER]: Nothing left after. What do you mean?

[PROSPECT]: Pay comes in, bills go out. My mum asks for her share.

[SETTER]: Her share.

[PROSPECT]: Rent. I live at home.

[SETTER]: How's that been?

[PROSPECT]: It's fine.

[SETTER]: Got it, man.

## What I should've picked up on
- **Three answers in a row of one or two words.** I don't know yet why he's quiet: shy, guarded, not interested, or just not used to talking about it. I'm not deciding which.
- **"Kinda."** to the guess. That's the first yes in the call. Small, but real.
- **"Nothing left after."** He picked an option, which means the option was close to the truth.
- **"My mum asks for her share."** The only detail he offered unprompted. It's the closest he came to a scene.
- **"It's fine."** The door closed. This is the second flat answer in a row.

## Why I asked what I asked
- **The "money's just a tool" line** is the standard first reply to "money". It didn't work, which is normal. I did not repeat it.
- **I guessed out loud** ("sounds like money's been tight lately?"). It's easier to say yes to a guess than to produce an answer.
- **A two-option question** after that, with both options plausible. Easier again.
- **"Her share."** I repeated the one phrase that was his and didn't ask anything. It got me "rent. I live at home."
- **I stopped after "it's fine."** That's two tries on the same layer and he's still on the surface.

## Where it could have gone wrong
- Asking three open questions in a row ("why is that important to you? what do you want to do with it?"). Someone this short on words shuts down.
- The default job chain. It would have got "I work at a shop. It's alright" and nothing more.
- Forcing the feeling question. He'd have said "I dunno" and I'd have made it worse.

## Exit test
**Not met, and that's the point.** What I have is:
- Money is tight; nothing left after bills.
- Lives with his mum and pays her a share.
- Not much else, and he doesn't want to talk about it.

I say "Got it, man" and move on. The rest of the call (how long, what he's tried, why now, the money) fills in the picture. For the closer I note: *guarded, lives at home, pays rent to mum, tight money. Pain not explored.*

This is a normal outcome, not a failure. Atefur: surface answers are normal; better to move on than force it.
