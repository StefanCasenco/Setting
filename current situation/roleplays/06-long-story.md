# 06 · A long story in the first answer

**Offer:** trading · **Where it ended:** exit test met

## The call

[SETTER]: What was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly it's a long story. I had a restaurant, it closed in covid, took a loan on it, then I did agency work, then my dad got sick and I was back and forth, and my wife went back to work, and my brother said look into trading, so I watched videos all summer.

[SETTER]: Hold on, back up. Your wife went back to work.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. She'd been home with the kids. She went back full time to cover the loan.

[SETTER]: To cover the loan. How's that been?

[PROSPECT]: It's fine. She doesn't complain.

[SETTER]: She doesn't complain.

[PROSPECT]: No. That's kind of the problem. She just does it.

[SETTER]: What do you mean?

[PROSPECT]: Like she's carrying it and I'm the one who took the loan. I feel like I should be the one fixing it.

[SETTER]: What's the hardest part of that?

[PROSPECT]: She's tired all the time. I see it. I can't do anything about it.

[SETTER]: *(pause)*

[PROSPECT]: I used to be the one who provided. Now I'm doing agency work for a fraction of what I made.

[SETTER]: How long's it been like this?

[PROSPECT]: Almost three years.

[SETTER]: Got it, man.

## What I should've picked up on
- **Six topics in one breath:** restaurant, loan, agency work, dad, wife, brother. I can't follow all of them and I shouldn't try.
- **The topic that had a person paying for it.** "My wife went back to work" is the only one where somebody else's life changed because of this. That's where the weight is.
- **"Dad got sick."** This is real, and I'm leaving it. It's heavy and it's not why he booked a trading call. If he comes back to it, I'll follow him.
- **"She doesn't complain."** followed by **"that's kind of the problem."** He's saying the real thing, very quietly.
- **"I used to be the one who provided."** Said after my silence. That's the cost, in his words.

## Why I asked what I asked
- **"Hold on, back up."** A warm interruption. Letting a talker keep going just gets more story. I picked one sentence and went back to it.
- **I picked the wife**, not the restaurant. The restaurant is the history; the wife is the present.
- **"How's that been?"** is open, but it's tied to one person and one fact, so it doesn't invite another monologue.
- **I repeated "She doesn't complain"** and said nothing else. A flat echo got more than a question would have.
- **The silence** after "I can't do anything about it" got me "I used to be the one who provided."

## Where it could have gone wrong
- Asking about the loan, the restaurant or the agency. They're facts. He'd have given me more facts.
- Picking "dad got sick" because it sounds the most serious. It would have turned the call into something he didn't book for.
- Following the default: "what do you do work-wise?" He told me: agency work. Asking again shows I wasn't listening.

## Exit test
- **Pain, in his words:** "my wife went back to work to cover the loan… she's tired all the time… I used to be the one who provided."
- **Feeling:** he should be the one fixing it, and he can't.
- **Cost:** almost three years of watching his wife carry it.
