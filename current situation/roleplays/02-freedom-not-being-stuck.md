# 02 · "Freedom, I guess. Not being stuck."

**Offer:** ecom · **Where it ended:** exit test met

## The call

[SETTER]: What had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Freedom, I guess. Not being stuck.

[SETTER]: What makes you feel like you don't have that freedom right now?

[PROSPECT]: The job. Never any time, you know.

[SETTER]: Never any time. What do you mean?

[PROSPECT]: I'm out the door at six, back at seven. Then I'm done.

[SETTER]: Home at seven and you're done. What's that like?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly the weekends too. I'm just wrecked.

[SETTER]: Wrecked. What does a weekend look like?

[PROSPECT]: Sleep till ten. Laundry. By Sunday I'm already thinking about Monday.

[SETTER]: Who else is in that picture?

[PROSPECT]: Why does that matter?

[SETTER]: I'm not asking just for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, like a personal trainer would.

[PROSPECT]: Okay, fair. My wife. And my daughter, she's four.

[SETTER]: What's it like with your daughter?

[PROSPECT]: She's usually asleep when I get home. Weekends I'm kind of half there.

[SETTER]: Half there.

[PROSPECT]: *(pause)* Yeah. She asked me the other day if I live at work. She was joking.

[SETTER]: *(pause)* How did that land?

[PROSPECT]: Not great. I laughed it off.

[SETTER]: Got it, man.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Not being stuck"** is a vague word, but it was followed within one sentence by **"never any time."** The weight was on "time", not "stuck".
- **"I'm just wrecked."** He moved from hours to the weekend by himself. That tells me it's not only a work problem; it's everything.
- **"Why does that matter?"** is a small test, not an attack. He was ready to answer once he knew why I was asking.
- **"Half there"** is the real line. It's his phrase, not mine, and it's the cost.
- **"I laughed it off."** He covers it with a joke. That's how he deals with it, and I won't push past it.

## Why I asked what I asked
- **"What makes you feel like you don't have that freedom right now?"** is the early-impact line. It uses his word and goes straight to the present.
- **"Never any time. What do you mean?"** instead of the default "what do you do work-wise?" He had already told me there's a job. Asking would have been a question I already knew the answer to.
- **"What does a weekend look like?"** is a scene. It's more specific than "how many hours do you work".
- **"Who else is in that picture?"** Time always costs somebody. Asking for the people is how L4 arrives without me saying "how does that make you feel".
- **"How did that land?"** He gave me a detail with emotion in it. This lets him say what it did, without a feelings word.

## Where it could have gone wrong
- "How do you feel about the job?" He didn't say he disliked it. He said there was no time. That question points him at the wrong complaint.
- Going to "what would you do with more time?" That's desire, and the pain hadn't landed yet.

## Exit test
- **Pain, in his words:** "never any time… I'm just wrecked… weekends I'm half there."
- **Feeling:** wrecked, guilty.
- **Cost:** his four-year-old asked if he lives at work.
