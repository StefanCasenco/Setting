# 15 · "I'm nineteen and I want to start a store, but I've got like two hundred bucks."

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 5½ minutes · **Ends:** not booked, kindly

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Okay, so, I'm nineteen, I'm in my second year of uni, and I've been watching a ton of ecom videos, and honestly I think I could do it. I just don't have a lot of money. Like at all. Is that a problem?

[SETTER]: We'll get to that. What got you into it?

[PROSPECT]: Okay so my cousin has a store. He sells these LED strip lights for gamers. And he's like twenty and doing about eight grand a month. And I was like, what? And then I started looking.

[SETTER]: Eight grand. Does he work on it full time?

[PROSPECT]: No, he's at uni too! That's what got me. He's just packing boxes between lectures.

*≈ 1:00*

[SETTER]: And what's it like for you right now? Do you work?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, I work at Chipotle, like twenty hours a week. It pays for my phone and food, and I live with my parents.

[SETTER]: How are they about all that?

[PROSPECT]: They're good, but, um. It's a bit tight at home. My dad lost his job last year and he's doing warehouse stuff now, it's not the same. And my mom keeps saying don't worry, but you can tell. So I don't ask for money.

[SETTER]: Mm. What do you do with that?

[PROSPECT]: I try to cover my own stuff. And I feel guilty, because they're paying my tuition on a loan. Every time they pay it, I'm like, I should be doing something about this.

[SETTER]: What would doing something look like?

[PROSPECT]: Making money. Real money. Not Chipotle money. Being the guy who helps. Right now I'm the guy who needs help.

[SETTER]: That's a heavy thing to think about at nineteen.

[PROSPECT]: Ha. Yeah. It's whatever. I just want to do something.

[SETTER]: Do your parents know you feel like that?

[PROSPECT]: No! God, no. My dad would be so embarrassed. He's like, "you just focus on school." But I see how he looks at the mail.

[SETTER]: How does he look at it?

[PROSPECT]: Like it's going to bite him. He opens it standing up, in the hall. Like he's going to be told bad news.

*≈ 2:45*

[SETTER]: How long have you been looking at ecom?

[PROSPECT]: Since the summer. Three, four months. I've watched probably a hundred videos. I haven't started a store because I don't have money for the stock or the ads.

[SETTER]: Anything in the way besides money?

[PROSPECT]: Not really. I'm busy, but I'd make time. Sorry, my roommate's telling me something. One sec. *(muffled)* Yeah, I'll be out in a minute! Sorry.

[SETTER]: No problem. What made you start looking properly, now?

[PROSPECT]: My dad. He was sitting at the table with the bills, and he didn't know I was there. And he just put his head in his hands. For like a minute. And I went back upstairs.

[SETTER]: Yeah. I'm sorry, man.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. It's fine. It's just, yeah.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Alright, I do have to ask about the money. Is that okay?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, sure.

[SETTER]: What would you want to be making every month from ecom, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly? Three, four grand. Then I could help.

[SETTER]: And what are you making now?

[PROSPECT]: About eight hundred a month, from Chipotle.

[SETTER]: And how much can you save from that?

[PROSPECT]: Maybe a hundred. Some months nothing.

[SETTER]: And in total, what do you have saved?

[PROSPECT]: Um. About two hundred. Honestly. I'm sorry. Is that bad?

[SETTER]: No, don't be sorry, it's a real answer. Is there any other money you could use? Like from your parents?

[PROSPECT]: My mom said she could maybe help. If it was real.

[SETTER]: I'd leave that one, man. I wouldn't want you using your parents' money, especially after what you told me about your dad. That's their breathing room.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. That's fair.

*≈ 5:15*

[SETTER]: Here's where I'm at. Right now it wouldn't make sense to book you in. It's not about you, it's the timing. You've got a good head and a good reason, and I'd rather not put you in a spot where you need money you don't have. I'll send you some free stuff to build on, and let's pick it up when you've built up a bit. Does that sound okay?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Yeah, no, I get it. Thanks for being honest with me.

[SETTER]: Of course. And your dad's lucky to have you thinking this way.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** came with nerves ("is that a problem?"). I said "we'll get to that," so he didn't feel judged by it.
- **The job and the family came out in the same answer.** "How are they about all that?" was the soft way into the money at home.
- **No recap.** A DQ call doesn't need a tidy summary.
- **Why now** was a plain "what made you start looking properly, now?", and he told me about his dad at the table.
- **The money part** found the honest answer ("about two hundred"). The close-out names why and leaves a door open.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Is that a problem?"** in the first minute. He was nervous about the same thing I was going to ask.
- **"Packing boxes between lectures."** What got him excited.
- **"I don't ask for money."** The detail that said what home is like.
- **"Right now I'm the guy who needs help."** His sentence for the pain.
- **"My mom said she could maybe help."** The point where a setter has to hold a line.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"How are they about all that?"** reached what's going on at home without asking about money.
- **"What would doing something look like?"** gave him a chance to say what he wants. I only asked it after the pain was on the table.
- **I told him not to use his parents' money.** He didn't ask. His dad's situation made it obvious.
- **The DQ is warm and specific.** It names why, and what the next step is. I left out any suggestions for where to find money.
