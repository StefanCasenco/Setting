# Roleplays: what a real CURRENT looks like

Twenty calls: ten trading, ten ecom. Each one runs from the intent question through the money questions, the way a live setting call does.

## What these are, and aren't
- **Not word tracks.** The order and the wording change from call to call. Recap is sometimes skipped. Why-now is sometimes asked, sometimes already answered. The money questions come in a different order when the prospect has already said something. That's on purpose.
- **Messy on purpose.** Prospects ramble, get interrupted, answer a different question, shrug, joke and contradict themselves. The setter misses things, guesses wrong and picks the thread that matters.
- **Not categorised up front.** The files are named after the first thing the prospect said, because that's all you know when you dial.
- **About 4–6½ minutes of spoken conversation each.** The "Real length" line in each file is an estimate from word count. Two are short on purpose (06, a low-intent caller, and 10, someone with five minutes).
- **Invented people.** None of them are from your real calls or roleplays.

## How to use them
1. Read a call straight through, out loud, setter side.
2. Cover the notes. At each setter line, guess what you'd ask.
3. Read "Where the framework landed" to see which steps were used, folded together, or skipped, and why.
4. Read "What I should've picked up on" and "Why those questions and not the defaults."
5. Then talk to real people. Reading is not reps.

## The fixed bits you'll see
The money ladder (goal, how far, how much saved a month, "ish", usual or tight month, total saved), the "it takes money to make money" pushback line, and the why-now wording appear in several calls, close to word for word. In others they're said differently, because the point is the principle: contextualise the question, and don't make it feel like a form.
