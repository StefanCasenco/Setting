# 04 · "I make decent money. I just don't want to depend on one paycheck."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 5 minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: I make decent money, to be clear. I'm not struggling. I'm a sales manager and I do well. I just don't like depending on one paycheck. Last year they laid off a third of our department, and I thought, that could've been me.

[SETTER]: Were you close to it?

[PROSPECT]: Not really. I'm good at what I do. But that's the thing, it doesn't matter if you're good. They called people in on a Tuesday. A guy I'd worked with for eleven years. Gone by lunch.

[SETTER]: Eleven years. That's rough.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. And I'm not a beginner at investing, I want to say that. Index funds, a 401k, I do my homework. I just want something that isn't tied to an employer.

*≈ 1:00*

[SETTER]: What happened to him? The guy.

[PROSPECT]: He's fine, he found something. Took him five months. He had a mortgage and two kids. He told me the worst part wasn't the money, it was three in the morning. Sitting up.

[SETTER]: Did that stay with you?

[PROSPECT]: *(pause)* Yeah. It did, actually. I've got a daughter. She's twelve. I thought, if that was me, what would I tell her? And I didn't have an answer.

[SETTER]: Mm.

[PROSPECT]: We're fine on paper. We've got savings. But it's all because of one job. And I've started noticing it in meetings, at the start of the quarter. Everyone's quiet. You can see everyone doing the math on who's next.

[SETTER]: So even when you're safe, it doesn't feel safe.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Exactly. That's it exactly.

[SETTER]: Does your wife feel it too?

[PROSPECT]: She doesn't know how much I think about it. She's an elementary school teacher, her job is basically untouchable. So she's like, "we're fine." And we are. On paper. I just can't switch it off.

[SETTER]: Do you ever tell her?

[PROSPECT]: I tried once. She said, "you're being dramatic." Not mean. And I thought, okay, maybe I am. So I stopped.

[SETTER]: Mm.

[PROSPECT]: But I also ran the numbers on what we'd do. Six months. We'd be okay for six months. After that, it's the house.

*≈ 2:45*

[SETTER]: Got it. So how long have you been looking at trading?

[PROSPECT]: Since the layoffs. About ten months. A couple of books. I took a course on options, which was honestly a waste. The guy talked about his Lamborghini.

[SETTER]: Ha. Yeah. Have you traded anything?

[PROSPECT]: Small positions. I'm up a little, down a little. I don't have a system. I have opinions.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* Opinions.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. That's the gap. I know enough to be dangerous.

[SETTER]: And what's different now, compared to six months ago?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly? I'm forty-four. If I wait another five years it's a different conversation. And they're talking about "restructuring" again next quarter.

*≈ 3:45*

[SETTER]: Okay. So on the money side. What would you want trading to be adding every month for it to be worth your time?

[PROSPECT]: Twelve thousand would be my number. Not to replace the job. To make the job optional.

[SETTER]: Okay. And what are you on at the moment?

[PROSPECT]: About ninety-five a year. So six and a half a month, after tax.

[SETTER]: Right. And what do you put away from that?

[PROSPECT]: Two thousand, give or take. Fifteen hundred in a slow month.

[SETTER]: And in total, what have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: Between retirement and cash, a fair bit. But what I could put toward this is about twenty thousand. And I'd want to do it right, not throw it at the market.

[SETTER]: Is that something you could put to work without it hurting you if it didn't go well?

[PROSPECT]: Yes. It's separate from the emergency fund. I'm not an idiot.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* No, no. That helps, man.

[PROSPECT]: Can I ask how much this costs? Before I'm too far into it.

[SETTER]: Fair. It's a four-figure investment, depending on the program, but we can't even know if we can help you yet. That's what the advisor call is for.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. That's about what I expected.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** returned a defensive start ("to be clear… I'm not a beginner"). I didn't argue with it.
- **There was a long current section**, and it was mostly about someone else (the colleague). The prospect's own fear came from "did that stay with you?"
- **The recap became a guess:** "even when you're safe, it doesn't feel safe." That did the job.
- **Timeline and why now** were folded into one stretch of three short questions.
- **He asked the price himself**, near the end. I gave the taster once.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"To be clear. I'm not struggling."** He needs me to know he isn't desperate. A status wall. I leave it alone.
- **"Gone by lunch."** The core fear, as a story about someone else.
- **"What would I tell her?"** The point where it stopped being about money.
- **"Everyone doing the math on who's next."** A detail only someone living in it would give.
- **"I have opinions."** He's self-aware, which makes it an easier call.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"Were you close to it?"** was a real question, not a technique.
- **"What happened to him?"** let him tell the story of the friend before the story of himself.
- **"Did that stay with you?"** moved from the friend to him, with no feelings word.
- **I asked if the twenty thousand could be lost without harm.** He had already separated the emergency fund, but I check anyway.
