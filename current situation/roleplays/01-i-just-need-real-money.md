# 01 · "Honestly? I just need to make some real money."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 6 minutes · **Ends:** qualified, with a flag

Times in italics are rough, just so you can feel the pace. The framework steps aren't labelled in the call on purpose; where each one landed is in the notes.

## The call

[SETTER]: Cool. So what was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Uh. Honestly? Man, I just need to make some real money. I've been looking at this stuff off and on for a while, and it just seems like one of the only things where somebody isn't deciding what you get paid, you know?

[SETTER]: Ha, yeah. So somebody's deciding right now?

[PROSPECT]: Every week when they post the schedule. They cut my hours last month. Didn't ask, just posted it. I was at forty-five, now I'm at like thirty-two. "Temporary."

[SETTER]: Temporary.

[PROSPECT]: That's what the supervisor said. Then I see two new guys on nights. *(laughs)* So.

[SETTER]: Damn. And that was last month?

[PROSPECT]: Four weeks ago. Second week of September. Hold on one sec. Nah, it's my phone. Okay. Sorry.

[SETTER]: You good?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, yeah, my cousin. I'll hit him back. Anyway.

*≈ 1:00*

[SETTER]: Is it the hours that's on your mind the most right now, or is it more than that?

[PROSPECT]: It's the hours, and it's, Keisha's pregnant. Due in January.

[SETTER]: Oh, congrats man.

[PROSPECT]: Thanks. Yeah. First one. I'm happy. I'm also kind of freaking out, I'm not gonna lie.

[SETTER]: What's the freaking out part?

[PROSPECT]: Just the math, man. Rent's twelve-fifty. I looked up daycare, like for real the other day, and it's fourteen hundred a month. That's more than rent! And my hours just got cut.

[SETTER]: Fourteen hundred. Wow.

[PROSPECT]: I know. And she does hair, so her money's whatever she books. Some weeks it's good, some weeks it's like a hundred bucks.

[SETTER]: Mm. How's she taking it all?

[PROSPECT]: She's good. She's the calm one. She keeps saying we'll figure it out. Which is nice, but I'm like, I'm supposed to have figured it out already, you know?

[SETTER]: Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: Anyway.

[SETTER]: Do you tell her that?

[PROSPECT]: Nah. She thinks we're tight. I think we're, like, one bad month from not okay.

[SETTER]: That's a lot to carry on your own.

[PROSPECT]: It is. *(pause)* It's cool though. I just gotta, yeah.

[SETTER]: When did it start feeling like that?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly? The ultrasound. Eight weeks. Everybody's crying and I'm in the corner doing math. Like, what is wrong with me?

[SETTER]: Nothing's wrong with you, man. You're thinking about them.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. That's what Keisha said when I told her like a tiny bit of it. She said "stop doing the math at the ultrasound."

[SETTER]: Ha. She knows you.

[PROSPECT]: She knows me too well.

*≈ 2:50*

[SETTER]: So did you start looking at trading before the hours thing, or after?

[PROSPECT]: Before. Like eight months ago. A buddy at work was doing it, showed me his phone. I watched a ton of YouTube. TJR, all those guys. Then I paper traded.

[SETTER]: How'd that go?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly I was making money on paper. Then I looked at the prop firm stuff, and it's a hundred and fifty bucks to try an evaluation, and I'd get to the checkout and just. Not do it. Eight months of that. Kinda circling.

[SETTER]: Mm. Yeah, that happens a lot.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah.

[SETTER]: So what made you book the call now? Eight months of circling, what tipped it?

[PROSPECT]: The hours, I guess. When they cut my hours, it was like, okay, I can't wait on this. I can't keep telling myself next month. Plus the baby, obviously. It's a clock now.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Got it. Let me ask you something quick on the money side so I know where you're at. If this works out, what are we looking to make from trading each month, say a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Eight. Like eight grand. That'd be, yeah.

[SETTER]: Okay, sweet. And how far are we from that right now? What are you pulling in monthly?

[PROSPECT]: Twenty-nine hundred, take-home. With the new hours.

[SETTER]: Right. And with how expensive everything is, how much are you able to save from that each month?

[PROSPECT]: Two hundred. Maybe. On a good month.

[SETTER]: Two hundred-ish. Is it more like one-fifty or two-fifty?

[PROSPECT]: One-fifty now. Since the hours.

[SETTER]: Got it. And in total, what do you have saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why? ...I mean, we've got about forty-five hundred. But that's the baby stuff. Crib, hospital, all that.

[SETTER]: Yeah, no, that makes sense. Is any of that free, or is it all spoken for?

[PROSPECT]: Maybe like two thousand isn't spoken for. But Keisha doesn't know I'm thinking about this.

[SETTER]: Okay. Honestly I'd leave the baby money out of it. Thanks for being straight with me, that helps.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Cool.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** was the first question. His answer was the cut hours, not "money."
- **Early impact** never needed its own line; "so somebody's deciding right now?" did the job.
- **Recap** was skipped. He'd said it all, and a tidy summary would have cut across "I just gotta, yeah."
- **Timeline and what got in the way** came together in one question ("before the hours thing, or after?").
- **Why now** was asked once, as "what made you book the call now?", because most of it was already on the table.
- **The money part** was the ladder, with a normal pushback ("Why?") and a lower bar (leaving it unanswered a beat).

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Somebody isn't deciding what you get paid."** A vague line that was really the hours cut.
- **"I'm supposed to have figured it out already."** The feeling, and his own words.
- **"She thinks we're tight. I think we're one bad month from not okay."** The cost: he's carrying it alone.
- **"It's cool though. I just gotta, yeah."** He closed the door. I moved on.
- **"Keisha doesn't know I'm thinking about this."** A flag for the closer, not a lever.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"So somebody's deciding right now?"** instead of "what do you do for work?" He told me anyway, in a way that mattered.
- **"What's the freaking-out part?"** came straight from his words.
- **"Do you tell her that?"** came from "I'm supposed to have figured it out." It went to who he can't say it to.
- **"I'd leave the baby money out of it."** He said it's for the baby. I won't help anyone put money they need at risk.
