## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** gave a social-proof answer, with a "should" in it. I asked about the "should."
- **The real subject was the marriage and the money,** not trading. It was one question away.
- **No recap.** "You're carrying it, and also protecting him" did the job.
- **Timeline and why now** were two short questions: what she's looked at, and what changed. The answer was a missed payroll.
- **Money** was the ladder, with the usual "is that necessary?" pushback.
- **The "no promises" line** went in near the end, because she's wary of people who promise.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"I should be doing the same."** and **"I should be able to figure out something like this."** Two "shoulds" in the first minute. The pressure is internal.
- **"We haven't really talked about how difficult."** The core of the call.
- **"It just kept being me."** How she became the one who carries it, without a decision.
- **"I log in on my phone in the toilets at work."** A concrete, private scene.
- **"It's a very polite house."** Her phrase for the silence. She laughed, and then she said "heavy."
- **"Nobody has said a word about December."** The cost, through the children.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"Should be?"** repeated her word, and she explained.
- **"What's the 'should' about?"** is a plain, curious question about a word. It turned a comparison into her situation.
- **"Does he know you're covering it?"** came right after "I just did it." It went to the person on the other end.
- **"So you're carrying it, and also protecting him."** A statement. She said "I didn't say it like that before."
- **I asked whether her husband knows about this.** Not as a hurdle. The advisor should know, and she should decide how to tell him.
