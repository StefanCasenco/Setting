# 17 · "Before anything, how much is it?"

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 5 minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Hey Elena? This is Ștefan, it looks like you booked from an ad about help with ecom. Is that right?

[PROSPECT]: Yes, hi. Before we start, how much is it? I just want to know the price.

[SETTER]: It's a four-figure investment, depending on the program. But we can't even know if we can help you yet. That's what the next call is for.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. That's, okay. Fine.

[SETTER]: Cool. So what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: I'm a freelance designer. In Berlin. I've been freelancing four years. And the income is either a good month or nothing. And I'm tired of not knowing.

[SETTER]: Not knowing what?

[PROSPECT]: If I can pay my rent next month. I'll have a month where I make four thousand euros and I think, great, and then the next two months I make nine hundred. And I'm constantly doing math.

*≈ 1:15*

[SETTER]: What does the math look like?

[PROSPECT]: I look at my account every morning. I know what's coming in and what's going out, and I do it in my head before I get out of bed. It's a bad habit.

[SETTER]: Every morning.

[PROSPECT]: Yes. And it makes me not want to take risks. Like, I turned down a trip with friends last month because I didn't know if I'd have the money. And I could have gone. It turned out I had a good month.

[SETTER]: That's frustrating.

[PROSPECT]: Yes. Because you never know. And it's not that I'm poor. I'm not. I'm just never sure. Hold on, my cat. Mimi! No. Sorry. Yes.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* All good. What would sure look like?

[PROSPECT]: A baseline. Something that comes in every month that I don't have to chase. Even if it's only a thousand. So I could do my work from a calm place and not from "I need this client."

[SETTER]: Do you have anyone you talk to about the money stuff?

[PROSPECT]: My boyfriend. He's a teacher, he has a salary. So he doesn't really get it. He says, "just take more clients." Which, thank you, very helpful.

[SETTER]: Ha. Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: He's nice about it. But it's like asking a bird to explain swimming.

*≈ 2:45*

[SETTER]: How long have you been looking at ecom?

[PROSPECT]: About eight months. Videos, podcasts. I thought about prints, since I design. Wall art, posters.

[SETTER]: Did you try any?

[PROSPECT]: I made a few designs. I even put up a test store. I sold four posters, to friends, I think. And then I stopped because I didn't know how to get strangers to buy.

[SETTER]: Mm. That's a real gap.

[PROSPECT]: Yes.

[SETTER]: What made you want help this month rather than six months ago?

[PROSPECT]: A big client paid late. Six weeks. I had to ask my sister to lend me money for rent. For four days. It was so embarrassing.

[SETTER]: Ah. Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: I paid her back. But I don't want to be that person again.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Okay, now the money side, if that's alright.

[PROSPECT]: Yes.

[SETTER]: What are we looking to make from ecom every month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Two thousand a month, steady. Then I'd be calm.

[SETTER]: And how far are we from that, in terms of what you earn?

[PROSPECT]: From ecom, zero. From design, it averages twenty-six hundred, but it jumps.

[SETTER]: Right. And how much do you save, on average?

[PROSPECT]: Four hundred, five hundred in a good month. Sometimes nothing.

[SETTER]: And in total, how much have you saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why do you ask?

[SETTER]: Fair. If you start a store there's usually some money going in at the start, so I just want to know you've got room. A ballpark's fine.

[PROSPECT]: About thirty-five hundred. I had more, but the client thing.

[SETTER]: Is that free to use, or is it your safety net?

[PROSPECT]: Hmm. It's the only cushion I have. But it's what I'd use to start something, if I did.

[SETTER]: I get that. I'd want you to think about it carefully, because it's the only cushion. That's what the call is for.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Thanks. I appreciate that you said it.

## Where the framework landed
- **The price came first,** before the intent. I gave the taster once, kept it short, and moved on.
- **Intent** brought out her income swing, in her words.
- **The scene** ("I look at my account every morning, before I get out of bed") came from one question, "what does the math look like?"
- **No recap.** Her "baseline" sentence was the summary.
- **Timeline and why now** came together, and the late client was the trigger.
- **The money part used your pushback line** and a check on whether the savings were her only cushion.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"How much is it?" in the first line.** A price-first caller is anxious about price, not necessarily cheap.
- **"I do it in my head before I even get out of bed."** The scene. It's specific and private.
- **"I turned down a trip with friends because I didn't know if I'd have the money."** The cost, in an event. It turned out to be unnecessary, which is the sting.
- **"A baseline… something I don't have to chase."** Her word for what's missing.
- **"I had to ask my sister to lend me money for rent."** The why-now. A short, mortifying moment.
- **"It's the only cushion I have."** The flag for the closer.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **I answered the price question honestly and briefly.** She needed it to relax.
- **"Not knowing what?"** came from her word. It's more natural than asking what she does for work, which she'd said.
- **"What would sure look like?"** was a gentle future question, but only after the pain was clear. It gave me "baseline."
- **I told her to think about the cushion.** I made no promises and didn't push.
