# 10 · "I've got about five minutes, I'm on a site."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 4 minutes · **Ends:** qualified, quickly

## The call

[SETTER]: Hey Chris? This is Ștefan, it looks like you booked from an ad about help with trading. Is that right?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, yeah. Listen, I've got about five minutes. I'm on a site.

[SETTER]: No stress, I've got about five minutes too. What was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Deposit, mostly. I'm trying to buy a house. Melbourne. And it keeps moving.

[SETTER]: How do you mean, it keeps moving?

[PROSPECT]: The target. I started saving four years ago and I thought I'd need a hundred and twenty for a deposit. Now it's a hundred and sixty. Every year the number goes up faster than I save. It's like running after a bus.

[SETTER]: Four years. Has it been getting you down?

[PROSPECT]: Not down. Annoying. Sorry, hold on. *(muffled)* Yeah, mate, put it by the door. Sorry. Yeah.

[SETTER]: All good.

[PROSPECT]: It's annoying more than anything. I'm an electrician, I make good money, I save. My wife and I do everything right. And it still doesn't move.

*≈ 1:30*

[SETTER]: Is your wife on the same page about it?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. She's actually more stressed than me. We've got a two-year-old and we're in a rental unit. She says, you know, he'll be at school and we'll still be here.

[SETTER]: Right.

[PROSPECT]: I don't love hearing it. But she's right.

[SETTER]: What's the hard part, you reckon?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly? That I'm doing everything right and I'm not getting anywhere. If I was doing something wrong, I'd fix it.

[SETTER]: Who else knows you feel that way?

[PROSPECT]: Nobody, mate. I'm a tradie. We don't have those conversations. My apprentice asked me yesterday if I was alright because I was quiet. I said I'd had a bad coffee.

[SETTER]: Ha. A bad coffee.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Anyway.

*≈ 2:15*

[SETTER]: Have you tried trading yet?

[PROSPECT]: A bit. I've done a couple of free courses. Put a grand in once, broke even, got bored. No plan.

[SETTER]: And what's making you look at it now?

[PROSPECT]: House prices went up another six percent this year. I looked at the numbers on the weekend and I thought, I can't just keep saving.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: Got it. Quick money stuff, since you're short on time. What are we looking to make from trading each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Three grand extra. That'd do it.

[SETTER]: And what are you on right now, take-home?

[PROSPECT]: Seven and a half. Plus my wife's part time, so another thirty-five hundred.

[SETTER]: Cool. How much of that do you save each month?

[PROSPECT]: About two and a half. Three, if there's no surprises.

[SETTER]: That your usual month?

[PROSPECT]: Usual. We're pretty disciplined.

[SETTER]: And in total, what have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: The deposit fund's about a hundred and ten. But that's the deposit, I'm not touching it. Outside of that, maybe fifteen in a separate account.

[SETTER]: Good. And is the fifteen sitting there for anything?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. That's the "this didn't work out and I'm fine" money.

[SETTER]: Perfect. That's helpful, mate. Let me get you your next step.

## Where the framework landed
- **A tight call**, because he said so. Everything is smaller, not skipped.
- **Intent** returned his entire problem in one sentence: "it keeps moving."
- **The wife and the two-year-old** came in through one question.
- **No recap.** At five minutes, the pain was clear and the call was moving.
- **Why now** was the only extra: six percent in house prices.
- **The money ladder** ran in one pass because his answers were clean.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"It keeps moving."** The first thing he said, and the whole problem.
- **"Like running after a bus."** His own image.
- **"Not down. Annoying."** He corrected my word. He's not a feelings guy. Don't force it.
- **"If I was doing something wrong, I'd fix it."** The cost, in a practical man's terms.
- **"She's more stressed than me."** The wife is the pressure point. He gave me enough.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"How do you mean, it keeps moving?"** instead of "what do you do for work?" He was already telling me about his work in the context of the house.
- **"Has it been getting you down?"** was a guess. It was wrong, and his correction gave me a better word.
- **"What's the hard part, you reckon?"** is a lighter version of "what's the hardest part."
- **I matched his speed.** I said five minutes and I kept to it. That's why he stayed.
