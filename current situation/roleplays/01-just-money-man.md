# 01 · "Just money, man."

**Offer:** trading · **Where it ended:** exit test met

## The call

[SETTER]: What was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Just money, man.

[SETTER]: Sure, everyone wants more money. But money's just a tool, right? What's the real reason behind it for you?

[PROSPECT]: Dunno. Tired of being broke, I guess.

[SETTER]: Broke. What do you mean?

[PROSPECT]: Like paycheck to paycheck. Rent goes out and I'm at nothing.

[SETTER]: What's the end of the month like?

[PROSPECT]: Meh. I skip stuff. Don't really go out.

[SETTER]: Skip stuff like what?

[PROSPECT]: Food, sometimes. Not like starving. I'll just say I already ate.

[SETTER]: Who do you say that to?

[PROSPECT]: My girlfriend. *(short laugh)* She thinks I'm just not hungry.

[SETTER]: What's the hardest part of that?

[PROSPECT]: Lying to her, honestly. She doesn't know it's that bad.

[SETTER]: *(pause)* How long's it been like that?

[PROSPECT]: Couple years. Since I moved out.

[SETTER]: Okay. Where does that go if nothing changes?

[PROSPECT]: Dunno. I try not to think about it, honestly.

[SETTER]: Got it, man. Appreciate you being straight with me.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Tired of being broke."** The first real word, and it's his, not mine. Everything after it comes from "broke".
- **"I skip stuff."** He offered this before I asked for it. It's the scene.
- **The short laugh at "she thinks I'm just not hungry."** A laugh that doesn't sound funny is a door.
- **"I try not to think about it."** A deflection. He's telling me where the floor is. I stopped.

## Why I asked what I asked
- **"Broke. What do you mean?"** instead of "what do you do for work?" Work is a fact I want. "Broke" is what he gave me. Facts about the job can come later, inside the story or in the handoff.
- **"What's the end of the month like?"** is a scene question. It gets the picture without a "how do you feel".
- **"Who do you say that to?"** He said "I'll just say I already ate", which means someone is on the other end of that. Asking about the person is how the impact shows up on its own.
- **"What's the hardest part of that?"** is the feeling question without therapy-speak.
- **One pass on the cost** ("where does that go if nothing changes"). He deflected, so I let it go.

## Where it could have gone wrong
After "paycheck to paycheck" the default would be "how much do you make?" That's a financial question for later, and it would have turned a person into a number just as he was getting honest.

## Exit test
- **Pain, in his words:** "paycheck to paycheck… I skip stuff… I'll say I already ate."
- **Feeling:** ashamed, hiding it.
- **Cost:** lying to his girlfriend, for a couple of years.
