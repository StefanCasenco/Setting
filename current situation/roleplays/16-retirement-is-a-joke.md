# 16 · "My retirement's a joke, to be honest."

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 5½ minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Well, it's. Hm. My retirement's a joke, to be honest. I'm fifty-two. I've been driving trucks twenty-six years. And I've got a 401k that wouldn't buy a used car.

[SETTER]: Twenty-six years.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Since I was twenty-six, actually. Half my life.

[SETTER]: What's the driving like these days?

[PROSPECT]: It's okay. It's long. I'm over the road, so I'm gone two, three weeks, home four days, gone again. Oklahoma City is home. My wife's there. We've got two grown kids.

[SETTER]: How's your wife with that?

[PROSPECT]: She's used to it. She's been used to it twenty years. She has her thing, her church, her sister. But when I'm home I can tell I'm kind of a guest.

[SETTER]: A guest.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. She's got her routines. I come in, I mess them up, I leave. It's nobody's fault.

*≈ 1:45*

[SETTER]: Does it bug you?

[PROSPECT]: I don't know. I don't know why I'm telling you this. I'm not much of a talker.

[SETTER]: No, I appreciate it.

[PROSPECT]: Well. It does. A little. I want to be there. I want to come home and not feel like I need to ask where the plates go.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* Where the plates go.

[PROSPECT]: She moved them. Twice. I'm serious.

[SETTER]: Do you two talk about it?

[PROSPECT]: About the plates? *(laughs)* No. We talk about the kids, the weather. She asks if I'm eating okay. I say I'm eating okay. That's most of it.

[SETTER]: Do you wish it was more?

[PROSPECT]: I do. I just don't know how you start. After twenty years you stop starting.

[SETTER]: And your body, how's that holding up?

[PROSPECT]: My back. My hands, I've got the thing, the numbness. And the sitting. My doctor says move more, and I'm like, I'm in a truck eleven hours a day, what do you want me to do? Sorry, there's a truck horn, I'm at a rest stop. Hang on.

[SETTER]: No worries.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Yeah. That's pretty much it.

*≈ 3:15*

[SETTER]: How did you end up looking at ecom?

[PROSPECT]: My nephew. He does it. Sells hats or something. He's twenty-six and he's got more going on than I do.

[SETTER]: Have you tried anything?

[PROSPECT]: I've watched a lot of videos at night in the truck. I bought a book. I haven't built anything. I don't have a computer I like, I do it all on my phone. And I'm tired by the time I'm parked.

[SETTER]: How long's that been?

[PROSPECT]: A couple of years, off and on.

[SETTER]: What's made now the time, then?

[PROSPECT]: I turned fifty-two in August. And I did a calculation. If I keep going like this, I can't retire until seventy. And I don't know if my back will let me.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That's a real number to look at.

*≈ 4:30*

[SETTER]: Alright, bit about money now. You alright with that?

[PROSPECT]: Sure.

[SETTER]: What would you want ecom to be bringing in each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Three, four thousand. That would cut my driving in half.

[SETTER]: And what are you clearing at the moment?

[PROSPECT]: About fifty-five hundred a month. Between us, with her job. So it's fine day to day. It's just there's no future in it.

[SETTER]: And how much do you put away from that?

[PROSPECT]: Eight hundred. A thousand if the truck doesn't need anything.

[SETTER]: And in total, what have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: That's personal, isn't it?

[SETTER]: Yeah man, but I'm sure you know, with a store it takes money to make money, right? So how much have you got?

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Eleven thousand. In a savings account. I was keeping it for the next truck.

[SETTER]: Is that free to use, or is it for the truck?

[PROSPECT]: It's for the truck. But I've got a couple of years before I need it. And if this worked, I wouldn't need the truck.

[SETTER]: Okay. I'd want you to think about that one, man. I'd never want you putting in money you need. That's what the next call is for.

[PROSPECT]: Fair. I'd rather think about it than rush it.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** was the retirement, not "money." A man of fifty-two said "a joke" and moved on.
- **Early impact never got its own line.** "What's the driving like?" did it, using his words.
- **No recap.** The plates line was the end of current, and it didn't need a summary.
- **Timeline and why now** came together: "how did you end up looking at it" and "what's made now the time."
- **The money part was the ladder,** with the usual pushback, and a check on the truck money.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"My retirement's a joke."** The first thing he said, and a big one. He didn't say "money." He said what it's for.
- **"Half my life."** The weight of twenty-six years in three words.
- **"I'm kind of a guest."** His word. It's the cost, and it's a specific one.
- **"I want to come home and not feel like I need to ask where the plates go."** The most concrete version of the problem.
- **"I don't know why I'm telling you this. I'm not much of a talker."** He's surprised he's opening up. Say thanks and don't make it a thing.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"What's the driving like these days?"** came from his own mention of twenty-six years.
- **"Does it bug you?"** is a plain question that suits a man who's not a talker.
- **I laughed about the plates.** He was joking. The laugh let him go further.
- **"And your body, how's that holding up?"** is a second cost, and it explains the urgency.
- **I told him to think about the truck money.** He said he'd rather think than rush. That's the right instinct.
