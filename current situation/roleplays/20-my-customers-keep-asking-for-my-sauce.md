# 20 · "My customers keep asking for my sauce. I should be selling it."

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 6 minutes · **Ends:** not booked, because the money is the restaurant's

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so tell me man, what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Okay, so I own a restaurant. Chicago, Mexican place, twelve years now. And I make this hot sauce, my grandmother's recipe, and people keep asking for it. Every table. They want to take a bottle home. And I thought, I should be selling this online. People in other states would buy it.

[SETTER]: Twelve years. That's a long time.

[PROSPECT]: It's my whole life, honestly. I opened it when I was thirty-five. My wife and I took a second mortgage. I've got fourteen employees, some of them since the start. It's family.

[SETTER]: How's the restaurant doing?

[PROSPECT]: It's okay. It's. Well. It's okay. Since the pandemic it's never been the same. The costs are all up. Chicken, rent, everything. And people go out less. I'm making about what I made ten years ago, but I'm working twice as hard.

[SETTER]: Mm.

[PROSPECT]: And I've never missed payroll. Not once, in twelve years. I'm proud of that. Even in the bad months I've taken less myself. My wife does the books and she says, "you're paying your staff more than you pay yourself."

*≈ 1:45*

[SETTER]: Does she mind?

[PROSPECT]: No, she gets it. But she's tired too. I can see it. We used to go away twice a year. We haven't been anywhere in three years.

[SETTER]: Three years.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. And I'm okay with it. I mean, I'm not okay. But it's the job. When you own a restaurant, you don't leave. Sorry, one sec. *(muffled)* No, the walk-in's the left one! Sorry. Yes.

[SETTER]: Who covers if you do leave?

[PROSPECT]: Nobody. My manager's good, but it's mine. If it goes wrong, it's me. And it's all of them. The cooks, the girls on the floor. I think about them constantly. I'm up at night doing payroll math.

[SETTER]: That's a lot to hold.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. It is. I don't say it out loud, because who's going to carry it if I don't?

[SETTER]: Is there anyone who can?

[PROSPECT]: My wife. But then she's carrying it too and I hate that. My daughter's at college, she calls on Sundays, and I tell her everything's great. I've told her everything's great for two years.

[SETTER]: Two years.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. *(laughs)* Everything's great.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: How long have you been thinking about the sauce?

[PROSPECT]: About two years. People started asking in 2022. I made up some labels, sold maybe a hundred bottles at a market, and I thought, there's something here. But I've no idea about online.

[SETTER]: What's in the way?

[PROSPECT]: Time. And I don't know what I'm doing. Bottling, shipping, ads. It's all new.

[SETTER]: What made you want to look at it now?

[PROSPECT]: The restaurant had its worst quarter ever this spring. I covered it from savings. And I thought, I can't do that twice. I need something that isn't the restaurant.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That's a big reason.

*≈ 4:15*

[SETTER]: Okay. Let me ask you about money, if that's alright.

[PROSPECT]: Sure.

[SETTER]: What would you want the sauce to be making each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Five thousand a month would be life-changing. That's a part-time salary for someone.

[SETTER]: And what's it making now?

[PROSPECT]: Basically nothing. Maybe two hundred a month from the market. The restaurant pays me about six thousand.

[SETTER]: How much do you manage to save?

[PROSPECT]: Hardly anything. Five hundred if the month is good.

[SETTER]: And in total, what have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why do you need to know?

[SETTER]: Fair. With something like this there's usually money going in at the start, so I'm checking there's room for that.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. About fifteen thousand. That's the cushion. For payroll.

[SETTER]: Is that the restaurant's cushion, or is it yours to use?

[PROSPECT]: It's both. It's the cushion. But if I put some in the sauce and it worked, then it'd be fine.

[SETTER]: Can I be straight with you?

[PROSPECT]: Go on.

[SETTER]: That's the money you told me keeps your staff paid. If it didn't work, would that put payroll at risk?

[PROSPECT]: *(pause)* Yeah. It would.

[SETTER]: Then I'm not going to book you in with that money. I'd rather you keep it where it is. The idea's good. The timing's not. If you get something together that isn't the payroll cushion, get back to me and we'll pick it up.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. You're right. Thank you. Most people wouldn't say that.

[SETTER]: I'd rather be straight. Look after yourself, man. Your staff are lucky.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** returned an idea and a business. The idea was the best part of the call, because people were asking for it.
- **The real subject was the restaurant,** and the pressure came out in "who covers if you leave?"
- **No recap.** "Who's going to carry it if I don't?" finished the section.
- **Timeline and why now** came as short questions, and the worst quarter of his career was the answer.
- **The money part** found the problem: the money is the payroll cushion.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Every table."** He has proof people want the product.
- **"I've never missed payroll. Not once."** The pride, and also the fear.
- **"You're paying your staff more than you pay yourself."** What his wife says. A love language and a complaint.
- **"We haven't been anywhere in three years."** A small, concrete cost.
- **"Who's going to carry it if I don't?"** The deepest thing he said, in a throwaway line.
- **"The cushion. For payroll."** The point. The money isn't free to use, and his "it's both" says he knows.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"How's the restaurant doing?"** was the honest next question. The sauce was the idea, but the restaurant was the pressure.
- **"Does she mind?"** was a small question about his wife that opened the three-years line.
- **"Who covers if you do leave?"** let him say the real fear: nobody.
- **I asked directly whether payroll would be at risk.** I didn't book him, I said why, and I said the idea is good.
