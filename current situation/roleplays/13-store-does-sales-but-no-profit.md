# 13 · "I've got a store, it does sales, but I can't tell you if it makes money."

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 5 minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: I've actually already got a store. It's running. I do about six, seven grand a month in sales. Phone cases mostly, some car stuff. I just feel like I should be getting more out of it, I don't know.

[SETTER]: How do you mean, more out of it?

[PROSPECT]: Like I'm putting in the hours and it's not showing up in my account. I'm at it every night after work, and I look at the dashboard and it says six grand, and my bank says, like, nothing.

[SETTER]: So the dashboard says one thing and the bank says another.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I've got a spreadsheet, but honestly I don't totally understand it.

*≈ 1:00*

[SETTER]: What's the job?

[PROSPECT]: I tend bar. In Madrid. Four nights a week, till three, four in the morning. Then I do the store in the afternoon.

[SETTER]: When do you sleep?

[PROSPECT]: *(laughs)* I don't. I mean, I sleep. Seven to eleven, maybe. It's fine, I'm used to it.

[SETTER]: Is it fine?

[PROSPECT]: No. Not really. I've got a girlfriend, she works normal hours, and we basically see each other on Mondays. I'm either asleep when she's up or at work when she's home.

[SETTER]: Mondays.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Mondays are our day. And even then I'm tired. Last Monday I fell asleep during a film and she just put a blanket on me and went to bed. Sorry, my neighbour's drilling. Hang on. Okay.

[SETTER]: How did that feel, with the blanket?

[PROSPECT]: I don't know. Like she's getting used to it. Which is worse.

[SETTER]: Worse than her being annoyed.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. If she was annoyed, she'd care.

[SETTER]: Have you told her that?

[PROSPECT]: No. I make her coffee on Mondays and I try to be there. But I'm half asleep. I know she can tell.

[SETTER]: What does she say about the store?

[PROSPECT]: She says "I'm proud of you." But she says it the way you say it to a kid who's trying hard. You know?

[SETTER]: Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: I don't want to be someone she's proud of for trying.

*≈ 2:45*

[SETTER]: How long's the store been running?

[PROSPECT]: Eleven months. I started in November. About eleven thousand in sales total, I think. Or more, I'd have to check.

[SETTER]: And what are you doing to grow it?

[PROSPECT]: Facebook ads, mostly. About a thousand a month. I don't really know if it's working. Sales are going up. But I'm not sure if I'm making or losing.

[SETTER]: Right. I'll leave that one for the advisor, honestly. They'd be better to dig into the numbers.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Yeah.

[SETTER]: What got you to book the call, though? Eleven months in.

[PROSPECT]: I had a bad month and I didn't know why. Sales were up twenty percent and I had less money. That's when I realised I have no idea what I'm doing with the numbers.

*≈ 3:30*

[SETTER]: Okay, can we talk numbers for a second? Nothing exact.

[PROSPECT]: Sure.

[SETTER]: What are we looking to make from the store every month, profit, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Three thousand euro. That would let me quit the bar.

[SETTER]: Nice. And how far are we from that, in terms of what you're taking home now?

[PROSPECT]: From the store, a few hundred, maybe? I'm not sure. From the bar, about fourteen hundred.

[SETTER]: Got it. How much of that do you manage to save?

[PROSPECT]: Not much. Two hundred. Maybe three hundred. On a good month, honestly.

[SETTER]: Two or three?

[PROSPECT]: Two. Let's say two.

[SETTER]: Okay. And in total, what's in the bank?

[PROSPECT]: Why do you need to know?

[SETTER]: It helps me know whether the advisor call would be worth your time. Ballpark's fine.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. About four thousand. Not counting what's in the store, the stock.

[SETTER]: Is that separate from rent and bills?

[PROSPECT]: Yes. It's separate from my rent money.

[SETTER]: Good. That helps, man.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** told me he has a store, and that it doesn't pay.
- **The job question came naturally** ("what's the job?") after he'd said "after work."
- **No recap.** The blanket line was the end of current, and a summary would have flattened it.
- **Timeline and why now** came together: "how long's the store been running," then "what got you to book."
- **I did not dig into his margins.** I said it's for the advisor.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"It's running. I do six, seven grand a month in sales."** Sales aren't profit. He half knows: "I'd have to check." I said nothing about it. It's for the advisor.
- **"The dashboard says six grand, and my bank says nothing."** His problem in one sentence.
- **"Mondays are our day."** The scene. A whole relationship fits into one day.
- **"She put a blanket on me and went to bed."** The image. It isn't dramatic. It's small and awful.
- **"If she was annoyed, she'd care."** The cost, and he said it without being asked.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"When do you sleep?"** was half a joke. It made it okay for him to say "it isn't fine."
- **"Is it fine?"** was the whole question. He had said "it's fine, I'm used to it" and I repeated half of it flat.
- **"How did that feel, with the blanket?"** came after a specific scene. It was earned.
- **I never told him he was losing money.** I don't know that. He doesn't either.
