# 05 · "Dunno. I just want out of the call centre, that's it really."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 6 minutes · **Ends:** not booked, because the money is her safety net

## The call

[SETTER]: Hey, so what was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Dunno. I just want out of the call centre, that's it really.

[SETTER]: Ha. Okay. Is that the whole story, or is there more to it?

[PROSPECT]: Bit more. It's just a lot to say on a phone call, isn't it.

[SETTER]: Fair. We can start anywhere. What's the call centre like?

[PROSPECT]: It's fine. It's people shouting at you about their broadband. I do split shifts, so seven to eleven and then four to eight, which is a joke because it's the middle of the day I'm just sat there. In the car park, mostly.

[SETTER]: In the car park?

[PROSPECT]: I can't go home, it's forty minutes. So I sit in the car. Sometimes I sleep. Sometimes I just scroll. It's pointless.

[SETTER]: That's a long day.

[PROSPECT]: It is. I've got a daughter, she's eight, she goes to my mum's after school. So my mum does tea and bath and everything, and I get in at nine and she's nearly asleep. Hang on. *(muffled, to someone)* No, it's fine, I'm on a call. Sorry. My mum's neighbour.

*≈ 1:30*

[SETTER]: No worries. How's that sitting with your mum?

[PROSPECT]: She never says anything. But she's seventy-one. She's getting tired, I can tell. I don't like asking her.

[SETTER]: Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: And I'm on a thing now at work. A performance plan. For lateness. Which is because my daughter had a fever twice. And they don't care. It's just a number on a screen.

[SETTER]: A performance plan. That's rough.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I haven't told my mum. I don't want her worrying.

[SETTER]: So you're carrying that one by yourself, too.

[PROSPECT]: I suppose. I'm not the type to go on about it.

[SETTER]: When did they put you on it?

[PROSPECT]: Last month. If I get another warning they can let me go. And then what?

[SETTER]: What does your daughter know about all this?

[PROSPECT]: Nothing. She just knows I'm tired. She drew me a picture last week of me asleep in the car and wrote "mummy's car bed."

[SETTER]: *(a beat)* She's seen the car park thing?

[PROSPECT]: I picked her up from my mum's once on a split day and I was asleep in the car outside. She knocked on the window. She thought it was funny.

[SETTER]: Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: It's not funny. Anyway.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: Is that what made you look at trading, or had you been looking before?

[PROSPECT]: Before, a bit. My cousin does it. He's always on about it. I watched a few things, but I don't understand it properly. And I'm not brave, to be honest. I don't want to lose money.

[SETTER]: Have you tried anything?

[PROSPECT]: No. Just watched.

[SETTER]: How long's that been?

[PROSPECT]: About a year, maybe. On and off.

[SETTER]: Okay. And what are you hoping it'd get you? Not the money, I mean in your week.

[PROSPECT]: I'd go part time. Be home for my daughter's tea. Not rely on my mum so much.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Got it. I'm going to ask about the money for a sec. It just helps me work out if this is a good fit, is that alright?

[PROSPECT]: Go on.

[SETTER]: What would you want trading to be bringing in every month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: An extra thousand? Fifteen hundred, say. I'd be happy with that.

[SETTER]: Okay. And what are you on now, roughly?

[PROSPECT]: About eighteen-fifty a month.

[SETTER]: Right. And how much can you save out of that?

[PROSPECT]: Fifty quid, if that. Some months nothing.

[SETTER]: And in total, what have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why do you need to know that?

[SETTER]: Yeah, but I'm sure you know, with trading it takes money to make money, right? So how much have you got?

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Six thousand. It was my nan's. She left it to me. I've never touched it.

[SETTER]: I'm sorry about your nan.

[PROSPECT]: Thanks. Three years ago now. I never wanted to spend it on rubbish.

[SETTER]: Is that money for anything, or is it free to use?

[PROSPECT]: It's for emergencies, really. If the car goes, or if my daughter. It's what I've got. But if it was going to get me out of there.

[SETTER]: Can I be straight with you?

[PROSPECT]: Go on.

[SETTER]: If it didn't work out, would losing that hurt you?

[PROSPECT]: *(pause)* Yeah. It would. It's all I've got.

[SETTER]: Then I'm not going to book you in with that money. I mean that. It's your safety net, and I'd rather you keep it. If things change, or you build up something that isn't your emergency money, get back to me and we'll pick it up. That okay?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. That's fair. Thank you. Most people would've just said yes.

[SETTER]: I'd rather be honest. Look after yourself, and your mum.

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** was a one-liner and then a wall ("a lot to say on a phone call"). I didn't push. I said we could start anywhere.
- **The best material came from a detail that didn't fit** (the car park).
- **No recap.** Her last answer was "to be honest, I don't want to lose money." Summing it up would have been wrong.
- **Timeline and why now** came together as "is that what made you look, or had you been looking before?"
- **The money part** produced the real issue: one pot of money and it's her safety net.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"It's a lot to say on a phone call."** She's guarded, not hostile.
- **"In the car park, mostly."** The oddest and most real detail. The gap between shifts is where her day goes.
- **"She's seventy-one. She's getting tired."** The cost falls on someone else.
- **"I haven't told my mum."** She's holding the performance plan alone.
- **"It's what I've got. But if it was going to get me out of there."** The most important line in the call. She's willing to put her safety net at risk because she's desperate.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"Is that the whole story?"** She said "that's it really," and her tone said otherwise.
- **"In the car park?"** A detail that didn't fit. I asked about it.
- **"So you're carrying that one by yourself, too."** The "too" connects two things she told me, so she doesn't have to say it.
- **"Would losing that hurt you?"** is the question that matters when someone has one pot. Her answer made the decision for me. I told her why, and I left the door open.
