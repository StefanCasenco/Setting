# 08 · "Money's been a bit of a mess since the divorce."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 5½ minutes · **Ends:** qualified, with flags

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Uh, money mostly. It's been a bit of a mess since the divorce.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That sounds rough.

[PROSPECT]: It is what it is. About two years ago. We sold the house, split things. She got the kids most of the time.

[SETTER]: How many kids?

[PROSPECT]: Two. A boy and a girl. Eleven and eight. I get them every other weekend and Wednesdays. Or I'm supposed to. Some Wednesdays I can't because of work.

[SETTER]: What's the work?

[PROSPECT]: HVAC installer. Columbus, Ohio. I'm on call a lot. It's good money, but a call comes in and you go.

[SETTER]: Mm. And the weekends you do have them?

[PROSPECT]: I rent a one-bedroom. So the kids sleep on an air mattress in the living room. It's not great. They don't complain. But my daughter, last time she goes, "Dad, why don't we just go to Mom's?" And I said, "You can if you want." And she said, "No, it's okay." But you could tell.

*≈ 1:30*

[SETTER]: That must be hard to hear.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I feel like I'm doing it all wrong, you know? I'm paying child support and the apartment and there's nothing left to take them anywhere. We go to the park. We go to the same pizza place. They're good about it. But I want them to want to come.

[SETTER]: Do they want to?

[PROSPECT]: My boy does. The little one, I don't know. She's a bit more like her mom. I just wish I could do more. Trips, or a place with a bedroom each. That's all it really is.

[SETTER]: That's not nothing.

[PROSPECT]: No. I know.

[SETTER]: What do you do with the kids when they're over?

[PROSPECT]: We cook. I'm trying to learn some stuff. Last time we made tacos and my daughter said, "Dad, these are actually good." Which, for me, that's a win.

[SETTER]: Ha. That's a win.

[PROSPECT]: But then Sunday night comes and I drop them off and I come back to the apartment and it's quiet. Real quiet.

[SETTER]: Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: I turn the TV on just so there's noise. Anyway.

*≈ 2:30*

[SETTER]: How long have you been looking at trading?

[PROSPECT]: A while. Year and a half, maybe. A buddy at work got into it. He's doing okay. I didn't want to just throw money at it.

[SETTER]: Have you tried anything?

[PROSPECT]: I did an options thing. Put in like four hundred and lost it. Then it was all the divorce and the move. I put it down.

[SETTER]: And what's different now, that you picked it back up?

[PROSPECT]: I think I'm settled a bit, for the first time. The apartment, the routine. And my daughter's birthday is coming up and I have no idea what to get her. I'm doing it the cheap way again.

*≈ 3:30*

[SETTER]: Okay. Let me ask you some money stuff so I know where you're at. Is that alright?

[PROSPECT]: Sure.

[SETTER]: What would you want trading to be adding each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly, another twenty-five hundred a month would change everything.

[SETTER]: And what are you bringing in at the moment, roughly? Just a ballpark.

[PROSPECT]: I'd rather not say exactly.

[SETTER]: No problem. Is it more like three or more like five, after tax?

[PROSPECT]: Closer to four. But with the child support it's more like twenty-six hundred I actually have.

[SETTER]: Right. And how much can you put away from that?

[PROSPECT]: Not much. Two hundred. Three on a good month.

[SETTER]: Two-ish or three, which is more normal?

[PROSPECT]: Two. Three is a good month.

[SETTER]: Got it. And in total, what have you saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why's that your business?

[SETTER]: Yeah, I get that. I'm just trying to see if it makes sense to book you in, so a ballpark is fine.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Thirty-five hundred. I was going to use it to get a place with a second bedroom.

[SETTER]: Is any of that set aside already, or is it all free?

[PROSPECT]: Kind of for the apartment. But if this worked, I wouldn't need to move for it. I'd have the money.

[SETTER]: Right. I'd want you to think about that one carefully, man. I don't want you putting in money you can't afford to lose.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, I hear you. I'd want to talk it through. That's why I'm here, right?

[SETTER]: Yeah. That's what the next call is for. That's helpful, man.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** brought out the divorce in half a sentence. I gave one human line, then asked how many kids.
- **The air mattress and the park** are the scene. They came from "what's the work?" and then "the weekends you do have them?"
- **No recap.** The pain was clear and quiet. A summary would have felt like a form.
- **Timeline and why now** are two short questions in a row. The why-now answer is a birthday present he can't afford.
- **He wouldn't give an income** ("I'd rather not say exactly"). I offered a range instead and got an answer.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"It is what it is."** A way of closing a subject. I didn't push on the divorce; I asked how many kids.
- **"Some Wednesdays I can't because of work."** A guilty detail dropped in the middle.
- **The air mattress.** The scene of his weekends. It's mundane and sad, which is how real ones sound.
- **"No, it's okay. But you could tell."** His daughter's line. He still hears it.
- **"I want them to want to come."** The feeling. It's what the money is for.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"How many kids?"** instead of "what happened with the divorce?" I don't need the story, and it isn't mine to extract.
- **"What's the work?"** was fine because he'd already said "because of work." He opened that door.
- **"Do they want to?"** gave him a chance to say what he was afraid of.
- **When he wouldn't give a number**, I made it easier ("more like three or more like five?") instead of pressing.
- **The apartment money.** He said it's for a second bedroom. I told him honestly to think it over. The closer will go through it. I flag it.
