# 07 · "I blew two funded accounts this year, so, yeah."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 5½ minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what had you interested in trading in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: So I've been trading about two years. I got funded twice this year, like proper funded, with the payouts and all that. And I blew both. So, yeah. I'm looking for someone to actually look at what I'm doing.

[SETTER]: Two funded, two blown. That's frustrating.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. The thing is I know my strategy works. I backtested it, it's like sixty percent win rate. It's more my execution. I take a trade, it goes against me, I move my stop. Or I add. And then it's gone.

[SETTER]: Mm.

[PROSPECT]: Like the psychology side. And risk management. I've been reading Mark Douglas. It helps but it doesn't fix it.

[SETTER]: What do you do for the evaluations? Do you pay for each one?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, about three hundred each. I've paid for four in total. Two passed, two failed outright. And then the two funded ones I blew.

[SETTER]: So that's around twelve hundred on fees.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Hold on, my wife's calling something from the kitchen. Yeah! One sec! Sorry. Yes. Anyway.

*≈ 1:15*

[SETTER]: What are you trying to get out of it, though? If you had it figured out, what would that give you?

[PROSPECT]: Money, I guess. I'd be able to scale it.

[SETTER]: Scale it to what?

[PROSPECT]: Whatever the account size is. Two hundred K, then another.

[SETTER]: Okay. What's it about for you, though? Not the account. You.

[PROSPECT]: *(pause)* I'm a welder. Thirty-six, in Brno. I've been welding since I was eighteen. The pay is okay. But my lungs aren't great. The fumes. I've got a cough that doesn't really go away.

[SETTER]: Seriously? Have you seen a doctor?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. They said it's not serious yet. But "yet." You know?

[SETTER]: Yeah. That's heavy.

[PROSPECT]: My wife worries. She doesn't say it, but I hear her when I cough at night. She gets quiet.

[SETTER]: What does she say when you talk about trading?

[PROSPECT]: She's supportive. But she saw both accounts blow. She saw me at the table at two in the morning. Before, she said "good luck." Now she says "just be careful." Which is fair. But it means she doesn't believe it anymore.

[SETTER]: And that matters to you.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I want to prove it, I think. To her and to me. That I'm not wasting this.

[SETTER]: Wasting what?

[PROSPECT]: The years. I'm thirty-six. I started welding at eighteen because my uncle said it's a trade, you'll never be out of work. And that's true. I'm never out of work. I'm also never going to retire from it. My uncle has no lungs at sixty.

[SETTER]: Is that what you're afraid of?

[PROSPECT]: Yes. That I'm not a trader, I'm not a welder, I'm just a guy who coughs. Sorry, that sounds dramatic.

[SETTER]: It doesn't.

[PROSPECT]: Okay.

*≈ 3:30*

[SETTER]: So what do you think went wrong with the two funded ones? Roughly.

[PROSPECT]: The first, I was greedy. The second, I knew it was me. I just did it again, even though I knew.

[SETTER]: And what have you tried in between? Besides the books.

[PROSPECT]: One group on Telegram, which was just signals. It didn't help. YouTube. A journal I stopped keeping.

[SETTER]: Okay. So what made you want help now, rather than trying a fifth evaluation?

[PROSPECT]: Because I can't do a third funded alone. The first time I could say it was bad luck. The second time it was me.

*≈ 4:30*

[SETTER]: Okay. Let me ask about the money. Since you said fees, how much do you have put away at the moment, not counting what's gone?

[PROSPECT]: Maybe nine thousand. What's left after the evaluations.

[SETTER]: Is that nine yours to use, or is it spoken for?

[PROSPECT]: It's mine. The family money's separate. My wife and I agreed. I can use this one.

[SETTER]: Good. And what are we looking to make from trading each month, say a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Four thousand euro would let me leave the job.

[SETTER]: Right. And how far are we from that, in terms of what you're on?

[PROSPECT]: About twenty-eight hundred from the job. Trading's minus, if I count the fees.

[SETTER]: And what can you put away from that each month?

[PROSPECT]: Four-fifty. Maybe four hundred. My wife also works, so that helps.

[SETTER]: Okay, got it. That helps, man.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** came with a long, confident strategy speech. A long strategy answer is a way of not answering.
- **I asked about fees**, a practical question that fit the story, and it kept him talking without a feelings word.
- **The money part is out of order** on purpose. He'd just said fees and four evaluations, so "what do you have put away?" was the natural next question. The goal, the income and the savings rate followed.
- **Why now** was a short question near the end ("rather than trying a fifth evaluation"), and he answered it precisely.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"I know my strategy works."** Tactical, detailed, confident. He's hiding in it.
- **"Money, I guess. I'd be able to scale it."** Still tactical.
- **"But my lungs."** The turn. I changed the question from "the account" to "you," and he moved.
- **"Yet."** One word with a lot in it.
- **"She gets quiet."** and **"now she says just be careful."** The cost: his wife's belief in him.
- **"The second time it was me."** His why-now, in six words.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"What are you trying to get out of it, though?"** is the link Atefur taught: tactical problem to the real life under it. He gave me "money" and "scale," so I asked again: "Not the account. You."
- **I never asked about setups or the win rate.** If you follow the method, you stay there.
- **"What does she say when you talk about trading?"** brought the wife in through his own words.
- **I asked if the nine thousand was free to use.** He'd already separated the family money, but I ask.
