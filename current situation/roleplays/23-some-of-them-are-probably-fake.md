# 23 · "I've seen so many people make money from trading. Most are probably faking it, but some are real."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 5 minutes · **Ends:** not booked, because he doesn't have the money

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly? I've seen so many people online make money from trading. Most of them are probably faking it, I'm not stupid. But some of them gotta be real. And I wanna do the same.

[SETTER]: Fair. I can't speak for them. What made you want to give it a shot?

[PROSPECT]: I need to do something. I had a barbershop. I opened it two years ago, and I closed it in March. Lost about twenty grand.

[SETTER]: Ah, man. That's rough.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. It is what it is. I still cut hair on the side, for my regulars, out of my place. But it's not the same.

*≈ 1:00*

[SETTER]: What happened with the shop?

[PROSPECT]: The rent went up. And the guy I had working for me left and took half my clients. I was doing sixteen-hour days, six days. And then I looked at the books and I was losing money. I didn't know. I thought I was fine.

[SETTER]: You thought you were fine.

[PROSPECT]: I'm good at cutting hair. I'm not good at the business side. Nobody told me.

[SETTER]: Sounds like you carried it on your own.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I did. It was my name on the door. Hold on, my dog's going crazy. Bruno! Sit! Sorry. Anyway.

[SETTER]: *(laughs)* All good.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. So that's it. I feel like, I tried something, it didn't work, and now I'm that guy. The one whose shop closed. Every time I see someone from the old neighbourhood, I cross the road.

[SETTER]: You cross the road.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. *(short laugh)* Literally.

*≈ 2:30*

[SETTER]: What's the work situation now?

[PROSPECT]: I do some delivery driving, a few days. And the hair. I make maybe two grand a month. Which is fine for one person. Not fine for what I owe.

[SETTER]: What do you owe?

[PROSPECT]: The loan from the shop. It's about eleven thousand left. I pay four-fifty a month.

[SETTER]: Mm. That's a lot of your month.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. So I look at those trading guys and I think, that's the one thing where you don't need a lease or staff. You just need your brain.

[SETTER]: What made you reach out now?

[PROSPECT]: I got a letter last week from the bank. Just a reminder. It wasn't anything. But I stood there in the hall with it for ten minutes.

*≈ 3:30*

[SETTER]: Okay. Let me ask you about the money. What would you want trading to be making each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Four grand. Enough to clear the loan fast and start again.

[SETTER]: And you said about two grand a month now. How much of that can you put away, after the loan?

[PROSPECT]: Nothing. I'm on zero. Some months I'm negative.

[SETTER]: And in total, what have you got saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why's that your business?

[SETTER]: Yeah man, but I'm sure you know, trading takes money to make money, right? So how much have you got?

[PROSPECT]: Okay. Like nine hundred. And I could borrow from my cousin. He'd lend me three, four grand.

[SETTER]: Hm. I wouldn't, man. I don't want you borrowing for this. Not with a loan still on your back.

[PROSPECT]: But it's the only way I can start.

*≈ 4:30*

[SETTER]: I get that. Here's where I'm at. Right now it wouldn't make sense to book you in. Not because of you, you've been through a lot and you clearly want to fix it. But it would put you in a worse spot if it went wrong, and I'm not willing to do that. I'll send you some resources, and let's pick it up when the loan's smaller or you've got some breathing room. Okay?

[PROSPECT]: *(pause)* Yeah. I think I knew that. Thanks for saying it straight.

[SETTER]: Of course. And man, the shop closing doesn't make you that guy. It makes you someone who tried.

[PROSPECT]: *(short laugh)* I'll try to remember that.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** returned the cynical-but-hopeful line, and I didn't argue with the "some are real." I said "I can't speak for them."
- **The real subject was the barbershop.** I went there as soon as it came up.
- **No recap, no timeline.** The money problem was clear enough that spending time on a timeline would have been pointless.
- **Why now** was a plain "what made you reach out now?", and the answer was a letter from the bank.
- **The call stopped at the money part.** He had under a thousand and a plan to borrow.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Most of them are probably faking it, I'm not stupid."** He's defensive, and a bit embarrassed to be asking.
- **"I closed it in March. Lost about twenty grand."** He mentioned it in the second sentence. It's the actual reason he's here.
- **"I thought I was fine."** The detail that makes it hurt: he didn't know.
- **"Every time I see someone from the old neighbourhood, I cross the road."** The cost, in a single action.
- **"I stood there in the hall with it for ten minutes."** The why-now, with the letter.
- **"I could borrow from my cousin."** The point where I hold the line.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"What made you want to give it a shot?"** went past the cynicism and got the barbershop.
- **"What happened with the shop?"** a plain question, asked as if I wanted to know.
- **"Sounds like you carried it on your own."** A statement, not a question. He said "yeah, I did."
- **I would not book him with borrowed money,** and I said exactly why. He said "I think I knew that." I also told him the shop doesn't define him, because it was true.
