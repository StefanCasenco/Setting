# 11 · Full call, intent to financial qualification (the human version)

**Offer:** trading · **Covers:** intent → current → recap → timeframe → what got in the way → why now → financial qualification

This one is different from 01–10 on purpose:
- The prospect doesn't give you three words and then open up. He rambles, deflects, shrugs, contradicts himself, gets impatient, and gives you six things at once. Your job is to pick the right one.
- Your guess is sometimes wrong.
- The fixed lines (recap, timeframe, why now, the money ladder) are in the call too, said the way a person would say them.

Numbers in brackets, like [4], point to the notes after the call.

## Notes: what I picked up on, and why that question

**[1] "Capped."**
His first answer had five things in it: two years, YouTube, "capped", "not trying to get rich", "obviously". I took the one word that was his: *capped*. Two years is the timeline, and I'm saving it for later. YouTube is tactical. "Not trying to get rich" is a deflection.
The default would be "what do you do work-wise?" I got his work anyway (HVAC), but through his word, and after a small human reaction ("Ha, fair") so it sounds like a person, not a form.

**[2] "It's fine, it's a good job."**
That's him protecting it. I didn't ask how he feels about the job. I named the feeling for him as two options, *bugs you or made peace*, so he could just pick. It's easier to correct a guess than to find the words.

**[3] I guessed wrong on purpose.**
Both options were rejected ("neither, really"). That's useful, not a failure. He corrected me and the real thing came out in the correction: *"I can just see how the next twenty years go."* People are more honest correcting you than answering you. Don't defend the guess.

**[4] Knees.**
Three details in one line (truck, knees, fifty-cent raise). I took the knees because it's physical, concrete and a bit funny. The joke is what let him say "my old man's back went at fifty-two."

**[5] "So that's, yeah."**
A sentence that trails off is a door. I said one soft word: *"Yeah?"* I did not say "tell me about your dad." He gave me a few lines and then closed it himself: "whatever. Anyway." That's his floor. I don't push past it.

**[6] "Does this actually work?"**
That's not a trap. He opened up and now he feels exposed, so he wants to hear the point. I told the truth (I can't tell you that yet), made no promise, and gave a time limit. I did not pitch and I did not get defensive.

**[7] "Your dad's version."**
A guess out loud, but it's built from his own words from a minute earlier. That's why it landed. He confirmed it and went further, to his son.

**[8] "That doesn't sound dramatic."**
One short, human sentence, then I stopped. His next line came out of the silence. Then *"What does she say?"* Quoting someone else is a safer way for him to say the hard thing.

**[9] Recap.**
It uses his words (*ceiling, around for your kids, your dad's version*), not a tactical goal like "consistent profits." I said "Is that right?" and not "correct?", which is just how a person says it.

**[10] I skipped a question.**
He'd already said "two years", so I confirmed it and went straight to what he'd tried. Confirm and skip, never reorder. He corrected himself to "closer to three." I don't chase that. It goes in the handoff as "2–3 years."

**[11] "What happened with the six hundred?"**
He had already started, so "what got in the way of starting?" would be the wrong question. When they've started, it's "what happened with that?" The reaction to the Discord is short and a bit funny, with no judgment and no "why did you buy it?"

**[12] "Who did you have to ask?"**
It got me the thing about Jess ($600 vs $200). That's a flag for the closer (partner). It's not mine to use. The next question, "what do you think would make the difference?", is the neutral version of the timeframe test. It does not ask why he failed. That would be "why haven't you…", which gets self-blame.

**[13] Why now, humanised.**
This is Atefur's question: acknowledge the time, then ask *why now*. What I changed is the delivery. I dropped "life goes on" (it hands him the shrug), dropped the second "why", and made it one question I can say in one breath.

**[14] The shrug.**
His answer was the exact one you predicted: *"always a priority… now more so… lots going on."* No event. Don't ask the same thing again with more force ("but why?"). I agreed with something true in what he said (*that stuff doesn't wait for anybody*) and then asked for a moment. That brought out the birthday and the furnace.
This follow-up is mine, not Atefur's. Check it against your own voice.

**[15] The move into money.**
It's tied to something I said I wanted ("so I know where you're at") and it asks permission. That keeps resistance low. Atefur's point in #4: don't spring it on them. Add context first.

**[16] "With everything going out, how much do you actually get to keep?"**
Same idea as Atefur's "jobs nowadays, taxes, bills." A little context in front of the question makes it feel normal, not nosy.

**[17] The emergency fund.**
He separated it himself. I leave it alone, plainly. Never help anyone risk money they can't spare. The partner flag goes into the note.

**[18] I don't react to the number.**
No "great" and no "oh, that's low." The next step (the pitch) isn't shown here.

## What makes it sound human

**1. React first, then ask.** A small reaction before the question: *"Ha, fair."* / *"Ah, man."* / *"Yeah, that stuff doesn't wait for anybody."* It takes one second and it's most of the difference.

**2. Use his word, not a label.** *Capped*, *knees*, *your dad's version*, *the six hundred*. Not "your situation" or "your challenges."

**3. Don't wind up.** The line you flagged, as you'd probably say it now:
> "Okay man, I know it's been like 2 years, life goes on, but specifically why now? Why is now that time where you feel like, let me actually do something about it, let me make that change?"

What's wrong with it when spoken: it's two *why*s, "life goes on" hands him the shrug he gave you, and it's too long to say without sounding rehearsed. Keep the logic and cut the run-up:
> "I get it, it's been there a while. But why now, man? What's making this the time you actually do something about it?"

**4. If you get the shrug, don't repeat the question harder.** Agree with something true in it, then ask for a moment: *"Yeah, they grow fast. Was there a moment lately where it hit you?"*

**5. Sound a little unfinished.** Contractions, "man" now and then (not every line), a trailing "…", "Yeah?" on its own. Perfect sentences sound like a script.

**6. Vary the shape.** In this call: a joke-question, a two-option, a scene question, a guess, a one-word "Yeah?", a statement followed by silence, and a plain "Okay." If every turn has the same shape, it feels like a form.

**7. Say less than feels natural.** Short turns. Let him fill the space.

**8. Keep the structure fixed.** The order and the key lines don't change. What you make human is the little bits around them: the reaction before, his word inside, the pause after.

## Where it can go wrong
- Re-asking the same why-now more forcefully when you get the shrug.
- Asking "how do you feel about the job?" after "it's fine."
- Digging into the dad after he closed the door.
- Reacting to the savings number.
- Touching the emergency fund, or letting the partner detail turn into pressure.
