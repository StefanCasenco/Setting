# 19 · "I work nights. I basically never see my kids awake."

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 5 minutes · **Ends:** qualified, partner flagged

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly? I work nights. I'm an ICU nurse. Seven p.m. to seven a.m., three, sometimes four nights a week. And I basically never see my kids awake.

[SETTER]: Wow. How old are they?

[PROSPECT]: Eight and eleven. Well, the eleven-year-old is awake. But she's in her room.

[SETTER]: How's the rest of the week?

[PROSPECT]: On my working days I sleep from like nine to three. They get home from school at three-thirty. So I wake up, I make a snack, and then I'm a zombie until I leave. On my off days I'm trying to reset my body, and I'm useless the first day.

[SETTER]: Useless how?

[PROSPECT]: I can't hold a conversation. I'm snappy. My son will ask me something and I'll say "ask your dad."

[SETTER]: Mm. How does your husband handle all that?

[PROSPECT]: He's great. He works days, so he does the mornings and the dinners. Honestly I feel like he's the parent and I'm the aunt who visits.

*≈ 1:45*

[SETTER]: The aunt who visits.

[PROSPECT]: *(laughs)* Yeah. And I love my job. I'm good at it. I just can't do it forever. I'm thirty-six and my body already hates me. Everyone I work with says the same thing.

[SETTER]: What do they say?

[PROSPECT]: That night shift ages you. That the older nurses all have heart problems. I look at the ones who've done it twenty years and I think, no thank you.

[SETTER]: And in the meantime?

[PROSPECT]: In the meantime I go to my daughter's school thing on a day I've slept two hours. And I'm there, but I'm not there. And I see the other mums and they look rested. And I feel like a ghost. Hang on, my phone's going. It's the hospital. I'll ignore it, I'm off. Sorry.

[SETTER]: Does anyone at work get it?

[PROSPECT]: Everyone. That's the problem. We all just joke about it. "Sleep when I'm dead." And then someone on the unit fell asleep at the wheel driving home last year. She's okay now. But it sits there.

[SETTER]: That's scary.

[PROSPECT]: It is. I drive home after twelve hours and I open the windows. I sing to myself. It's a thing.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: How did you get to ecom?

[PROSPECT]: A coworker has a small candle thing. She does it on her days off. I thought, if she can, I can. I read a bit. I bought a course, but I haven't finished it.

[SETTER]: What's been in the way of finishing it?

[PROSPECT]: I'm too tired. I sit down with it and my eyes close.

[SETTER]: How long's that been?

[PROSPECT]: About six months.

[SETTER]: What made you want help now?

[PROSPECT]: My daughter said something last week. She said, "Mum, you're always sleeping." And I said, "I'm working, baby." And she said, "I know, but you're always sleeping." That's when.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That'd do it.

*≈ 4:15*

[SETTER]: Alright, a few questions about money, just so the advisor has the picture. Is that okay?

[PROSPECT]: Sure.

[SETTER]: What would you want ecom to be making each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: About three thousand. That's roughly what I'd lose if I dropped to part-time days.

[SETTER]: And what are you bringing in now?

[PROSPECT]: I take home about sixty-five hundred. My husband makes about five. So we're okay.

[SETTER]: How much do you put away each month?

[PROSPECT]: About a thousand. Fifteen hundred, if no one needs braces.

[SETTER]: And in total, how much have you saved?

[PROSPECT]: Hmm. Why do you need that?

[SETTER]: Yeah, I get it. It's just so the advisor knows how much room there is. Ballpark's fine.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. In my own account, about three thousand. The joint account has more, but that's his and mine together. We'd talk about that.

[SETTER]: Okay. Does he know you're thinking about this?

[PROSPECT]: He knows I'm looking. He doesn't know how serious I am. I'd need to talk to him first.

[SETTER]: That makes sense. I'll make a note of that for the advisor, so nobody's surprised.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** returned the pain in the first sentence, and I asked about the kids, not the job.
- **There was no recap.** "A ghost" said it better.
- **Timeline and what got in the way** came as three short questions, and the real blocker was tiredness.
- **Why now** was "what made you want help now?" and the answer was her daughter's sentence.
- **The money part used your pushback line,** and a plain question about whether her husband knows.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"I basically never see my kids awake."** She led with the pain.
- **"Ask your dad."** A detail she dropped in without noticing what it says.
- **"The aunt who visits."** Her own image. Funny, and it hurts.
- **"I'm there, but I'm not there."** and **"I feel like a ghost."** The feeling, in her words, twice.
- **"I'd need to talk to him first."** A partner flag. The right response is to note it, not push and not avoid it.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"How old are they?"** moved from her job to her people.
- **"Useless how?"** is a little blunt, but she'd said it herself.
- **"How does your husband handle all that?"** brought him in without making it about money.
- **"And in the meantime?"** brought her back from the future to the school event.
- **I asked whether her husband knows.** Not as a hurdle. It's honest, and it's something the advisor should know.
