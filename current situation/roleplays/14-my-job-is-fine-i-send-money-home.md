# 14 · "My job's fine, honestly. I just want a second income."

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 4½ minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: My job's fine, honestly. I'm in marketing at a bank, it pays well. I just want a second income. Something that's mine, I guess.

[SETTER]: Okay. What's pushing you toward something of your own, if the job's fine?

[PROSPECT]: Um. It's not like a crisis. I just want more room. A bit more breathing room.

[SETTER]: Breathing room.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Financially, mostly.

[SETTER]: What's eating the room?

[PROSPECT]: *(laughs)* That's a good way of putting it. I send money home. To the Philippines, for my parents and my younger brother's school. It's about nine hundred a month. And with rent in Toronto it adds up.

*≈ 1:15*

[SETTER]: How long have you been doing that?

[PROSPECT]: Since my first job. Seven years.

[SETTER]: Do you mind it?

[PROSPECT]: I don't mind it. I want to. It's my family. But it's always there. It's the first thing that comes out of my account and I don't get to decide. I haven't had a proper holiday in four years. I always say next year.

[SETTER]: Does anyone back home know that?

[PROSPECT]: No. I'd never say. My mum would stop me from sending it. Then she'd be upset and I'd be upset. So I just keep it quiet.

[SETTER]: So you're covering for them and for yourself.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. *(pause)* That's kind of true, isn't it.

[SETTER]: It's a lot.

[PROSPECT]: It is.

[SETTER]: Do you ever resent it, even a little?

[PROSPECT]: Sometimes. And then I feel awful for feeling that. My dad cleaned hotel rooms to put me through school. So how can I resent anything? It's just a lot of years of being the one.

[SETTER]: The one.

[PROSPECT]: The one who's doing okay. Everyone looks at me like I've made it. And I'm sitting here doing the math on a two-hundred-dollar dinner.

[PROSPECT]: I don't really think about it. I've kind of got used to it. Sorry, my roommate's cooking and the smoke alarm's going. Hang on. Okay.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: How long have you been looking at ecom?

[PROSPECT]: About a year. I read a lot. I took a course on Shopify. I got as far as picking a niche. Candles, which I know, everyone does candles.

[SETTER]: Ha. Did you launch?

[PROSPECT]: No. I got stuck on the product photos. Then work got busy. Then it was Christmas.

[SETTER]: What's changed since Christmas?

[PROSPECT]: My brother's starting university next year. It's going to cost more. And I thought, if I don't do something this year, I'll just absorb it, like I've absorbed everything else.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Okay, last bit, just some numbers so the advisor has context. That okay?

[PROSPECT]: Sure.

[SETTER]: What would you want to be making from ecom each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: An extra two thousand would be a game changer.

[SETTER]: Nice. And what are you taking home now?

[PROSPECT]: About four-two, in CAD.

[SETTER]: And after everything, what can you put away?

[PROSPECT]: Four hundred, maybe five. If I don't go out.

[SETTER]: Is that your usual month, or a tight one?

[PROSPECT]: Usual. I'm quite disciplined.

[SETTER]: And in total, how much have you saved?

[PROSPECT]: Why do you ask that? Sorry, that's a weird question to ask someone.

[SETTER]: No, I get it. It's just so the advisor doesn't waste your time. With ecom it takes some money to get going, so I'm checking you've got something to work with.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. About nine thousand. Plus the emergency fund, which is separate. That's another six.

[SETTER]: And the nine, is that yours to use?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I'd be okay with that.

[SETTER]: Good. Thanks, that helps.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** was "my job's fine," so I didn't ask about the job. I asked what was pushing her.
- **The "breathing room" phrase** turned into "what's eating the room?" and she laughed. That's the question working.
- **No recap,** because "covering for them and for yourself" had already closed it.
- **Why now** was "what's changed since Christmas?", a softer version, and it got her brother's university.
- **The money part used a different answer to "why do you ask,"** a plain reason instead of the "money to make money" line.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"It's not like a crisis."** She's not in the kind of pain the other callers are. She's tired in a quieter way.
- **"Breathing room."** Her phrase for what's missing.
- **"It's the first thing that comes out of my account and I don't get to decide."** It isn't the money. It's the lack of choice.
- **"I haven't had a proper holiday in four years. I always say next year."** A small cost.
- **"I'd never say. My mum would stop me from sending it."** She's keeping a secret to protect the people she's helping.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **I didn't ask about the job.** She said it's fine. The hint was "breathing room," not a complaint.
- **"Do you mind it?"** is a risky question. It could imply resentment. She said "I want to," which is the true answer, so it opened the real thing.
- **"Does anyone back home know that?"** went to the person who isn't on the call.
- **"So you're covering for them and for yourself."** A statement. She said "kind of true, isn't it," which means it's right.
