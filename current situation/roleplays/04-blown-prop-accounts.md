# 04 · "I've blown like four prop accounts."

**Offer:** trading · **Where it ended:** exit test met

## The call

[SETTER]: What was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly I've been at it two years. I've blown like four prop accounts, so, good question.

[SETTER]: Four. What happened on the last one?

[PROSPECT]: Revenge traded. Lost it in like twenty minutes.

[SETTER]: Where were you when that happened?

[PROSPECT]: Kitchen table. Around midnight.

[SETTER]: What was that like?

[PROSPECT]: Stupid, mostly. I knew the rules and broke them.

[SETTER]: Stupid, how?

[PROSPECT]: Like, I'm an adult. I shouldn't be doing that.

[SETTER]: What happens after you close it?

[PROSPECT]: I just sit there. Then I go to bed and stare at the ceiling.

[SETTER]: What's the hardest part of that?

[PROSPECT]: Telling my wife.

[SETTER]: *(pause)*

[PROSPECT]: I told her this one was gonna be the one. I said that last time too.

[SETTER]: What did she say?

[PROSPECT]: Nothing. She just went quiet and said okay. That's worse.

[SETTER]: What are you trying to get out of trading?

[PROSPECT]: Get off the tools. I'm an electrician. My back's shot and I'm thirty-six.

[SETTER]: Back's shot. How long can you keep going like that?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly? I don't know. That's what scares me.

[SETTER]: Got it, man.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Good question."** with a laugh. He's using humour to cover. Don't laugh along and don't fix it.
- **"Four."** The number is the whole story. He's counting.
- **"I knew the rules and broke them."** This is the tactical complaint. The method is not what I'm digging.
- **"Telling my wife."** The turn. He'd just run through the kitchen table and the ceiling, and this is the first time he named a person.
- **"She went quiet and said okay. That's worse."** He told me what hurts, in his words.

## Why I asked what I asked
- **"What happened on the last one?"** instead of "why do you think you blow them?" A "why" about his failure gets self-blame ("because I'm not good enough"). A "what happened" gets a scene.
- **"Where were you when that happened?"** I wanted the place, not the setup. Place makes it real.
- **"What happens after you close it?"** is the part nobody talks about, which is exactly where the feeling is.
- **The silence after "Telling my wife."** He kept going on his own. Filling it would have cut him off.
- **"What are you trying to get out of trading?"** I'd got the pain; this is the line from Atefur that links the real-life emotion to the trading. It's where "get off the tools" comes from, and it moves us from the method to his life.
- **"How long can you keep going like that?"** is the light cost question. One pass.

## Where it could have gone wrong
- Asking about the strategy, the risk or the psychology. He'd have talked for ten minutes about setups and I'd have learned nothing about him.
- "Why haven't you been able to stop revenge trading?" That's the "why haven't you" question. It would have gone straight to "because I'm weak."

## Exit test
- **Pain, in his words:** "blew four prop accounts… revenge traded… told my wife this was the one… she went quiet."
- **Feeling:** ashamed, scared.
- **Cost:** his back is shot at thirty-six and he doesn't know how long he can keep working on the tools.
