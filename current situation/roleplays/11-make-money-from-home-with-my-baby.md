# 11 · "I want to make money from home. I've got a baby."

**Offer:** ecom · **Real length:** about 5½ minutes · **Ends:** qualified

## The call

[SETTER]: Alright, so what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

[PROSPECT]: Oh, hi. Sorry, one second, she's, okay. Sorry. Um, I want to make money from home. I've got a baby, she's seven months, and I'm meant to go back to work in three weeks.

[SETTER]: No worries. Seven months, congratulations. What do you do?

[PROSPECT]: Thank you. I'm a dental assistant, in Tampa. I love the girls I work with, it's a good office. But it's eight to five, I'm on my feet, and the baby's going to daycare.

[SETTER]: How do you feel about the daycare?

[PROSPECT]: Terrible, honestly. It's eleven hundred dollars a month. And I'd be working just to pay for somebody else to hold her. Which, I'm not saying that's wrong. It's just not what I pictured.

[SETTER]: What did you picture?

[PROSPECT]: I don't know. Being with her more. Being the one who's there when she says her first words, you know? My mom's like, "you'll be fine, she won't even remember." And I'm like, but I will.

*≈ 1:30*

[SETTER]: Yeah. What's your husband's take?

[PROSPECT]: He's supportive. He's in construction, he can't change his hours. So it's me or daycare. He says do what you need to do. But we did the math, and if I don't go back we're short by like fifteen hundred a month.

[SETTER]: So that's the number to replace.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. Fifteen to two thousand. If I could replace that from home, I'd stay.

[SETTER]: Have you thought about what that'd look like?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly, I've been up at night with her, scrolling. These people post their shops. I don't know if it's real. But I've got an hour, a couple of hours when she naps. Maybe.

[SETTER]: So you're looking at it during the night feeds.

[PROSPECT]: *(laughs)* Yeah. It sounds crazy. It's three in the morning and I'm scrolling and I'm like, "what if."

[SETTER]: Doesn't sound crazy.

[PROSPECT]: Thank you. My sister thinks I'm nuts. She says, "just go back, you'll be fine." She doesn't have kids.

[SETTER]: What do you say to her?

[PROSPECT]: I say, "I know." And then I cry in the car. *(laughs)* Sorry. Too much.

[SETTER]: Not too much.

[PROSPECT]: It's just, I didn't expect to feel this way. I always thought I'd go back and be fine. And then I had her.

*≈ 3:00*

[SETTER]: How long have you been scrolling?

[PROSPECT]: Since about month three. So four months, off and on.

[SETTER]: Anything you've tried?

[PROSPECT]: Nothing really. Some videos. I downloaded a free guide, that's how I got here, I think. I made a Shopify account and then I didn't know what to put in it, so it's just sitting there.

[SETTER]: Mm. What's keeping you from getting into it?

[PROSPECT]: Time. And I'm so tired. And I keep thinking, who am I to do this?

[SETTER]: Who are you?

[PROSPECT]: I mean, I'm a dental assistant. I don't know anything about this. I feel like everyone on there's got a business degree.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: And why now? You said three weeks.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. It's a clock. Every day I look at the date and I get a pit in my stomach.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That's a real deadline.

*≈ 4:20*

[SETTER]: Okay, I'll ask about the money side now. Nothing scary.

[PROSPECT]: Sure.

[SETTER]: What would you want to be making from ecom each month, say a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Four thousand, I think. Then I'd be comfortable and I wouldn't go back.

[SETTER]: And what are you making when you're working?

[PROSPECT]: About twenty-six hundred. Right now I'm on leave, so nothing.

[SETTER]: Got it. And how much of your household income can you actually put away?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly nothing, since she was born. Maybe two hundred.

[SETTER]: And in total, what have you got saved up?

[PROSPECT]: Um. That's a weird question. Why do you ask that?

[SETTER]: It's just so I know whether it makes sense to book you in. A rough number's fine.

[PROSPECT]: Okay. About five thousand. Mostly from our tax return and my maternity leave. It's kind of our buffer. Hold on, sorry, she's up. One second. *(muffled)* Okay. I'm back.

[SETTER]: All good. Is that your buffer, or is it free to put toward something like this?

[PROSPECT]: Probably half. Twenty-five hundred I'd feel okay with. The rest we need.

[SETTER]: Good. I'm glad you said that. Keep the buffer where it is. Thanks for being straight with me.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** came with a baby. I let the first minute be chaotic.
- **Early impact** was "what did you picture?", asked after "how do you feel about the daycare?" She had just said "terrible."
- **Timeline and what got in the way** came in a short run, and her real blocker was "who am I to do this?"
- **Why now** was a deadline she'd already stated. I only had to confirm it.
- **The money ladder** was interrupted by the baby. I didn't hurry her.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"I'd be working just to pay for somebody else to hold her."** Her sentence, and she half-apologised for it.
- **"She won't even remember. And I'm like, but I will."** The pain, by quoting her mother.
- **"Three in the morning, I'm scrolling, and I'm like, 'what if.'"** Where she found this. She's tired, hopeful and a bit scared.
- **"Who am I to do this?"** The real blocker. It's about confidence, not time.
- **"Probably half. Twenty-five hundred."** She set her own limit before I did.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"How do you feel about the daycare?"** I normally avoid feeling questions. Here "the baby's going to daycare" was the loaded word in her answer.
- **"What did you picture?"** is a soft question about her expectation, and it gave me her real image: first words.
- **"What's your husband's take?"** brought the other person into the picture, and the number she needs to replace.
- **I didn't challenge "scrolling at 3 a.m."** I said it didn't sound crazy.
- **I checked the buffer.** She answered honestly, and I told her to keep it.
