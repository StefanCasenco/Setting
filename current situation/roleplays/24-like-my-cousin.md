# 24 · "I've seen so many people make money from trading. I wanna do the same, like my cousin."

**Offer:** trading · **Real length:** about 5 minutes · **Ends:** qualified, family flagged

## The call

[SETTER]: Hey, so what was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Yeah, I've seen so many people online make money from trading, and I wanna do the same. Like my cousin. He's doing it. He's got a car, he's got a flat, he goes on holidays.

[SETTER]: Your cousin. How long has he been doing it?

[PROSPECT]: Like two years, I think. He started when he was nineteen. He's twenty-one now.

[SETTER]: And you're how old?

[PROSPECT]: Twenty. I'm at uni in Manchester. Second year. And I work part time at Amazon, nights, a couple of times a week.

*≈ 1:00*

[SETTER]: What's uni like?

[PROSPECT]: It's fine. It's. Hold on. *(muffled)* Mum, I'm on the phone! Sorry. It's fine. It's engineering.

[SETTER]: Do you like it?

[PROSPECT]: I don't hate it. It's what my dad wanted. He's got a takeaway, in Rochdale. Has had it for twenty years. Six days, twelve hours. He wanted me to get a degree so I wouldn't have to do it.

[SETTER]: Mm. And do you want to?

[PROSPECT]: Do the takeaway? No. God, no. But I also don't want to be an engineer. Not really. I'm not sure what I want.

[SETTER]: What does your dad say about it?

[PROSPECT]: He doesn't know I'm thinking about this. If I said I wanted to do trading he'd think I'd gone mad. He'd say, "you've got a degree, finish the degree."

[SETTER]: What would you say?

[PROSPECT]: I wouldn't say anything. That's the thing. I don't say anything. I do my shifts at Amazon, I go to lectures, I come home and my dad's falling asleep on the sofa in his shop clothes. And I think, I can't do that. And I can't tell him I can't do that.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That's a hard one to hold.

*≈ 2:45*

[PROSPECT]: Anyway. My cousin. He's not like, rich. But he's free. He can go to his dad's wedding in Karachi for three weeks and not ask anyone.

[SETTER]: Free from what?

[PROSPECT]: From being told. Where to be, when. My dad's never left the shop for more than a weekend. Ever.

[SETTER]: Have you tried it yourself?

[PROSPECT]: A bit. My cousin showed me. I did a demo for a few months. I'm doing okay on the demo. Then I put in two hundred pounds and I lost most of it in a week.

[SETTER]: What happened?

[PROSPECT]: I panicked. And I didn't have anyone to ask. My cousin said "just keep going," which wasn't helpful.

*≈ 3:30*

[SETTER]: What made you reach out now?

[PROSPECT]: I had a conversation with my dad at Eid. He said, "after your degree, you'll have a good job." And I nodded. And I went in the kitchen and stood there. I don't know why. I just stood there.

[SETTER]: *(a beat)* Yeah.

[PROSPECT]: Anyway.

*≈ 4:00*

[SETTER]: Let me ask you about the money. What are we looking to make from trading each month, a year from now?

[PROSPECT]: Honestly, even a thousand a month would be good. I'd be, I don't know. I'd have a choice.

[SETTER]: And what are you making now?

[PROSPECT]: Amazon pays about six-fifty a month. That's all.

[SETTER]: How much of that do you save?

[PROSPECT]: Maybe two hundred. I live at home so I don't pay rent.

[SETTER]: And in total, how much have you saved?

[PROSPECT]: Um. About four thousand. It's from Eid money and Amazon. I've been saving it for years. Is that okay?

[SETTER]: That's a real number. Is it free to use, or is it for something, like uni?

[PROSPECT]: It's mine. It's not for uni, my dad pays that. I've never touched it.

[SETTER]: Okay. Does your dad know you've got that?

[PROSPECT]: He knows I save. He doesn't know how much.

[SETTER]: Right. I'll note that for the advisor, so nobody's caught off guard. And man, I can't promise you'll get what your cousin's got. Nobody can. What we can do is look at where you're at.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I know. Thanks for saying that.

---

## Where the framework landed
- **Intent** gave me the cousin, so I asked about the cousin, not "what do you do?"
- **The takeaway came out of "do you like it?"** about uni. That question touched the real tension.
- **No formal recap.** "I can't do that, and I can't tell him I can't" was the whole picture.
- **Timeline and why now** were short: "have you tried it yourself?" and "what made you reach out now?"
- **The money part was gentle** because he's twenty and his savings were real: Eid money and Amazon shifts.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Like my cousin."** The person behind "so many people online" is one person he knows.
- **"He's free. He can go to his dad's wedding for three weeks and not ask anyone."** What the cousin has that he wants: not asking.
- **"It's what my dad wanted."** The real pull. The degree is his dad's idea.
- **"I can't do that. And I can't tell him I can't."** The pain, in his words.
- **"I went in the kitchen and stood there."** The why-now. He doesn't know why he stood there. I do.
- **"He knows I save. He doesn't know how much."** The flag.

## Why those questions and not the defaults
- **"Your cousin. How long has he been doing it?"** I wanted facts about the person he named.
- **"Do you like it?"** was the question that opened the dad. It's a plain question, not a technique.
- **"And do you want to?"** said gently, to see if he'd say no to the family business. He did, and then he said he wasn't sure what he wanted either, which is the honest answer.
- **I told him I can't promise what his cousin has.** He half-expected a pitch. Saying it first made him trust the rest.
