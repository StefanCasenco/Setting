# 10 · "Money's been a mess since the divorce."

**Offer:** trading · **Where it ended:** exit test met

## The call

[SETTER]: What was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: Money, mostly. It's been a mess since the divorce.

[SETTER]: Yeah. That sounds rough.

[PROSPECT]: *(pause)* It is.

[SETTER]: *(pause)*

[PROSPECT]: I see my son twice a month. That's what I've got.

[SETTER]: Twice a month.

[PROSPECT]: Weekends. And I can't take him anywhere. Last time we sat in a McDonald's most of the day.

[SETTER]: What's the hardest part of that?

[PROSPECT]: He doesn't say anything. He's nine. He just looks around at everybody else.

[SETTER]: Okay.

[PROSPECT]: Why are you asking about my son? I thought this was about trading.

[SETTER]: Fair. I'm not asking just for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, like a personal trainer would. We can leave it if you'd rather.

[PROSPECT]: No, it's fine.

[SETTER]: How long ago was the divorce?

[PROSPECT]: A year and a half.

[SETTER]: Got it, man. Thanks for telling me.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"Since the divorce"** dropped in the middle of a sentence about money. That's the loaded line. It's the real subject, said as if it's a side note.
- **"It is."** followed by a pause. He wasn't ready to say more. He needed the space.
- **"That's what I've got."** about seeing his son twice a month. It's matter-of-fact, so it's heavy.
- **"I can't take him anywhere."** Money is the cause. This is the money pain, and it's about his son.
- **"Why are you asking about my son?"** came after I'd said "Okay." He got uncomfortable right after being honest. That's common.

## Why I asked what I asked
- **One human sentence** ("that sounds rough"), then **silence.** Not a question. If I'd asked "what happened?" I'd have treated it like a detail to extract.
- **"Twice a month."** I repeated his phrase and said nothing. He gave me the next piece himself.
- **"What's the hardest part of that?"** is the light feeling question.
- **The reason line, plus an exit.** "We can leave it if you'd rather" is what makes it safe to continue. He chose to stay.
- **"How long ago was the divorce?"** is a plain fact, asked after the emotional part. It's a gentle landing and gives me the timeline for the closer.
- **I didn't ask about the divorce itself.** I don't need the reason, who left or what happened. It isn't my business and it's not what the call is for.

## Where it could have gone wrong
- Pouncing: "what happened with the divorce?" It treats the loss like a hook.
- Using it later as pressure ("you said you want to see your son…"). Never. It's a person's life, not a lever.
- Moving on too fast with "okay, and what do you do for work?" That would have said I wasn't listening.
- Pushing on when he said "why are you asking?" without an answer or an out.

## Exit test
- **Pain, in his words:** "money's been a mess since the divorce… I can't take him anywhere."
- **Feeling:** his nine-year-old looks around at everyone else, and he can't give him more.
- **Cost:** a year and a half of two weekends a month in a McDonald's.

If the call had turned heavier than that (crisis, grief), I'd have stopped the framework. The booking can wait.
