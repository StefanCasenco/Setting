# 07 · "My job is fine, I just want more."

**Offer:** trading · **Where it ended:** exit test met

## The call

[SETTER]: What was the main intent behind you wanting to get into trading?

[PROSPECT]: My job's fine honestly. I just want more.

[SETTER]: More of what?

[PROSPECT]: More money. More options, I guess.

[SETTER]: Sure, most people want more money. What's the real reason you started looking at this?

[PROSPECT]: I'm thirty-eight. If I don't build something now, I never will.

[SETTER]: Build something. What do you mean?

[PROSPECT]: Something that's mine. I work hard, but it's all for somebody else.

[SETTER]: What makes you feel like you don't have something of your own right now?

[PROSPECT]: My name's on nothing. If I stop going in, the money stops.

[SETTER]: The money stops.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. I'm a nurse. I love the patients. But I've got no ceiling, no way of moving faster.

[SETTER]: What's the hardest part of that?

[PROSPECT]: Looking at forty and knowing I'll still be doing the same thing at sixty.

[SETTER]: Same thing at sixty.

[PROSPECT]: Yeah. It's not a bad life. It just feels small.

[SETTER]: Small.

[PROSPECT]: Like I'm not using all of me.

[SETTER]: Got it, man.

## What I should've picked up on
- **"My job's fine."** I didn't dig into it. That's a statement, not a complaint. A person who says it first is protecting it.
- **"More options."** vague, but it's the second thing she said, and "options" implies "I don't have them."
- **"I'm thirty-eight. If I don't build something now…"** Her answer to "what's the real reason" carries a deadline. The age is the urgency.
- **"My name's on nothing."** The most precise sentence in the call. It's the pain, in her words.
- **"It just feels small."** A feeling word she chose herself.

## Why I asked what I asked
- **"More of what?"** She gave me a vague thing; I wanted to know what "more" meant before assuming money.
- **"What's the real reason you started looking at this?"** is Atefur's motivation question, and it's what you use when someone says their job is fine. I didn't ask a single question about the job.
- **"Build something. What do you mean?"** instead of "what do you do?" I echoed the verb she used. The job comes later, and she said "nurse" on her own.
- **"What makes you feel like you don't have something of your own right now?"** is the softened early-impact line with her words in it.
- **I echoed "same thing at sixty" and "small"** and let her fill in. Both times she said more than a question would have got.

## Where it could have gone wrong
- "How do you feel about the job?" She'd have said "it's fine" again and I'd have no thread.
- "You must like it then." That hands her a hook and ends the digging.
- Going to what she'd do with the money. That's desire, and she hadn't told me the pain yet.

## Exit test
- **Pain, in her words:** "my name's on nothing… if I stop going in, the money stops."
- **Feeling:** it feels small; she isn't using all of herself.
- **Cost:** turning forty and expecting the same at sixty.
