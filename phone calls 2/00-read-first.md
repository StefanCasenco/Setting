## 1. The stages (every call has them, marked like *— OPEN —*)

| Stage | Job | The line you'll hear most |
|---|---|---|
| **OPEN** | Get them talking about themselves | "Hey Marcus, how you doing, man? It's Stefan from Ray's team. You filled out an application about trading. What got you interested in that?" |
| **SOFT QUAL** | Who are they? Working? New, or already doing it? | "Where you based, and what are you doing for work right now?" |
| **CURRENT** | Situation → impact, in *their* words | "How's that actually been affecting you?" |
| **GOAL** | Number → what it means → why now | "What would that actually mean for you?" |
| **ROADBLOCK** | Knowledge, guidance or accountability, said by them | "What do you think it'd take for you to actually get there?" |
| **URGENCY** | 1–10, and why that number | "What makes it a 7?" / "What happens if nothing changes in six months?" |
| **MONEY** | Can they actually invest? (section 4) | "With how expensive everything is, how much are you able to put aside each month?" |
| **BOOK** | Recap in their words → bridge → value → two times | "Does tomorrow afternoon work, or is Thursday better?" |
| **LOCK** | Email, accept the invite now, check for clashes | "Check your email real quick and hit accept for me." |

**Timeframes run through all of it.** If they only started wanting this recently, ask "what's happened recently?" (there's usually an event). If they've wanted it a while, ask "what have you tried?". If they tried, ask how it went (were they burnt?). If they did nothing, ask why not (limiting beliefs and fears to flag for the closer).

**Tone.** Familiar at the open, curious when digging, empathetic when they open up, assumptive when you move on and book.

## 2. Scott's rules you'll see broken by prospects, and kept by the setter

- **You get no points for knowing the answer.** Don't say "nights must be rough". Ask "how's that been affecting you?"
- **Open questions in discovery.** Binary or option questions only to **challenge**, to **book**, or when they really have no idea what the problem is.
- **Short lead-ins.** A sentence of context, then the question, clean. Over-talking takes the edge off it.
- **Vague prospects:** don't ask the same "why" again. Change the angle. Use a reframe ("trading's repetitive too, so why change?"), pull them down to current income, then a target number, then what it changes. Follow each new word once. Use "If you had to guess?" and a two-second silence. If they say they'll settle: "Is that what you're willing to settle for?" … "How come you're on the call today, then?" Then let them go.
- **Don't fish for a trigger, and don't argue.** You can win the argument and lose the call.
- **One real pain is enough.** Dig straight down, not around.
- **Disqualify to protect the closer's calendar:** fit, financial or interest. A clean no is a good call.

## 3. What Atefur did well when you were the prospect

These are moves from your own roleplays. They're in the calls over and over.

1. **He repeats their word as a question.** "Left behind?" "Not good enough? What do you mean?" "Lonely?" "Right headspace? How do you mean?" "What makes you use the word *cycle*?" Short, curious, no lead-in.
2. **"What are you hoping [their goal] will give you that you don't have right now?"** and **"What makes you feel like you don't have [X] right now?"** That second one is in your script as the early impact question. Atefur used it every time.
3. **The contrast challenge.** "Most guys in your position are getting a few dates, some get none… so why is it still a problem you want to solve?" / "A lot of guys just focus on work and do dating later. Why is it a problem for *you*?" It makes them defend their own reason, and that's much stronger than you explaining it.
4. **Reason lines when they get suspicious of the questions.** "I'm not asking for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, just like a personal trainer would." / "You can be honest, man. No judgment." / "The reason I ask is to understand what's actually driving you."
5. **The time roadblock (#4).** "If it's just a time thing, why not cut your hours and do it yourself, as opposed to getting help from someone like us? … So what were you hoping we could do for you?" They have to say why they need help.
6. **Pinning a vague "why now" (#5).** "When's 'just now'?" → "How long ago was that?" → "What shifted for you when you saw that ad?" → "What was the message?" Then: "The whole year before that, what has doing it on your own cost you?"
7. **Using their own numbers.** "How many quality dates in the last three months? … And the three before that?" Six in a year is a fact they can't argue with. (The full maths and the "do you want to keep lying to yourself?" part are closer-level. As a setter, take the numbers and move on.)
8. **Asking for money with confidence (#4, #5).** "Bro, you know how expenses are nowadays, the way the economy's going. How much are you actually able to save from that every month?" If they push back: "In trading you gotta spend money to make money, right? Talk to me. How much do you actually have saved?" His feedback to you: *"You ask it like you're scared… like I'm a cute girl at the bar and you're asking for my number."* Say it in fewer words, don't stutter, and say it like it's normal, because it is.
9. **Giving a reason before the question.** "With the economy and stuff, it's kind of crazy, so how much are you able to save?" The economy has nothing to do with it, but adding a reason makes people answer.
10. **The frame is the same every time, and it changes with the lead source (#2).** "I'm with X. The goal of the call is X. By the end, if we can help, I'll walk you through the next step." And: someone who gave you their full name and number already trusts you a bit, so you're not a total stranger.
11. **Don't make it feel like a sales call (#5).** "You want to steer away from the fact that they'll have to get their card out. Present the call as a solution to their problem." Never say "it's a proper mentorship, it's not 50 bucks". That gets you price questions.
12. **A setter's #1 job is the problem (#1).** "Your number one goal is establish the problem. You're not there to hold them accountable."
13. **What Atefur said he did badly:** going in circles at the start. *"If we go in circles at the start, the prospect's guard goes up, and nothing after lands."* That's why the calls here get to the first real thing fast.

## 4. The money section: two kinds of offer

Money goes **before the pitch** nearly every time. Ask it like it's normal.

### A. Offers that need capital: trading, ecom, dropshipping, Amazon FBA
The business itself needs money (trading capital, stock, ad spend), so you can say it straight:
> "So what are we looking to make from [X] every month, a year from now?" → "And how far are we from that right now, with your current monthly income?" → "And you know how expenses are nowadays, the way the economy's going. How much are you actually able to put aside from that every month?" → "Is that on a tight month, or is that your usual?" → "And in total, how much have you been able to save up?"

If they push back: **"Yeah man, I'm sure you know in [trading] it takes money to make money, right? So how much would you say you've set aside in terms of capital?"**

### B. Offers where only the program costs money: AI agency, high-ticket sales, tech sales, SMMA, fitness-coach bizopp, fitness, weight loss, credit repair, dating, confidence
There's no capital to talk about, so "it takes money to make money" sounds weird. Be smooth:

**Bizopp-style (AI agency, HTS, tech sales, SMMA, fitness-coach):** go through income, which ties to the goal.
> "A big part of what we do is helping people grow their income. If this worked, what would you want to be making a month?" → "And where are you at right now?" → "With how expensive everything is, how much are you able to put aside each month?" → "And in total, what have you got saved up at the moment?"

**Health, dating, confidence, credit:** go through what they already spend, then savings.
> "What are you spending on [the gym, supplements, the apps, credit monitoring] right now?" → "And what's coming in for you each month, roughly?" → "Are you able to put anything aside?" → "In total, what have you got saved up?"

**The reason line if they ask why (program offers):**
> "Just so [closer] can put a plan together that's realistic for where you're at. There's no point building a plan around money you don't have."
> "The last thing I want is to book you into something that doesn't make sense for you right now."

**To check they'd actually invest, without saying price:**
> "And if [closer] finds the right fit for you, is investing in yourself something you'd be in a position to do right now, or is money genuinely tight?" *(binary on purpose: it's a challenge)*

**Students, unemployed, low income, early:** Scott's transparent line.
> "Just so I don't waste your time, if you're looking for our coaching, it's a mid four-figure investment. Is that something that'd be viable for you right now, or is it completely out of the question?"

**Credit (optional, only with confidence):**
> "Some people prefer to spread things out rather than paying all at once. Have you got credit available, or would you rather keep it to savings?"

**"How much is it?"**
> "Honestly, it depends on what you need, and I don't know that yet. That's literally what [closer]'s call is for. He'll go through all of it."

## 5. Openers by lead source

| Lead | Open |
|---|---|
| **Booked a slot (triage)** | "Hey Marcus, how you doing, man? It's Stefan from Ray's team. I've got you down for 3 o'clock about trading, perfect timing. So what got you booking in?" |
| **New form, minutes ago** | "Hey, it's Stefan from Ray's team. You literally just put your details in about trading. What made you fill it out?" |
| **Application** | "…you put an application in about [X]. What made you apply?" |
| **Resource / guide / PDF** | "…you grabbed our free guide on [X] a few days ago. What made you grab it?" |
| **Webinar attended** | "…you were on [Owner]'s webinar last night. What stood out to you?" |
| **Webinar no-show** | "…you signed up for [Owner]'s webinar Tuesday. Didn't see you on. What happened?" (light, with a laugh) |
| **Watched the video (VSL)** | "…you watched [Owner]'s video about [X] and left your number. What caught your eye?" |
| **Quiz / challenge** | "…you did the [X] quiz / you're on day 3 of the challenge. How's it going?" |
| **Community member upsell** | "…you've been in the [Academy] a few weeks. How are you finding it so far?" |
| **Old lead** | "This is a bit of a random one. You reached out back in [month] about [X] and never got a proper call. Where are you at with it now?" |
| **No-show rebook** | "…you were booked in with [closer] yesterday. No stress, just making sure you're good. What happened?" |
| **They call you back** | "Hey, yeah, it's Stefan from Ray's team. I tried you earlier about the trading application. Glad you called back. What made you fill it out?" |

Use a short frame when it helps, usually after their first answer: "My goal is just to see where you're at and where you want to be. If we can help, I'll walk you through the next step." No "are you free?" and no "sound fair?" chasing yeses.
