# 016 · Ecom · New form · Vague: "My job's boring" (Scott's reframe)

**Prospect:** Emily, 27, claims processor at an insurance company, Austin · **Lead:** form filled 15 minutes ago · **Money:** capital · **Ends:** booked

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Emily, how's it going? It's Stefan from Leo's team. You just put your info in about starting an online store. What made you fill it out?

**[E]:** Hi. Um, my job's boring. *(laughs)* That's basically it.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Fair. Where you based, and what's the job?

**[E]:** Austin. I process insurance claims. Like, all day.

**[S]:** And is ecom totally new for you?

**[E]:** Totally new.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** So when you say boring, what's boring about it?

**[E]:** It's the same thing every day. Open claim, check documents, approve or deny, next claim. Like four hundred a week.

**[S]:** Okay. I'll be honest with you, Emily. Running a store is pretty repetitive too. Same products every day, same emails, packing the same boxes, checking the same ads. A lot of days it's just admin. So knowing that, why do you actually want to change?

**[E]:** *(pause)* …Huh. Okay. That's fair. I guess… it's not the boring. It's that it's not mine.

**[S]:** Not yours. What do you mean?

**[E]:** Like, I process four hundred claims a week and nobody knows my name. If I quit tomorrow they'd have someone in my chair by Monday. My manager calls me "Emma" half the time.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Emma. How long you been there?

**[E]:** Three years. *(laughs)* Still Emma.

**[S]:** And how's that been sitting with you?

**[E]:** Honestly, it makes me feel kind of invisible. Like I'm a machine. And I used to be really creative. I did art in college. I haven't drawn anything in like two years.

**[S]:** Two years since you drew anything.

**[E]:** Yeah. I come home and I'm just… done. I watch Netflix. That's my life. *(laughs)* It sounds so sad when I say it out loud.

**[S]:** What's happened recently that made you look at a store, specifically?

**[E]:** My friend from college started selling her prints on Etsy and then her own site, and she just posted that she quit her job. And I was like… I was better at art than her. *(laughs)* Is that mean?

**[S]:** *(laughs)* It's honest.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If you started a store and it went the way you want, where would it be in a year?

**[E]:** Making like two thousand a month? Selling my own designs. Like prints, stickers, tote bags.

**[S]:** And what would two thousand a month actually mean for you?

**[E]:** That I'm making stuff again. That people would know my name. And I could drop to part-time at work and actually have energy.

**[S]:** Why's that important now?

**[E]:** 'Cause I'm 27, and if I keep waiting, I'll be 30 and still Emma. *(laughs)*

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** What do you think's been stopping you from starting already, like your friend did?

**[E]:** I don't know how. Like, at all. Shopify, printing, shipping, how do people even find you? It feels like a hundred things.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take for you to actually get going?

**[E]:** Someone to tell me what order to do things in. Like, step one, step two. I'd do the work. I just don't know where to start.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is this to you?

**[E]:** Seven.

**[S]:** What makes it a seven?

**[E]:** Because I miss making things. And I don't want to be invisible forever.

**[S]:** What's keeping it from a nine or ten?

**[E]:** That nobody would buy it. That I'd spend money and it's just a hobby.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Fair. Couple money ones. What's the insurance job paying you, after taxes?

**[E]:** Like thirty-four hundred.

**[S]:** And you know how Austin rent is now. How much are you able to put aside each month?

**[E]:** Like four hundred. I have a roommate.

**[S]:** Usual?

**[E]:** Usual.

**[S]:** And in total, how much you got saved?

**[E]:** Like seven thousand.

**[S]:** And you know in ecom it takes some money to make money, like samples, a site, ads. Have you thought about what you'd put in to start?

**[E]:** Maybe two thousand? I don't know what's normal.

**[S]:** That's what Nina's call is for.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, it's not really the boring. It's that you process four hundred claims a week, nobody knows your name, and your manager calls you Emma. You haven't drawn anything in two years, and your friend just quit her job selling prints. You want two thousand a month from your own designs so you're making things again and you're not invisible. What you said you need is someone to tell you what order to do things in. Next step would be a Zoom with Nina, one of Leo's head coaches. She'll map out step one, step two, and whether your designs could actually sell. After work, or on a weekend?

**[E]:** After work. I'm off at 4:30.

**[S]:** Nina's got Wednesday at 5:30 or Thursday at 6.

**[E]:** Wednesday.

**[S]:** Wednesday at 5:30. Best email?

**[E]:** *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Accept it for me. And if you've got any old college art, have a couple pieces ready to show her.

**[E]:** Oh my god, okay. Yes. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** See you Wednesday, Emily. Not Emma. *(laughs)*

## What to notice

- **This is Scott's reframe, ported to ecom.** "Running a store is pretty repetitive too… so knowing that, why do you actually want to change?" One line, and "boring" stopped working. She found the real reason: "It's not mine."
- **Follow the new word once.** "Not yours. What do you mean?" got her manager calling her Emma, and that became the pain.
- **"What's happened recently?"** The timeframe branch for a recent want. It got her friend quitting, and the honest jealousy.
- **The roadblock is guidance, in her words:** "Someone to tell me what order to do things in."
- **Ending on her name is a callback.** Small, but it shows you listened to the whole call.
