# 056 · High-ticket sales · No-showed twice · Name the pattern, gently

**Prospect:** Jake P., 23, works at a Foot Locker, Columbus · **Lead:** booked twice with the closer, didn't show either time · **Money:** qualified on the first call · **Ends:** rebooked with three small commitments

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Jake, how's it going, man? It's Stefan from Luke's team. *(laughs)* I think you know why I'm calling.

**[J]:** *(awkward laugh)* Yeah. My bad, man. I know. Twice.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Twice. Honestly, I'm not mad. I'm just curious. Can I ask you something straight, and you be straight with me?

**[J]:** Yeah.

**[S]:** Do you actually want to do this? Because if you don't, that's completely fine. I'll take you off, and you won't hear from me again. No hard feelings.

**[J]:** *(pause)* No, I do. I do want to.

**[S]:** Okay. So what happened, both times?

**[J]:** First time, I genuinely forgot. I was at work, didn't check my phone. Second time… *(pause)* honestly, I got nervous. I thought he was gonna ask me a bunch of stuff I didn't know. And it'd get to money, and I don't have a lot.

**[S]:** Thank you for being honest, man. That's actually really normal. Loads of people get nervous before that call.

**[J]:** Yeah?

**[S]:** Yeah. And the call isn't a test. There are no wrong answers. He's going to talk about where you're at and whether it makes sense for you. And if the money doesn't work, he'll say, and that's fine. Nobody's going to trick you.

**[J]:** Okay. That helps, actually.

**[S]:** Remind me, when we spoke, what was the main thing?

**[J]:** I'm at Foot Locker, I'm 23, and my manager just got promoted to district, and he told me I'm "not management material." And I want to prove him wrong.

**[S]:** "Not management material." Is that still true, that it's bugging you?

**[J]:** Even more now. He keeps saying it in front of the new guys. Like it's a joke.

**[S]:** That's rough, man. So, Jake, I'll rebook you, but I want to do it properly this time. When's a time you'll 100% be home, not at work, phone in your hand?

**[J]:** Sunday afternoon. I'm off Sundays.

**[S]:** Sunday at 2 or 4?

**[J]:** 2.

**[S]:** Sunday at 2. I'm going to send the invite, and I want you to do three things for me. One: accept it right now, while we're on the phone. Two: set an alarm on your phone for 1:45. Three: if you get nervous on Sunday, text me before. Not after. Deal?

**[J]:** *(laughs)* Deal.

**[S]:** Okay, sent. Accept it?

**[J]:** Done. Setting the alarm now… okay. 1:45.

**[S]:** Perfect. And Jake, it's a conversation, not an exam. Just be yourself. "Not management material" doesn't get to decide anything.

**[J]:** *(quiet)* Thanks, man. Sorry again.

**[S]:** Don't be sorry. Just be there. *(laughs)*

## What to notice

- **"I think you know why I'm calling."** Light, with a laugh. It names the elephant without blame.
- **"Do you actually want to do this? If not, that's completely fine."** After two no-shows you need a real answer, and offering the exit makes the yes real.
- **The real objection was fear** (questions, money). "The call isn't a test." Most repeat no-shows are scared, not lazy.
- **Recap from the first call, in his words.** "Not management material" reminds him why he booked.
- **Three small actions, done on the phone:** accept now, an alarm at 1:45, and text before you cancel. Small steps work better than promises.
