# 051 · High-ticket sales · Webinar attendee · Single mom: "I'm not a sales person"

**Prospect:** Leanne, 33, teacher's aide, single mom of two, Knoxville · **Lead:** attended the webinar, left early · **Money:** program, tight · **Ends:** booked, with honesty about money

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Leanne, how are you? It's Stefan from Luke's team. You were on Luke's webinar last night. What stood out to you?

**[L]:** Hi. Um, the part about working from home. But honestly, I left early because I thought, who am I kidding. I'm not a sales person.

**[S]:** That's really honest, thank you. What made you sign up in the first place, before that thought kicked in?

**[L]:** Money, honestly. I'm a teacher's aide. I make like fifteen hundred a month. I've got two boys, eight and five. Their dad helps a little. Not much.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** Fifteen hundred with two boys. How does that work?

**[L]:** *(laughs)* It doesn't, really. I clean houses on Saturdays too. And we go to my mom's for dinner twice a week. Don't tell anyone. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Your secret's safe. And have you ever done any sales?

**[L]:** No. Never.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** How has that been, juggling all of it?

**[L]:** Exhausting. And the boys want stuff. My oldest wanted football camp this summer. It was a hundred and fifty dollars, and I had to say no. He was so upset.

**[S]:** What was it like, saying no to him?

**[L]:** *(quiet)* It's the worst part. Every time. He said, "it's okay, Mommy." He's eight. He shouldn't have to say that.

**[S]:** Yeah. That's hard.

**[L]:** *(pause)* Sorry.

**[S]:** Don't be. How long has money been this tight?

**[L]:** Since the divorce. Two years.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If remote sales worked, what would make a difference?

**[L]:** Honestly, two thousand a month would be life-changing. I could stop cleaning houses. And say yes to football camp.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you?

**[L]:** That he doesn't have to say "it's okay, Mommy."

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** Can I ask you about the "I'm not a sales person" thing?

**[L]:** Sure.

**[S]:** You work with kids all day. How do you get a five-year-old to sit down and do reading when they don't want to?

**[L]:** *(laughs)* You make them think it's their idea. Or you make it a game.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Leanne, that's sales. Honestly. Most of good sales is listening and getting someone to want to do something, without forcing them. I'm not saying it's easy, it's a skill you'd have to learn. But "I'm not a sales person" isn't really true. You just haven't done it for money.

**[L]:** *(pause)* …Huh. Okay. Maybe.

**[S]:** So what do you think it'd take for you to actually do it?

**[L]:** Someone to teach me from scratch. And honestly, someone telling me I'm not crazy for trying.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[L]:** Eight.

**[S]:** What makes it an eight?

**[L]:** Football camp.

**[S]:** And what's keeping it from a ten?

**[L]:** Fear. And money.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Let me ask a couple money questions then. No judgment, I know it's tight. Are you able to put anything aside right now?

**[L]:** Honestly, no. Some months I'm in my overdraft.

**[S]:** Thank you for being honest. Have you got anything saved at all?

**[L]:** About eighteen hundred, from my tax refund. For emergencies.

**[S]:** And credit, anything available?

**[L]:** I have a card with about two thousand on it. From last Christmas.

**[S]:** Okay. Leanne, I'll be straight with you, because I don't want you to panic later. Coaching like this is a real investment, and money's really tight for you right now. The next call is going to be honest about whether this is the right time. If it isn't, the coach will say so, and that's okay. Does that feel alright?

**[L]:** *(pause)* Yeah. Honestly, that makes me feel better.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, you're on fifteen hundred a month with two boys, cleaning houses on Saturdays, and you had to say no to football camp. He said "it's okay, Mommy" at eight. You want two thousand a month so you can drop the cleaning and say yes. You think you're not a sales person, but you get five-year-olds to do reading every day. What you said you need is someone to teach you from scratch. Next step would be a Zoom with Amara, one of Luke's head coaches. She's a mom too. She'll go through it all, including whether it makes sense right now, with no pressure to decide anything. When are the boys asleep?

**[L]:** *(laughs)* 7:30, if I'm lucky.

**[S]:** Tuesday at 8 or Wednesday at 8?

**[L]:** Tuesday.

**[S]:** Best email?

**[L]:** *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. And Leanne, if you feel the panic coming before Tuesday, text me before you cancel. Deal?

**[L]:** *(laughs)* Deal.

## What to notice

- **"I left early because who am I kidding."** Ask what got her to sign up *before* the doubt. That's where the real reason is.
- **"It's okay, Mommy."** Repeat it, then say very little. Empathetic tone.
- **"I'm not a sales person"** was handled with *her own skill* ("how do you get a five-year-old to do reading?"). A story beats an argument. Then don't oversell it: "I'm not saying it's easy."
- **Tight money: be honest before the closer.** "The next call will be honest about whether it's the right time." That's right for her, and it's why she felt safe enough to book.
- **"Text me before you cancel."** Name her pattern with a laugh. It gives her a step before running.
