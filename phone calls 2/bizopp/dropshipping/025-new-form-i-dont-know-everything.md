# 025 · Dropshipping · New form · "I don't know" to everything

**Prospect:** Mia, 25, receptionist at a car dealership, Sacramento · **Lead:** filled out the form 10 minutes ago · **Money:** capital · **Ends:** booked

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Mia, how are you? It's Stefan from Nate's team. You just filled out a form about dropshipping. What made you fill it out?

**[M]:** I don't know. I just saw an ad.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Fair. What about the ad got you?

**[M]:** I don't know. It looked easy?

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** Where you based, and what do you do?

**[M]:** Sacramento. Receptionist at a car dealership.

**[S]:** And have you ever sold anything online?

**[M]:** No.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** How long you been at the dealership?

**[M]:** Like two years.

**[S]:** And what's it like?

**[M]:** I don't know. Fine.

**[S]:** *(pause, two seconds)*

**[M]:** …I mean, the salesmen are kind of gross. *(laughs nervously)*

**[S]:** Gross how?

**[M]:** Like, comments. About my outfits. "Smile more, sweetheart." That kind of thing. And my manager just laughs.

**[S]:** Your manager laughs.

**[M]:** Yeah. I told HR once. Nothing happened. Actually, it got worse after.

**[S]:** That's not okay. How has that been for you, going in every day?

**[M]:** I don't kn— *(stops)* …I hate it. I sit in my car before my shift for like ten minutes. Just sitting there.

**[S]:** Ten minutes, before you can go in.

**[M]:** Yeah. *(quiet)* I've never said that out loud.

**[S]:** Thank you for telling me. How long has it been like that?

**[M]:** Since like March. When the new sales guy started.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If dropshipping worked, what would you want it to do?

**[M]:** I don't know. Make money.

**[S]:** How much would you need, for it to matter?

**[M]:** I don't know.

**[S]:** If you had to guess?

**[M]:** *(pause)* …Enough to quit. Like… twenty-eight hundred? That's what I make now.

**[S]:** And what would quitting mean for you?

**[M]:** Not sitting in my car. *(small laugh)* Not being called sweetheart. Working from home, where nobody's looking at me.

**[S]:** Why's that important now?

**[M]:** Because I'm starting to dread Sundays. Like, Sunday at 4 I already feel sick about Monday.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** What do you think's been stopping you from finding something else already?

**[M]:** I don't know. I looked at other jobs. Everything pays less. And I don't have a degree.

**[S]:** And with dropshipping, what do you think it'd take for you to actually do it?

**[M]:** I don't know anything about it. So… someone to teach me. From zero.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it to you to get out of there?

**[M]:** Eight.

**[S]:** What makes it an eight?

**[M]:** The car thing. And Sundays.

**[S]:** And what's keeping it from a ten?

**[M]:** That I'm not smart enough for business stuff. I don't know.

**[S]:** What makes you say you're not smart enough?

**[M]:** My dad always said I wasn't a business person. My brother was. *(laughs)* He sells insurance.

**[S]:** And you run the front desk at a dealership with gross salesmen and an HR department that doesn't help. *(laughs)* That's not nothing.

**[M]:** *(laughs)* I guess.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Couple money questions. What's the dealership paying you a month?

**[M]:** Twenty-eight hundred.

**[S]:** And you know how expensive Sacramento is. How much are you able to put aside each month?

**[M]:** I don't know. Like three hundred?

**[S]:** Is that usual?

**[M]:** Usually. I live with my sister.

**[S]:** And in total, saved?

**[M]:** Like five thousand.

**[S]:** And in dropshipping it takes money to make money, for testing and ads. Would any of that five be something you'd put in?

**[M]:** I don't know. Maybe. If I knew what I was doing.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, the salesmen make comments, your manager laughs, and HR made it worse. You sit in your car for ten minutes before every shift, and by Sunday at 4 you already feel sick about Monday. You want to make the twenty-eight hundred from home so nobody's looking at you, and what you said you need is someone to teach you from zero. Next step would be a Zoom with Leah, one of Nate's head coaches. She's great with people who are brand new, and she'll map out what step one even looks like. Is after work better, or a weekend?

**[M]:** Saturday. Not Sunday. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Not Sunday, deal. Saturday at 11 or 2?

**[M]:** 11.

**[S]:** Saturday at 11. Best email?

**[M]:** *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Can you accept it for me now?

**[M]:** Okay. Done.

**[S]:** And Mia, you said more on this call than "I don't know." Just so you know.

**[M]:** *(laughs)* Yeah. I noticed.

## What to notice

- **She said "I don't know" about ten times.** The setter never answered for her. He used silence, "if you had to guess?", and fact questions (how long, what's it like).
- **The two-second silence after "Fine."** That's where the real call started: the salesmen.
- **"I've never said that out loud."** That's what happens when they verbalise it themselves. Scott: the impact only lands when they say it.
- **The limiting belief came from her dad.** "What makes you say you're not smart enough?" got it. A light reframe, then move on. You're not her therapist.
- **This is also a workplace harassment story.** The setter said "that's not okay" once and didn't play lawyer.
