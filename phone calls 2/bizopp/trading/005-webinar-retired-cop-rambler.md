# 005 · Trading · Webinar attendee · Retired cop, 56, tells long stories

**Prospect:** Ron, 56, retired police officer, now part-time security, Tampa · **Lead:** stayed for the whole webinar last night, asked two questions · **Money:** capital · **Ends:** booked with his wife

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Ron, how you doing? It's Stefan from Ray's team. You were on Ray's webinar last night, stayed for the whole thing. What stood out to you?

**[R]:** Well, I'll tell you what, young man. *(laughs)* I've been around a long time and I've seen a lot of these things, and most of them are garbage. My brother-in-law got into one of those crypto deals in '21, lost his shirt. Thirty grand. Some kid in a hoodie. So I came in skeptical. But your guy Ray, he talked about risk first. Risk first. Nobody talks about risk first. I wrote that down.

**[S]:** Risk first. That's the bit that got you?

**[R]:** That and he didn't show me a Lamborghini. *(laughs)*

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* No Lambos, that's the rule. So where you based, and what are you doing these days, work-wise?

**[R]:** Tampa. Retired from Tampa PD after twenty-six years. Now I do security at a hospital three nights a week. Keeps me out of my wife's hair. *(laughs)* She says I was getting under her feet. Which, I'll be honest with you, Stefan, the first six months after I retired I reorganized the garage four times. Four times. She came out one day and said, "Ronald, if you touch that garage again I'm leaving." *(laughs)* So I got the security gig.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Four times. And have you done any trading yourself yet?

**[R]:** I've got a Schwab account. My pension guy set it up. I don't touch it. I watch it, I don't touch it.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** So what's got you looking at trading now, if the Schwab account's already there?

**[R]:** Well, here's the thing. *(pause)* The pension's fine. It's fine. But my daughter, she's a single mom, two boys, she's struggling. Her ex is no help. And I been helping her out. Four, five hundred a month. And Carol, my wife, she's worried we're eating into our retirement. And she's right. We are.

**[S]:** How's that been for you, helping her and watching the retirement go down?

**[R]:** *(long pause)* It's hard. I'm her dad. I'm not gonna let my grandsons go without. But I lay awake. I did twenty-six years on the street, I've been shot at, and this is what keeps me up at night. Money. *(laughs)* Isn't that something.

**[S]:** Twenty-six years on the street, and money's what keeps you up.

**[R]:** That's right.

**[S]:** How long has it been like that?

**[R]:** Since she got divorced. Two years.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If trading worked out for you, what would you want it doing, say a year from now?

**[R]:** If it could cover what I give her. Five hundred a month. That's it. I'm not greedy. Then the pension's the pension and Carol sleeps at night.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you, if it covered that five hundred?

**[R]:** I'd stop feeling like I'm choosing between my daughter and my wife. *(pause)* That's what it feels like sometimes.

**[S]:** Yeah. That's a lot to carry.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** You've had the Schwab account a while. What's been stopping you from using it to do that already?

**[R]:** I don't know what I'm doing, plain and simple. I'm a cop, not a stockbroker. And I'm scared to death of doing what my brother-in-law did. I'd never forgive myself.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take for you to feel safe doing it?

**[R]:** Somebody who'll teach me the boring way. The risk-first way. Like Ray said. And somebody I can call and say, "Is this stupid?" before I do it.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it to you to get that five hundred covered?

**[R]:** Eight. Maybe a nine.

**[S]:** What makes it that high?

**[R]:** 'Cause the boys are only gonna be little once. And I want Carol to stop worrying.

**[S]:** And what's holding it back from a ten?

**[R]:** Carol. *(laughs)* She thinks this is all a scam. She saw what happened to her brother.

**[S]:** That's fair, she's seen it up close.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Ron, couple quick questions on the money side. I'll say this up front: the last thing I'd want is you touching your pension for this. Okay?

**[R]:** Good. I appreciate you saying that.

**[S]:** So what's coming in each month, between the pension and the security job?

**[R]:** Pension's about forty-one hundred. Security's another eleven hundred.

**[S]:** And with helping your daughter, how much are you two able to put aside each month?

**[R]:** Not a lot now. Maybe three hundred.

**[S]:** And outside the pension and retirement accounts, savings?

**[R]:** We've got about twenty-five thousand in a savings account. Rainy day.

**[S]:** And I'm sure you know, in trading it takes money to make money. So if you did start, have you thought about what you'd want to put in?

**[R]:** I'd never put in more than I can lose. Maybe five thousand. Maybe.

**[S]:** That's exactly the right way to think about it.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So, from what you said, you're helping your daughter and the boys with five hundred a month, and it's coming out of retirement. Carol's worried, and you're lying awake. You've got the Schwab account but you don't touch it, because you're scared of doing what your brother-in-law did. You want something that covers that five hundred so you stop feeling like you're choosing between them. What you said you need is somebody to teach you the boring, risk-first way, somebody you can call before you do something stupid. Next step would be a Zoom with Steve, one of Ray's head coaches. He's in his fifties too, and he'll talk risk first. And I'd really like Carol on the call. She's seen it go wrong, so she should get to ask every question. Would tomorrow evening work for both of you, or is Saturday morning better?

**[R]:** Saturday morning. She'll have her questions written down, believe me.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Good. Steve's got 10 or 11:30.

**[R]:** 10. I'm off Friday night, I'll be up.

**[S]:** Saturday at 10. Best email?

**[R]:** It's ron dot… *(spells it slowly)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Do you check email on your phone, or should I text it to you too?

**[R]:** Text it too. Carol does the emails.

**[S]:** Done. And forward it to Carol so it's in her calendar. Anything Saturday at 10? Grandkids?

**[R]:** They come Sunday. We're good.

**[S]:** Perfect. Thanks, Ron. And stay out of the garage. *(laughs)*

**[R]:** *(laughs)* Don't you start.

## What to notice

- **Let older ramblers ramble, then pick one thread.** Out of the brother-in-law, the garage and the hoodie kid, the setter asked one thing: "What's got you looking at trading now?" That got the daughter.
- **"Twenty-six years on the street, and money's what keeps you up."** Repeat their strongest line back and stop. That's empathetic tone, with no extra words.
- **The pension is protected before the money questions,** so he answered everything. You don't do this to sound nice. It's right, and it builds trust.
- **The sceptical spouse goes on the call.** "She's seen it up close" respects Carol. It doesn't try to get around her.
- **Practical booking for older prospects:** text plus email, forwarded to the spouse.
