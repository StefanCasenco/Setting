# 032 · Amazon FBA · Booked a slot (triage) · Engineer: "What's your success rate?"

**Prospect:** Raj, 41, mechanical engineer at an auto parts supplier, Columbus, Indiana · **Lead:** booked a lunchtime slot · **Money:** capital · **Ends:** booked

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Raj, how are you? It's Stefan from Adam's team. I've got you down for 12:15 about Amazon FBA. What got you booking in?

**[R]:** Hello. Yes. Before we go further, can you tell me what percentage of your students become profitable?

**[S]:** Good question, and honestly, it's the one I'd ask too. I don't have the official numbers in front of me, and I'm not going to guess, because you'd catch me. *(laughs)* Daniel, the coach on the next call, can show you real data. My job is to see if that call even makes sense for you. Fair?

**[R]:** *(pause)* That is reasonable.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** So what's got an engineer looking at Amazon?

**[R]:** I have done the analysis. Private label has better margins than dropshipping, and the brand is an asset. It can be sold for three to four times annual profit.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* You've done the homework. And besides the analysis, why do you want an asset like that, for you?

**[R]:** Diversification. My income depends on one employer.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** And what's the problem with that, for you specifically?

**[R]:** *(pause)* It is a risk.

**[S]:** A risk how? Has something happened?

**[R]:** …We were acquired in April by a German company. They have already let go of twenty-two engineers. I am still there. For now.

**[S]:** Twenty-two. And you're waiting to see if you're twenty-three.

**[R]:** That is accurate.

**[S]:** How long have you been there?

**[R]:** Fourteen years.

**[S]:** Fourteen years, and it could be one email. How has that been, since April?

**[R]:** *(long pause)* I do not sleep well. I check my email at 5:30 in the morning to see if there is a meeting invitation from HR. My wife has noticed.

**[S]:** What has she noticed?

**[R]:** That I am distracted. She says I am doing calculations at dinner.

**[S]:** Calculations at dinner. What calculations?

**[R]:** *(slight laugh)* How many months we would last. Mortgage, two children, my parents live with us.

**[S]:** And what's the answer?

**[R]:** Seven months. Perhaps eight.

**[S]:** Yeah. That would keep anyone up.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If FBA worked, what would you want it making, monthly, in a year?

**[R]:** Four thousand dollars net profit. That covers the mortgage and essentials.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you?

**[R]:** That I could stop checking email at 5:30. *(pause)* And sleep.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** You've done the analysis. What's stopped you from launching already?

**[R]:** I have a spreadsheet of 280 potential products. I cannot decide. Every product has risk. I keep adding columns.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Keep adding columns. That's a very engineer problem.

**[R]:** *(laughs)* My wife says the same.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take to actually pick one?

**[R]:** Someone with experience to look at my data and challenge my assumptions. I am alone with the spreadsheet.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[R]:** Eight.

**[S]:** What makes it an eight?

**[R]:** Twenty-two engineers.

**[S]:** And what's holding it back from a ten?

**[R]:** The data. I need to see it works.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Couple money questions, and I know you like numbers. *(laughs)* What's your take-home, monthly?

**[R]:** Seven thousand two hundred.

**[S]:** And monthly savings, on a usual month?

**[R]:** One thousand five hundred.

**[S]:** And total savings, outside retirement?

**[R]:** Forty-eight thousand. Of which I would consider fifteen to twenty as risk capital.

**[S]:** That's very clear. *(laughs)* And would you make the decision with your wife?

**[R]:** Jointly. Always.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, the German acquisition has already let go of twenty-two engineers, and after fourteen years you're checking email at 5:30 every morning and doing calculations at dinner. Seven or eight months. You've got 280 products in a spreadsheet and keep adding columns, and what you said you need is someone experienced to challenge your assumptions so you can pick one and get to four K net. Next step would be a Zoom with Daniel, one of Adam's head coaches. He was a financial analyst before Amazon. He'll go through your spreadsheet and bring the real data on results you asked about at the start. Your wife's welcome too. Lunch again, or an evening?

**[R]:** Saturday. My parents take the children to temple.

**[S]:** Saturday at 10 or 11:30?

**[R]:** 10.

**[S]:** Saturday at 10. Personal email, not work. *(laughs)*

**[R]:** *(slight laugh)* Definitely personal. *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Accept it, and bring the spreadsheet.

**[R]:** Accepted. I will.

**[S]:** And Raj, maybe check email at 7 tomorrow instead of 5:30. *(laughs)*

**[R]:** *(pause)* I will try.

## What to notice

- **The data test at the open.** "It's the one I'd ask too… I'm not going to guess, because you'd catch me." He respects honesty more than a made-up number.
- **The logical answer hides the feeling.** "What's the problem with that, for you specifically?" then "Has something happened?" Analytical people give you specifics if you ask for specifics.
- **Use their language: numbers.** "What calculations?" "Seven months." That's how an engineer tells you how he feels.
- **"Keep adding columns."** A gentle joke that names his blocker. The roadblock is guidance, in his words: "I am alone with the spreadsheet."
- **Close the loop at the booking.** He asked for data at the start, and the pitch promises it.
