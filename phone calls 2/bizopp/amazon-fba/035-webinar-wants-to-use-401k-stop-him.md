# 035 · Amazon FBA · Webinar attendee · 59, offers his 401(k): stop him

**Prospect:** Frank, 59, regional sales manager for industrial supplies, Cincinnati · **Lead:** attended the webinar, stayed for Q&A · **Money:** capital · **Ends:** booked, with retirement money off the table

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Frank, how are you doing? It's Stefan from Adam's team. You were on Adam's webinar last night. What stood out?

**[F]:** Well, son, I'll tell you. *(laughs)* I've been selling for thirty-five years, and Adam's a good salesman. I liked him. But what really got me was the guy who started at 55. I'm 59. I thought, well, Frank, you're not dead yet.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* You're not dead yet. So what's got you thinking about Amazon at 59?

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[F]:** The company's "restructuring the regions." That means they're getting rid of the old guys and hiring kids at half the salary. I've seen it happen to three of my buddies. I give it six months.

**[S]:** Thirty-five years in sales. What do you sell?

**[F]:** Industrial supplies. Shop equipment, safety stuff. I know that market cold.

**[S]:** And have you done anything online?

**[F]:** I bought something on Amazon once. *(laughs)* That's it.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** Six months. How's that sitting with you?

**[F]:** *(pause)* I put on a brave face. But it stings, son. I gave them thirty-five years. I missed a lot. Hotels, airports. My daughter's prom, I was in Ohio. Well, I live in Ohio. I was in a different part of Ohio. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* And what did missing those things cost you?

**[F]:** My daughter's 31 now. She's got a little girl. Two years old. And I've seen her maybe six times. I'm always on the road. I don't want to miss my granddaughter like I missed my daughter.

**[S]:** "Like I missed my daughter."

**[F]:** *(quiet)* Yeah. That's the real thing, I suppose.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If Amazon worked, what would you want it doing in a year?

**[F]:** Four, five thousand a month. With my wife's teacher pension, we'd be fine when they let me go. I could even retire a couple years early.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you?

**[F]:** Sunday dinners. With my granddaughter. Every Sunday.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** What do you think's stopped you from starting already?

**[F]:** Never needed to. *(laughs)* The job was the plan. And I don't know the first thing about online. I'm a handshake guy.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take?

**[F]:** Somebody patient to walk an old dog through it. And I know the industrial market. That's gotta be worth something.

**[S]:** That's a real advantage, knowing a market from the inside.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[F]:** Nine. Six months, son. I can count.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Couple money questions. What's coming in now, monthly?

**[F]:** About eighty-five hundred after tax. My wife's retired, she gets her pension.

**[S]:** And are you putting aside each month?

**[F]:** About two thousand.

**[S]:** And for Amazon, it takes money to make money, inventory and so on. What would you—

**[F]:** Oh, I'll make it easy for you. I've got about four hundred thousand in my 401(k). I can pull from that. Money's not a problem.

**[S]:** *(pause)* Frank, I appreciate you being so open. But I'm going to stop you there, because I'd say this to my own dad. Retirement money stays retirement money. You're 59. If anything went sideways, there's no time to rebuild it. I wouldn't want you touching that for this. Is that okay?

**[F]:** *(pause)* Well. That's not what I expected you to say.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* I know. I'd rather you trust me than regret it.

**[F]:** *(laughs)* My wife's gonna love you.

**[S]:** So outside the 401(k), regular savings?

**[F]:** About sixty thousand in savings. Emergency and extra.

**[S]:** And from that, what would feel comfortable?

**[F]:** Fifteen, maybe twenty.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, after thirty-five years they're restructuring and you give it six months. You missed your daughter's prom on the road, and you've seen your granddaughter maybe six times, and you don't want to miss her like you missed your daughter. You want four, five thousand a month so you can have Sunday dinners with her. You know the industrial market cold, and what you said you need is somebody patient to walk you through the online side. Next step would be a Zoom with Steve, one of Adam's head coaches. He's in his fifties too, and he'll look at whether your industrial knowledge can be a product. And Frank, I'm putting a note for him: no retirement money. Your wife should be on the call. When are you home?

**[F]:** I'm on the road Monday to Wednesday. Thursday or Friday.

**[S]:** Thursday at 5 or Friday at 10?

**[F]:** Friday at 10. I'll work from home.

**[S]:** Best email?

**[F]:** *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Can you check it on your phone? Take your time.

**[F]:** Give me a sec, I'm not great with these… there. Accepted!

**[S]:** Perfect. Speak Friday, Frank.

---

## What to notice

- **When they offer retirement money, stop them on the spot.** "I'd say this to my own dad." It's right, it's legally safer, and it's the biggest trust moment on the call.
- **Put it in the closer note, and tell him you did.** He knows you meant it.
- **"Like I missed my daughter."** For older prospects the pain is often the years, not the money. Repeat it back once.
- **"That's a real advantage, knowing a market from the inside."** Turn their age and experience into a strength.
- **Be patient at the email step.** "Take your time." Don't rush older prospects there.
