# 061 · Tech sales · Application · Lives outside the market the program serves: fit DQ

**Prospect:** Ahmed, 30, customer support at a telecom company, Lagos, Nigeria · **Lead:** application from a YouTube ad (US-focused program) · **Money:** n/a · **Ends:** fit DQ, with honest direction

> **Fit DQ.** The program places people into US tech companies, and needs US work authorization. That's not about him as a person, it's about what the program can actually do. Say it plainly, and don't waste his time.

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Ahmed, how are you doing? It's Stefan from Chris's team. You put an application in about getting into tech sales. What made you apply?

**[A]:** Hello, Stefan! Yes, thank you for calling. I have been in customer support for six years, and I want to move into sales. I watched Chris's videos. He is very good.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** Thank you. Where are you based, Ahmed?

**[A]:** Lagos, Nigeria.

**[S]:** And are you looking for remote roles with US companies, or roles there in Nigeria?

**[A]:** US companies. Remote. Because the pay is much better.

**[S]:** That makes sense. Do you have authorization to work for US companies, like a work visa or a green card?

**[A]:** *(pause)* No. I thought remote work would not need that.

**[S]:** I understand. I'm going to be straight with you, Ahmed, because I don't want to waste your time. Chris's program places people into SDR jobs as employees of US companies, and those companies need US work authorization for that. So right now, it wouldn't be a fit, and I'd be doing you wrong if I booked you in.

**[A]:** *(pause)* Ah. I see. That is disappointing.

**[S]:** I know, I'm sorry. It's nothing to do with you or your skills. Six years in support is real experience. It's just how those jobs work.

**[A]:** Thank you for being honest. The last company that called me tried to sell me anyway.

**[S]:** No, that's not right. Can I give you a couple of ideas?

**[A]:** Yes, please.

**[S]:** One: a lot of companies hire remote sales contractors, not employees, especially in high-ticket coaching and online businesses. Appointment setting is one of them. Those often don't need US authorization, because you're paid as a contractor. Look up remote appointment setter roles. Two: some big tech companies have sales teams in Lagos or across Africa. Look at their careers pages directly. And three: your customer support experience is a real advantage either way. Make sure your resume says how many customers you handled, what you resolved, and any numbers you hit.

**[A]:** Appointment setting. I will look into that. Thank you, Stefan.

**[S]:** Good luck, Ahmed. I mean it.

---

## What to notice

- **Soft qual catches it early:** "Are you looking for roles with US companies, or there?" then a direct, neutral question about work authorization.
- **A fit DQ is about the program, not the person.** "It's nothing to do with you or your skills… it's just how those jobs work."
- **Don't fudge it to book the call.** Booking someone the program can't help is a refund waiting to happen, and it's wrong.
- **Give real, honest direction** (contractor roles, local tech teams, resume numbers). Don't promise anything.
- **"The last company tried to sell me anyway."** Your honesty is what people remember.
