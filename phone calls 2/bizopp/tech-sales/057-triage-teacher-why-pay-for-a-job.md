# 057 · Tech sales (career change) · Booked a slot (triage) · Teacher: "Why would I pay to get a job?"

**Prospect:** Megan, 32, middle school English teacher, 8 years, Columbus, Ohio · **Lead:** booked a 4:15 slot from Facebook · **Money:** program · **Ends:** booked

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Megan, how are you? It's Stefan from Chris's team. I've got you down for 4:15 about getting into tech sales. What got you booking in?

**[M]:** Hi! Sorry, I'm in my car in the school parking lot. *(laughs)* It's the only quiet place.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* The parking lot office. Are you parked?

**[M]:** Parked. Just sitting here.

**[S]:** So what got you booking in?

**[M]:** Honestly? A parent emailed my principal last week because I gave her kid a B-minus. And I had to have a meeting about it. About a B-minus.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** A meeting about a B-minus. How long you been teaching?

**[M]:** Eight years. Eighth grade English.

**[S]:** And have you done any sales, or looked into tech sales properly yet?

**[M]:** I've applied to like thirty SDR jobs on LinkedIn. One interview. They said "no relevant experience."

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** What happened after the B-minus meeting?

**[M]:** I sat in this exact parking spot and cried. And then I went home and graded till 11. *(laughs)* And I thought, I can't do this for another twenty years.

**[S]:** What's the job actually doing to you?

**[M]:** I'm making forty-nine thousand. I buy my own supplies. I grade every night and every Sunday. My husband says I'm married to my red pen. And the parents treat me like I work for them.

**[S]:** "Married to my red pen." How long have you felt like that?

**[M]:** The last three years. Since COVID, honestly. It's a different job now.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If you landed a tech sales role, where would you want to be in a year?

**[M]:** Even sixty-five base would be more than I make. With commission, my friend who switched is at ninety-five.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you?

**[M]:** Sundays. *(laughs)* No grading on Sundays. And honestly, we want a baby, and I don't want to be grading essays with a newborn.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** Thirty applications, one interview. What do you think's been stopping them?

**[M]:** My resume screams "teacher." They see it and skip it.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take?

**[M]:** Somebody to rewrite it so it doesn't scream teacher. And practice interviews. I've never done a sales interview in my life.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[M]:** Nine.

**[S]:** What makes it a nine?

**[M]:** The B-minus. *(laughs)* And Sundays. And the baby.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Couple money questions, just so Jess can map out something realistic. With how expensive everything is, how much are you two putting aside each month?

**[M]:** About six hundred. Can I ask something, though? Why would I pay for something to get a job? Shouldn't jobs pay me? My friend didn't pay anything.

**[S]:** Totally fair question, and I'm glad you asked. Honestly, some people do get in for free, like your friend. But you've already tried the free way, thirty applications and one interview. What usually makes the difference is someone fixing your resume so it doesn't scream teacher, practising the interviews with you, and sometimes intros to companies. That's what costs money. And if Jess thinks you don't need that, she'll tell you. Fair?

**[M]:** *(pause)* Yeah. That actually makes sense.

**[S]:** And in total, saved?

**[M]:** About nine thousand.

**[S]:** And if Jess finds the right fit, is investing in yourself to make the switch something you'd be in a position to do?

**[M]:** I'd talk to my husband. But yes, probably.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, after eight years you had a meeting about a B-minus and cried in this parking spot. You're making forty-nine, buying your own supplies, grading every Sunday, married to your red pen. You want sixty-five base or more so Sundays are yours before a baby. Thirty applications got you one interview because your resume screams teacher, and what you said you need is someone to rewrite it and practise interviews with you. Next step would be a Zoom with Jess, one of Chris's career coaches. She was a teacher too. She'll go through your resume and tell you straight what it'd take. Your husband's welcome. Evening or Saturday?

**[M]:** Saturday. Less grading on Saturday.

**[S]:** Saturday at 10 or 11:30?

**[M]:** 10.

**[S]:** Best email? Not the school one.

**[M]:** *(laughs)* Definitely not. *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Bring your resume. And Megan, go home. *(laughs)*

**[M]:** *(laughs)* Going now.

## What to notice

- **"Are you parked?"** Safety first, and it also tells you her state (sitting in the lot after school).
- **"A meeting about a B-minus."** Repeat the absurd detail. She laughs, then tells you the real story.
- **"Married to my red pen."** Her husband's line is the impact. Repeat it once.
- **"Why would I pay to get a job?"** Agree first ("some people get in for free, like your friend"). Then her own thirty applications are the reason, then what actually costs money, then the out: "if she thinks you don't need it, she'll tell you."
- **The why-now in the goal** (Sundays, a baby). "What would that mean for you?" finds it.
