# 064 · SMMA · Old lead, 7 months · Tried Fiverr, stuck at $50 gigs

**Prospect:** Victor, 31, graphic designer at a print shop, Chicago · **Lead:** applied in April, never booked · **Money:** program · **Ends:** booked

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Victor, how's it going, man? It's Stefan from Sam's team. Random one. You applied back in April about starting a social media agency. Where are you at with it now?

**[V]:** Oh, man. Yeah. I actually tried. On Fiverr.

**[S]:** Nice, you actually did something. How'd it go?

**[V]:** *(laughs)* I've done like forty gigs. At fifty bucks each. Instagram post packs.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** Forty gigs. That's real work. And you're still at the print shop?

**[V]:** Yeah. Full-time. Graphic design. Menus, banners, business cards.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** Forty gigs at fifty bucks. How's that been?

**[V]:** Exhausting, honestly. Every client wants like ten revisions. One lady made me redo a post eleven times. For fifty dollars. And Fiverr takes twenty percent.

**[S]:** Eleven revisions, for forty bucks after fees.

**[V]:** Yeah. I did the math once. I was making like six dollars an hour.

**[S]:** And how did that feel, doing the math?

**[V]:** Like an idiot. *(laughs)* I'm a professional designer. I went to SAIC. And I'm on Fiverr competing with guys charging five bucks.

**[S]:** And the print shop?

**[V]:** Pays the bills. Barely. Forty-two K. My boss has me designing menus for every Thai restaurant on the North Side. *(laughs)* I could design a pad thai menu in my sleep.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* How long have you felt stuck like that?

**[V]:** Since I graduated, honestly. Seven years.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If the agency worked properly, where would you want it in a year?

**[V]:** Five clients on retainer. Real retainers, like a thousand, fifteen hundred a month each. Not fifty-dollar gigs.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you?

**[V]:** That I'm getting paid like a professional. And I could leave the print shop and stop doing Thai menus. *(laughs)* And honestly, my girlfriend and I want to move in together. We can't on forty-two K in Chicago.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** You're clearly a good designer, and you've done forty gigs. What do you think's stopped you from getting real retainer clients?

**[V]:** I don't know how to get clients off Fiverr. I don't know how to sell. I just make stuff. And I don't know how to price higher without people laughing at me.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take?

**[V]:** Someone to show me how to find real businesses, and how to pitch a retainer. And honestly, give me the confidence to charge what I'm worth.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[V]:** Eight.

**[S]:** What makes it an eight?

**[V]:** Six dollars an hour. *(laughs)* And moving in with her.

**[S]:** And what happens if nothing changes in six months?

**[V]:** Eighty Fiverr gigs and a hundred more Thai menus. *(laughs)* No.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Couple money questions, so Rachel can build a realistic plan. With the print shop and Fiverr, what's coming in each month?

**[V]:** Like thirty-two hundred, plus maybe four hundred from Fiverr.

**[S]:** And how much are you able to put aside?

**[V]:** Like four hundred.

**[S]:** And in total, saved?

**[V]:** About eight K.

**[S]:** And if Rachel finds the right fit, is investing in yourself to get off Fiverr something you'd be in a position to do?

**[V]:** Yeah. I'm basically paying Fiverr twenty percent already. *(laughs)*

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, you're a professional designer from SAIC doing forty Fiverr gigs at fifty bucks, eleven revisions, six dollars an hour after fees, competing with five-dollar guys. At the print shop you're doing Thai menus on forty-two K, and you can't move in with your girlfriend in Chicago on that. You want five real retainers at a thousand to fifteen hundred each. What you said you need is someone to show you how to find real businesses, pitch a retainer, and charge what you're worth. Next step would be a Zoom with Rachel, one of Sam's head coaches. She'll look at your portfolio and map out how to go from gigs to retainers. After work?

**[V]:** After 6.

**[S]:** Best email?

**[V]:** *(gives it)*

## What to notice

- **The old lead tried and is stuck.** Praise the action first ("you actually did something"), then the numbers: forty gigs, eleven revisions, six dollars an hour.
- **"Did the math once."** Prospects who've worked out their own hourly rate already feel the pain. Ask "how did that feel?"
- **"I went to SAIC."** That's identity. A professional on Fiverr against five-dollar guys. Let him say it.
- **The roadblock is knowledge plus confidence:** selling, pricing, "charge what I'm worth."
- **"I'm basically paying Fiverr twenty percent already."** He made the investment argument himself. Don't add to it.
