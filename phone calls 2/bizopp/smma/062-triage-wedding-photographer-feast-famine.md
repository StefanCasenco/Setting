# 062 · SMMA (social media agency) · Booked a slot (triage) · Wedding photographer, feast or famine

**Prospect:** Ellie, 29, wedding photographer, self-employed, Nashville · **Lead:** booked an 11am call · **Money:** program · **Ends:** booked

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Ellie, how are you? It's Stefan from Sam's team. I've got you down for 11 about starting a social media agency. What got you booking in?

**[E]:** Hi! So I'm a wedding photographer, and every year I go through the same thing. Wedding season, April to October, I'm slammed, making good money. Then November hits and… nothing. I've had one booking since October.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** One since October. How long you been shooting weddings?

**[E]:** Six years.

**[S]:** And have you done any social media work for anyone?

**[E]:** I run my own Instagram. Fourteen thousand followers, most of my bookings come from it. And I did my friend's coffee shop for free. Their followers went from nine hundred to four thousand in five months.

**[S]:** Nine hundred to four thousand. For free. *(laughs)*

**[E]:** *(laughs)* I know. I'm bad at charging.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** What's the slow season like for you?

**[E]:** Scary. Every year I tell myself I'll save enough in summer. And every year I don't, quite. And then January comes, and I'm checking my account every morning.

**[S]:** Every morning in January.

**[E]:** Like a ritual. *(laughs)* Not a fun one.

**[S]:** And the busy season, how is that on you?

**[E]:** Honestly? I'm getting tired of weddings. Twelve-hour days on my feet, then sixty hours editing each one. And all my friends are getting married, and I can't go to their weddings because I'm shooting strangers' weddings.

**[S]:** Wait. You miss your friends' weddings to shoot strangers' weddings?

**[E]:** *(laughs)* Yes! I missed my best friend's bachelorette this year. I was in Asheville shooting a couple I'd met twice.

**[S]:** How did that feel?

**[E]:** *(pause)* Awful. She was nice about it. But I saw the photos on Instagram, and I just felt left out of my own life.

**[S]:** "Left out of my own life."

**[E]:** Yeah. That's most summers, honestly.

**[S]:** How long has it felt like that?

**[E]:** The last two years.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If the agency worked, where would you want it in a year?

**[E]:** Twenty-five hundred a month from retainers, all year. Then I only shoot ten weddings I actually want, not thirty.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you?

**[E]:** I'd go to my friends' weddings. *(laughs)* And not check my bank account every morning in January.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** You already grew a coffee shop four times over. What's stopped you from doing it for paying clients?

**[E]:** Pricing. Contracts. I don't know what to charge, and I don't know how to find clients who'll pay. It feels pushy.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take?

**[E]:** Someone to show me how to package it. And how to pitch without feeling gross. *(laughs)*

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[E]:** Eight.

**[S]:** What makes it an eight?

**[E]:** January's coming. *(laughs)* And the bachelorette.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Couple money questions, so Rachel can build something realistic. Averaged across the year, what do you take home a month?

**[E]:** About thirty-two hundred. But it's like six in summer and five hundred in winter.

**[S]:** And how much is saved right now?

**[E]:** About seven thousand. That's meant to get me to April. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** And if Rachel finds the right fit, is investing in yourself to set up the agency side something you'd be in a position to do, or is it too tight before April?

**[E]:** It's tight. But if it fixes winters, it's worth it. I'd want to see the plan.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, you're slammed April to October, then one booking since October, and you check your bank account every morning in January. You missed your best friend's bachelorette to shoot strangers in Asheville, and you feel left out of your own life. You grew a coffee shop from nine hundred to four thousand for free. You want twenty-five hundred a month in retainers so you can shoot ten weddings you actually want. What you said you need is someone to show you how to package and pitch without feeling gross. Next step would be a Zoom with Rachel, one of Sam's head coaches. She'll look at the coffee shop results and map out pricing, and she'll be straight about the money before April. I'm guessing you're free-ish right now? *(laughs)*

**[E]:** *(laughs)* Unfortunately very free.

**[S]:** Tomorrow at 11 or Thursday at 2?

**[E]:** Tomorrow.

**[S]:** Best email?

**[E]:** *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Bring the coffee shop's before and after. And Ellie, go to the next bachelorette. *(laughs)*

**[E]:** *(laughs)* I will!

---

## What to notice

- **She's already done it once** (the coffee shop). Ask for the numbers early. It's proof for her and for the closer.
- **"Wait, you miss your friends' weddings to shoot strangers' weddings?"** Saying the irony out loud with surprise. She laughs, then tells you the real cost.
- **"Left out of my own life."** That's the pain. Repeat it once.
- **The roadblock is guidance on the business side:** packaging and pitching "without feeling gross".
- **Seasonal money:** ask the average, the swing, and how long the savings have to last. Then an honest binary on whether it's too tight.
