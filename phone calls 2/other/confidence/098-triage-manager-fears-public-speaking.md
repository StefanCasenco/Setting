# 098 · Confidence coaching · Booked a slot (triage) · New manager, terrified of presenting

**Prospect:** Rachel, 35, just promoted to operations manager at a hospital system, Nashville · **Lead:** booked a lunch call from LinkedIn · **Money:** program · **Ends:** booked

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hi Rachel, how are you? It's Stefan from Ellis's team. I've got you down for 12:30. What got you booking in?

**[R]:** Hi. So, I got promoted. Which is great. *(laughs)* But now I have to present to the leadership team every month. And I'm terrified.

**[S]:** Congrats on the promotion. Terrified how?

**[R]:** Like, physically. My hands shake. My voice goes up. Last week I presented the Q3 numbers and I blanked on a slide. In front of the CEO. Just… nothing. For like ten seconds.

**[S]:** Ten seconds in front of the CEO.

**[R]:** It felt like ten minutes. *(laughs nervously)*

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** How long have you been in the new role?

**[R]:** Two months. I was a supervisor for six years before.

**[S]:** And have you worked on public speaking before?

**[R]:** I took a Toastmasters class once. Went twice. *(laughs)*

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** What happened after the blank?

**[R]:** My boss was nice about it. But I went to my car after and cried. And then I couldn't sleep, thinking about next month's.

**[S]:** And how's it been affecting you, since then?

**[R]:** I'm dreading it constantly. I've got the next one in three weeks, and I already feel sick. I'm second-guessing everything. Like, maybe I shouldn't have taken the job.

**[S]:** "Maybe I shouldn't have taken the job."

**[R]:** *(pause)* Yeah. Which is crazy, because I'm good at the actual job. I fixed their scheduling. I saved them like four hundred K in overtime last year. That's why they promoted me.

**[S]:** Four hundred K. And you're thinking you shouldn't have taken it because of a presentation.

**[R]:** *(laughs)* When you say it like that.

**[S]:** How long has speaking in front of people been hard for you?

**[R]:** Honestly? Forever. I used to fake sick on presentation days in school.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If this worked, where would you want to be by that presentation in three weeks?

**[R]:** Not shaking. Getting through it without blanking. And honestly, in six months, actually being good at it. Like, owning the room.

**[S]:** And what would that mean for you?

**[R]:** That I deserve the job. *(pause)* That's the real one.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** What do you think's been stopping you from getting past it?

**[R]:** I avoided it my whole career. I was good at the work, so nobody made me present. Now I have to, and I've never practiced.

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take?

**[R]:** Someone to practice with. And someone to help with the panic, the shaking. The content I know.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[R]:** Ten. Three weeks.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Couple money questions, so Priya can put something realistic together. What's coming in each month, with the new role?

**[R]:** About seventy-four hundred.

**[S]:** And are you able to put aside each month?

**[R]:** About fifteen hundred.

**[S]:** And if Priya finds the right fit, is investing in yourself something you'd be in a position to do?

**[R]:** Yes. Honestly, my company might reimburse it. Professional development.

**[S]:** That's worth asking. Priya can give you what you'd need for that.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, you got promoted two months ago because you saved them four hundred K. Now you present monthly, you blanked for ten seconds in front of the CEO, cried in your car, and you've started thinking maybe you shouldn't have taken the job. The next one's in three weeks and you already feel sick. You want to get through it without shaking, own the room in six months, and feel like you deserve the job. What you said you need is someone to practise with and help with the panic. Next step would be a Zoom with Priya, one of Ellis's coaches. She works with new leaders on exactly this, and with three weeks she'll map out a plan for that presentation. Lunch again?

**[R]:** Yes. Lunch is safest. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** Thursday at 12:30 or Friday at 12:30?

**[R]:** Thursday.

**[S]:** Best email? Personal one.

**[R]:** *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. Bring the slides from last time and next month's if you have them. And Rachel, four hundred K. You deserve the job.

**[R]:** *(quiet)* Thank you.

## What to notice

- **"Ten seconds in front of the CEO."** Repeat the specific moment. "It felt like ten minutes."
- **"Maybe I shouldn't have taken the job."** That's the limiting belief. Repeat it, then put her own facts next to it: "Four hundred K. And you're thinking you shouldn't have taken it because of a presentation."
- **"Fake sick in school."** The timeframe branch: she's wanted to fix this forever and avoided it. That's the roadblock.
- **A ten with a real deadline** (three weeks). Don't over-question a clear ten.
- **Employer reimbursement is common for professional coaching.** Point it out and pass it to the closer.
