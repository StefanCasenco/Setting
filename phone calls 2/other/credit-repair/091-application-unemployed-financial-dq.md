# 091 · Credit repair · Application · Unemployed, no money at all: financial DQ, with real help

**Prospect:** Dwayne, 50, laid off from a factory 5 months ago, Gary, Indiana · **Lead:** application from a Facebook ad · **Money:** none · **Ends:** financial DQ, pointed to free help

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Dwayne, how are you doing? It's Stefan from Andre's team. You put an application in about your credit. What made you apply?

**[D]:** Hey. Yeah. Honestly, I'm just trying to get my life back together.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** I hear you. What's going on?

**[D]:** I got laid off in June. Steel plant. Twenty-two years. They shut down my line.

**[S]:** Twenty-two years. I'm sorry, man.

**[D]:** Yeah. And since then, I been behind on everything. Car note, credit cards. My credit's shot. And now I can't even get approved for an apartment, 'cause my landlord's selling the building.

**[S]:** And are you working anywhere right now?

**[D]:** No. Unemployment ran out last month. I'm applying everywhere.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** How are you holding up?

**[D]:** *(pause)* Not great, honestly. I'm 50. Nobody wants to hire a 50-year-old steelworker. And I got to be out of my place by January.

**[S]:** That's a lot at once. Is there anyone helping you?

**[D]:** My daughter, a little. She's got her own kids.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Dwayne, let me ask a couple money questions, just so I know what's realistic. Is anything coming in right now?

**[D]:** No. Nothing since unemployment ended.

**[S]:** Anything saved?

**[D]:** Like four hundred dollars.

**[S]:** Okay. Thank you for being honest with me. I'm going to be straight with you, because you've been straight with me, okay?

**[D]:** Okay.

**[S]:** Our service is paid, monthly, and right now, with no income and four hundred dollars, I'd be doing you wrong if I signed you up. That money needs to go to food and getting into a place by January. Not to us.

**[D]:** *(pause)* Yeah. I figured. I just didn't know where else to go.

**[S]:** I hear you. And there are places that can help for free. Can I give you a few?

**[D]:** Please.

**[S]:** One: look up nonprofit credit counseling. NFCC is the big one, nfcc.org. They're nonprofit, and they can go over your debts and talk to creditors for you, often free or very cheap. Two: call 211. It's a free number, and they connect you to local help with rent, housing, food, all of it. In Indiana they can point you to emergency rental assistance. Three: ask your union, if you were union, about displaced worker programs. Some plants that close have retraining money, and some of it's for people over 50.

**[D]:** I was union. USW. I didn't know about any of that.

**[S]:** Call them first. Seriously. And when you're working again and things are steadier, call me back, and we'll look at the credit properly. I mean that.

**[D]:** *(quiet)* Thank you, man. Everybody else just wanted my card number.

**[S]:** Not me. I'll text you those three things now. Hang in there, Dwayne.

## What to notice

- **You can feel the DQ coming, and you still ask the money questions.** The answers decide, not your gut. With no income, four hundred dollars and January coming, booking him would be wrong.
- **"I'm going to be straight with you, because you've been straight with me."** Ask permission, then give the reason, which is about *him*: food and getting into a place.
- **Give real free help:** nonprofit credit counselling (NFCC), 211 for local housing help, and his union's displaced worker programs. You don't need to know everything, just where to point him.
- **"Everybody else just wanted my card number."** This is the difference between a setter and a scammer.
- **A clean, kind DQ is part of the job.** Scott: it respects your time, the closer's time, and the prospect's.
