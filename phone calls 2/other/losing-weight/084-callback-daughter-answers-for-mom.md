# 084 · Losing weight · He calls back, but the daughter answers for her mom · Language barrier, family on speaker

**Prospect:** Rosa, 55, house cleaner, speaks mostly Spanish · her daughter **Ana**, 24, answers and translates · San Antonio · **Lead:** Ana filled out the form for her mom · **Money:** program (health) · **Ends:** booked with both

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hi, it's Stefan from Claire's team. I'm calling for Rosa?

**[A]:** Hi, this is her daughter, Ana. My mom's right here, but her English isn't great. I actually filled out the form for her. Is that okay?

**[S]:** Of course, that's totally fine. Thank you, Ana. Would it be easier if I talk to you, and you help your mom? Or is she okay with some English?

**[A]:** She understands a lot. Speaking's harder. Let me put you on speaker. *(to Rosa, in Spanish)* Mami, es el señor de la página.

**[ROSA]:** Hola. Hello.

**[S]:** Hola, Rosa! Mucho gusto. *(laughs)* That's about all my Spanish.

**[ROSA]:** *(laughs)* Is good!

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** Ana, what made you fill it out for her?

**[A]:** Her doctor told her she has high blood pressure and her sugar's high. She needs to lose weight. But she doesn't know where to start, and everything online is in English, for, like, young people.

**[S]:** And what does your mom do for work?

**[A]:** She cleans houses. Five days a week. It's really physical.

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** Rosa, how do you feel? Day to day?

**[ROSA]:** *(slowly)* Tired. Always tired. My knees… *(to Ana in Spanish)*

**[A]:** She says her knees hurt when she cleans, especially bathrooms. Kneeling.

**[S]:** That sounds hard. And how has it been for the family?

**[A]:** *(pause)* Honestly, I'm scared. My dad had a stroke two years ago. He's okay now, but he can't work. So my mom's the main income. If something happens to her…

**[S]:** That's a lot on her. And on you.

**[A]:** *(quiet)* Yeah. I check on her every night.

**[S]:** Rosa, what does your family mean to you, in all this?

**[A]:** *(translates)*

**[ROSA]:** *(in Spanish, then Ana translates)* She says she wants to see her grandkids grow up. My sister just had a baby. She wants to dance at my wedding someday. *(laughs, emotional)* She's never said that before.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If this worked, where would you want her to be in six months?

**[A]:** Her blood pressure down. Her doctor happy. And honestly, less tired.

**[S]:** Rosa, what would that mean for you?

**[ROSA]:** *(in English)* Play with baby. On the floor. *(laughs)*

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** What do you think it'd take?

**[A]:** Something in Spanish, or at least simple. With the food she actually cooks. Not "eat kale salads." She makes tamales. *(laughs)* She's not giving up tamales.

**[ROSA]:** *(laughs)* No!

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10, Ana?

**[A]:** Ten. Because of my dad.

**[S]:** And Rosa?

**[ROSA]:** *(holds up hands, Ana laughs)* She's saying ten.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Okay. And just so you know, our coaches aren't doctors, so anything about her blood pressure or sugar stays with her doctor. Couple money questions, so the coach knows what's realistic. What's coming in for the house each month?

**[A]:** My mom makes about twenty-two hundred. My dad gets disability, about nine hundred. I help with three hundred.

**[S]:** And are they able to put anything aside?

**[A]:** Not really. Maybe a hundred.

**[S]:** Thank you for being honest. And savings?

**[A]:** About two thousand. For emergencies.

**[S]:** Okay. Ana, I'll be transparent: coaching is an investment, and money's tight. The coach will be honest about whether it fits, and whether there are cheaper options that make more sense. Is that okay?

**[A]:** Yes. Thank you. That's really important.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, Rosa's doctor says her blood pressure and sugar are high. She's cleaning houses five days a week and kneeling hurts her knees. Your dad had a stroke, so she's the main income, and you check on her every night. Rosa wants to play on the floor with the new baby and dance at your wedding. What you need is something simple, with the food she actually cooks, tamales included. Next step would be a Zoom with Lucia, one of Claire's coaches. She speaks Spanish, so Rosa can talk to her directly. She'll be honest about cost. Ana, can you be on it too?

**[A]:** She speaks Spanish? Oh my God. Yes. *(to Rosa in Spanish)* Mami, la señora habla español.

**[ROSA]:** *(emotional)* Gracias.

**[S]:** When are you both free?

**[A]:** Saturday afternoon.

**[S]:** Saturday at 2 or 4?

**[A]:** 2.

**[S]:** Best email, Ana?

**[A]:** *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** Sent. And Rosa, keep the tamales. *(laughs)*

**[ROSA]:** *(laughs)* Sí!

## What to notice

- **The wrong person answers, and it's a family helper.** Say thank you, ask how they want to do it ("talk to you, or is she okay with some English?"), then go to speaker.
- **Speak to the prospect directly, even with a translator.** "Rosa, how do you feel?" She's the one deciding.
- **A tiny bit of their language, said humbly** ("that's about all my Spanish"), gets a laugh and builds warmth.
- **The family's pain is part of the call.** The dad's stroke makes her the main income, and that's the urgency.
- **Match the coach to the person** (a Spanish speaker). It removes the biggest barrier, and that's what got the "gracias."
