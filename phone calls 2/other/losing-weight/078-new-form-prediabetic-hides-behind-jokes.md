# 078 · Losing weight · New form · 52, doctor said "pre-diabetic," hides behind jokes

**Prospect:** Phil, 52, long-haul trucker, home on weekends, Indianapolis · **Lead:** form filled 15 minutes ago from a truck stop · **Money:** program (health) · **Ends:** booked with his wife

---

*— OPEN —*

**[S]:** Hey Phil, how's it going? It's Stefan from Claire's team. You just put your info in about losing some weight. What made you fill it out?

**[P]:** *(laughs)* My wife made me. I'm at a Love's in Missouri and she texted me the link with like six exclamation points.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Six exclamation points. That's serious.

**[P]:** That's DEFCON 1, buddy.

*— SOFT QUAL —*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* So what made her send it?

**[P]:** Ah, I had my DOT physical. Doc says I'm "pre-diabetic." Blood pressure's up. Gotta lose forty pounds. The usual. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** The usual. And how did it feel, hearing that?

**[P]:** Ah, it's fine. Everybody's pre-something at my age. *(laughs)*

*— CURRENT —*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Maybe. But your wife sent you six exclamation points to a truck stop in Missouri. So I'm guessing she's not taking it as "fine."

**[P]:** *(pause)* No. She's not. *(less laughing)* Her dad had diabetes. Lost a foot. She took care of him at the end.

**[S]:** Oh. That's serious.

**[P]:** Yeah. When I told her "pre-diabetic," she went white. Didn't talk to me the rest of the call.

**[S]:** And how about you, honestly? Not her. You.

**[P]:** *(long pause)* I guess it scared me a little. If I can't pass my DOT physical, I lose my CDL. That's my whole living. Thirty years. And I've got a granddaughter. She's two. I want to see her grow up. I don't want to be the grandpa with the bad foot.

**[S]:** Yeah. That makes total sense.

**[S]:** What's a normal day eating, on the road?

**[P]:** *(laughs)* Truck stops, all day. Roller grill hot dogs. Gas station breakfast burritos. Coffee with four creams, four sugars. I'm in the cab eleven hours.

**[S]:** Four and four. *(laughs)*

**[P]:** I like it sweet. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** How long have you been eating like that?

**[P]:** Thirty years. That's the job, buddy.

*— GOAL —*

**[S]:** If this worked, where would you want to be by your next physical?

**[P]:** Pass it. Without the doc giving me the look. And get the wife to stop sending exclamation points. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** And underneath the jokes, what would that mean for you?

**[P]:** *(pause)* Keeping my CDL. And being around for the little one.

*— ROADBLOCK —*

**[S]:** Have you tried anything before?

**[P]:** The wife put me on a diet once. Lasted four days. I was starving in the cab and caved at a Waffle House.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Waffle House always wins. What do you think it'd take this time?

**[P]:** Something that works in a truck. Nobody understands the truck. Every diet's like "meal prep on Sunday." Buddy, I'm in Missouri on Sunday.

*— URGENCY —*

**[S]:** On a scale of 1 to 10?

**[P]:** Eight. For the CDL.

**[S]:** What makes it an eight?

**[P]:** If I lose the CDL, I lose everything. And the foot thing. I can't do that to her.

*— MONEY —*

**[S]:** Has your doctor put you on anything?

**[P]:** She said maybe metformin if it doesn't come down in three months. She wants me to try first.

**[S]:** Okay. And just so you know, our coaches aren't doctors, so anything medical stays with her. That okay?

**[P]:** Yeah, sure.

**[S]:** Couple money questions. What are you spending on food on the road, roughly a week?

**[P]:** *(laughs)* Don't tell my wife. Like a hundred and fifty.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Secret's safe. And what's coming in, monthly?

**[P]:** About sixty-five hundred.

**[S]:** And are you comfortable putting some aside?

**[P]:** Yeah, we're alright. House is almost paid off.

**[S]:** And if Gary finds the right fit, is investing in yourself something you'd be in a position to do?

**[P]:** If it keeps my CDL, yeah. And she'll want in on it.

*— BOOK —*

**[S]:** So from what you said, the jokes are the jokes. But your DOT physical said pre-diabetic, and if you don't pass, you lose the CDL after thirty years. Your wife's dad lost a foot to diabetes and she went white when you told her. You've got a two-year-old granddaughter you want to see grow up. You're eating roller grill hot dogs and four-and-four coffee eleven hours a day, and every diet tells you to meal prep on Sunday when you're in Missouri. What you said you need is something that works in a truck. Next step would be a Zoom with Gary, one of Claire's coaches. He works with truckers all the time. And your wife should be on it. When are you home?

**[P]:** Saturday morning.

**[S]:** Saturday at 9 or 10:30?

**[P]:** 10:30. I'll be off the road by then.

**[S]:** Best email?

**[P]:** The wife's. She does all that. *(gives it)*

*— LOCK —*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Of course. Sent. And Phil, maybe three sugars this week instead of four. *(laughs)*

**[P]:** *(laughs)* Easy now.

## What to notice

- **Go along with the jokes first.** "Six exclamation points. That's serious." Match his energy. Don't kill it.
- **Then gently point at the gap between the joke and reality.** "Your wife sent you six exclamation points to a truck stop. I'm guessing she's not taking it as fine." It's still light, but true.
- **"And how about you, honestly? Not her. You."** Funny people talk about other people's feelings. Bring it back to him.
- **The real stakes came out:** the CDL. In this niche the pain is often about the job, not looks.
- **"Waffle House always wins."** Light humour that takes the shame out of a failed diet.
