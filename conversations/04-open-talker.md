# 04 · "Let me tell you how I got here"

**First answer type:** Open talker · **Offer:** trading · **Outcome:** booked
**Lead:** Sean, 44, ex-restaurant owner, Dublin, Ireland · **Surface story:** restaurant closed, wife back at work, looking for something to replace the income · **Hidden truth:** he feels like a failure, he is "the one who's meant to be bringing it in" · **Savings truth:** about €9,000 from selling the kitchen equipment, kept apart from the family accounts
**Resistance style:** talks a lot, wanders, jokes, gets the facts a bit wrong · **Depth target:** L4 · **Approx. duration:** ~12 min
**Setter focus:** one warm interruption, then "back up", then find the one real sentence in the story and follow it.

## The call

Now the wife's back full-time in the hospital, I do the school runs, send out CVs that go nowhere, and a mate said have a look at trading, so here I am, God help me.

> [CURRENT L1] One allowed burst, and it has everything in it. Don't summarise it. Find the one real sentence.
> [WHY THIS LINE] A warm interruption: acknowledge the story before you steer, so he doesn't feel cut off.

**S:** Sean, let me jump in, because that's a lot. Back up for a second. What was it like when the restaurant closed?

**P:** Ah, you get on with it. Cleared out the Monday.

**S:** Cleared out the Monday.

**S:** Not how it was meant to be?

> [CURRENT L3]

> [CURRENT L4]

**S:** Okay.

> [WHY THIS LINE] Silence after "Okay". He volunteers the next line. Don't comfort him.

**P:** I used to be the one on my feet till midnight. Now I'm watching the clock for the school pick-up.

**S:** What does that do to you?

**P:** Ah. I feel like I let them down, if I'm honest. The staff too. Fourteen of them.

**S:** Fourteen.

**P:** Fourteen. I still know their kids' names.

> [CURRENT L5] Feeling: let them down. Cost: the staff, his wife's hours, his own sense of himself. Exit test met. Stop here.

> [RECAP]

**S:** Okay so basically your main goal is to be the one bringing money in again, so your wife doesn't have to carry it, correct?

**P:** That's it. That's exactly it.

> [TIMEFRAME] [TEST A]

**S:** Got it. Give me the bigger picture, like how long have you been looking into trading?

**P:** Six months, maybe? Since the summer, anyway.

**S:** Okay and within the last six months… what have you tried in trading?

**P:** Watched a fella on YouTube. Opened an account, put a few quid in, never really got going.

**S:** Why haven't you been able to see success within trading over six months?

**P:** Ah, because I'm an eejit, probably. I keep stopping to do the school run and the CVs.

> [RECOVERY] Test A pulled self-blame, as it often does. Logged for the tally. The setter stays neutral and doesn't rescue him.

**S:** Okay.

> [WGW]

> [WHY NOW]

**S:** How much of that are you able to save?

**S:** I'm sure you know it takes money to make money. How much would you say you have saved up for your trading account?

**P:** I sold the kitchen equipment. About nine grand, give or take. That's separate from the family money, I made sure of that.

> [WHY THIS LINE] "Separate" is the word that matters. Note it, don't react and don't mention any price.

**S:** Okay, that's clear.
