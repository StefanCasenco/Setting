# 15 · "Maybe a few hundred, and I'd have to borrow the rest"

**First answer type:** No money (DQ at the ladder) · **Offer:** trading · **Outcome:** DQ
**Lead:** Tobi, 28, night security guard, Cape Town, South Africa · **Surface story:** "more money, the night shifts don't pay much" · **Hidden truth:** pays his younger sister's school costs, sleeps while she's awake, she told him she's "used to it" · **Savings truth:** a few hundred rand at most; would have to borrow the rest
**Resistance style:** honest, tired, jokes to cover the heavy parts, asks "why are you asking?" once · **Depth target:** L5 · **Approx. duration:** ~10 min
**Setter focus:** do the full CURRENT with care even on a call that will end in a DQ. Then keep the DQ line plain and never help anyone borrow.

## The call

> [FRAME]

**S:** Heeey… Tobi?

**P:** Ja, speaking.

**S:** This is Ștefan from $COMPANY, it looks like you've booked from an ad, about possible help with trading, is that right?

**P:** Ja, I think so. Saw it on Instagram.

**S:** Cool, just to give you some insight into our application process, it's two steps. The first step is this call. My goal here is to get an understanding of where you're at, some of your goals and see if we can actually help you. If by the end of the call I feel like that's the case, I'll walk you through the next stage, cool?

**P:** Okay, sure.

> [INTENT]

**S:** Okay, so tell me man, like… out of all the ways to make money, what had you interested in trading in the first place?

**P:** Honestly? Money. Night shifts don't pay much.

> [CURRENT L1] Vague money again. Ask for the real reason.

**S:** Sure man, most people want more money. What's the real reason behind it for you?

**P:** My sister. School stuff adds up.

> [CURRENT L2]

**S:** School stuff adds up. What do you mean?

**P:** Fees, transport, uniform. Her whole life, basically. Our mom can't work now, so it's me.

**S:** What do you do for work?

**P:** Security guard. A mall in Bellville. Nights.

> [CURRENT L3]

**S:** Walk me through a normal week.

**P:** Twelve hours, six nights. Well, five, sometimes six if someone's sick. Sleep from nine to two, sort her stuff, then work.

**S:** Nine to two. Where does your sister fit in?

**P:** I get home at seven. She's leaving for school as I'm walking in.

**S:** So you pass each other.

**P:** Ja. At the gate, mostly. *(short laugh)* Like colleagues.

> [WHY THIS LINE] The laugh covers it. Echo "colleagues" and leave the space.

**S:** Colleagues.

**P:** She's sixteen. She used to wait up for me. Last week she said she's used to it.

> [CURRENT L4] "Used to it" is the line that matters.

**S:** Used to it.

**P:** Ja. That one stayed with me. Anyway, I'm not here for therapy, hey.

**P:** Why are you asking all this? I thought this was about trading.

**S:** I'm not asking just for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, like a personal trainer would.

**P:** Okay. Fair.

**S:** What's the hardest part of that?

**P:** Sleeping while she's awake. I feel like I'm missing the whole thing.

> [CURRENT L5] One pass on cost.

**S:** What happens to that if nothing changes?

**P:** Next year is her matric. I'll sleep through it.

> [CURRENT L5] Exit test met: nights, sleeping while she's awake, "used to it", missing matric. Stop digging.

> [RECAP]

**S:** Okay so basically your main goal is to get off the night shifts and be there for your sister, correct?

**P:** Ja. And pay her school without the stress.

> [TIMEFRAME] [TEST B]

**S:** Got it. Give me the bigger picture, like how long have you been looking into trading?

**P:** Maybe eight months. Videos, mostly.

**S:** Okay and within the last eight months… what have you tried in trading?

**P:** A demo account on my phone. In the quiet hours on shift. It goes up and down.

**S:** What do you feel has been missing to get you there?

**P:** Someone to show me properly. I just copy the guys on YouTube.

> [WGW]

**S:** What happened with that?

**P:** Nothing, really. No money to start properly, so I stayed on demo.

> [WHY NOW]

**S:** What made you start looking now?

**P:** The matric thing. I thought, now or never.

> [FIN]

**S:** What would you want to be making every month from trading, to make it worth your time?

**P:** Like twenty thousand rand? Is that stupid?

**S:** It's a number. How much are you making right now? (just so I can understand how far away you are from that)

**P:** About nine thousand. Ten with the night allowance, if I do extras.

**S:** How much of that are you able to save?

**P:** Save? Ha. Maybe two hundred rand in a good month.

**S:** I'm sure you know it takes money to make money. How much would you say you have saved up for your trading account?

**P:** Maybe a few hundred. And I'd have to borrow the rest, I guess.

**S:** Okay. Thanks for being straight with me.

**P:** My cousin lends sometimes. Or those loan apps. Would that work?

> [RECOVERY] The setter starts to say something encouraging about finding the money, hears it, and stops. He does not help with borrowing, and he does not wrap it in hope.
> [WHY THIS LINE] Loan apps against a night guard's pay, with a sister's school fees depending on it, is exactly what he must not push.

**S:** There are ways to find the money, and... sorry, scratch that. I shouldn't have started that sentence.

**S:** Borrowing for this isn't something I'd ever suggest, least of all with your sister's school depending on your pay.

**P:** Ja. Okay. So that's a no.

**S:** Got it man. Right now it wouldn't make sense to book you. I'll send you some resources and we pick this up in a month or two.

**P:** No, I get it. Better than wasting your time.

**S:** It's not wasted. Carrying her school on night shifts is a lot, so look after your sleep, yeah?

**P:** Ha, I'll try. Nights don't make it easy.

**S:** I'll message you in a month or two. Take care of yourself, Tobi.

**P:** Thanks, Ștefan. I appreciate it.

**S:** Stay safe on shift, brother.

## What to notice
- The CURRENT was done in full even though the call never reached the pitch. It costs nothing, and the follow-up in two months starts from "used to it", not from a cold list.
- The slip is the point of the call. The setter hears himself and says so, plainly. A DQ that sounds hopeful about borrowing turns into a push.
- The DQ line is word for word and the ending is only human. No pitch, no "when you have the money" speech.

## Worse version

Bad line: "Don't worry about the money, there are loans and payment plans, we can find a way to get you started."
He answers: "Okay, I'll ask my cousin and take one of those apps."

Why it fails: it nudges someone with a sister's school fees to borrow for trading. The fix is the one used above: stop the sentence, say plainly that borrowing isn't something you'd suggest, then use the DQ line.
