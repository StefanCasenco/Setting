# 20 · "I'm a student, my mum could lend me"

**First answer type:** Student with no capital (DQ at the ladder) · **Offer:** ecom · **Outcome:** DQ
**Lead:** Sam, 19, second-year university student, Leeds, UK (£) · **Surface story:** "I need money, student life is expensive" · **Hidden truth:** his parents are in debt and both out of work, so he is the one trying to get ahead for the whole house · **Savings truth:** about £300 from a summer job; his mum "could lend" the rest
**Resistance style:** polite, a bit rushed between lectures, jokes about being skint, one wrong belief about how fast ecom pays · **Depth target:** L4 · **Approx. duration:** ~9 min
**Setter focus:** run the full CURRENT with care, then hear "my mum could lend me" and refuse to build on it. Plain DQ line, kind ending.

## The call

> [FRAME]

**S:** Heeey… Sam?

**P:** Yeah, hi.

**S:** This is Ștefan from $COMPANY, it looks like you've booked from an ad, about possible help with ecom, is that right?

**P:** Yeah. I've got about ten minutes before a seminar.

> [WHY THIS LINE] He gave a time limit. The frame is the same as always; the call simply stays inside his ten minutes.

> [FRAME] continues

**S:** Cool, just to give you some insight into our application process, it's two steps. The first step is this call. My goal here is to get an understanding of where you're at, some of your goals and see if we can actually help you. If by the end of the call I feel like that's the case, I'll walk you through the next stage, cool?

**P:** Yeah, fine.

> [INTENT]

**S:** Okay, so tell me man, like… out of all the ways to make money, what had you interested in ecom in the first place?

**P:** Student life's expensive. Figured I'd try something online.

> [CURRENT L1] Surface answer. "Expensive" is a fact, not yet a reason. Ask for the real one.

**S:** Sure man, most people want more money. What's the real reason behind it for you?

**P:** Just to not be skint all the time, I guess.

> [CURRENT L2] He deflected with a joke word. Echo it.

**S:** Skint. What do you mean?

**P:** Like, counting every pound. I work at a supermarket, ten hours. It doesn't go far.

**S:** Is that for rent, or for everything?

**P:** Rent mostly. My loan covers about half of it.

> [WHY THIS LINE] That question assumed the rent was the issue. It is only the surface of it, so the next one goes to the week itself.

> [CURRENT L3]

**S:** Walk me through a normal week.

**P:** Lectures Monday to Thursday. Shifts Friday and Saturday evenings. Sunday I'm in the library.

**S:** And money, in that week?

**P:** Tight. Pasta a lot. *(laughs)* Honestly it's fine.

> [WHY THIS LINE] "Honestly it's fine" with a laugh is the same door as before. The setter does not take it as an answer.

**S:** Fine?

**P:** Yeah. It's just… I'm not the only one it affects, if that makes sense.

**S:** Who else does it affect?

**P:** My mum and dad. Dad's business folded last year. Neither of them has a job right now.

> [CURRENT L4]

**S:** What's that like, being at uni with that going on?

**P:** I go home and there are letters on the mat. Red ones. Mum hides them, badly.

**S:** What's the hardest part of that?

**P:** Feeling like I should be helping and I'm just sat in a lecture. Like I'm wasting a year.

> [CURRENT L5] Pain in his words: "letters on the mat", "sat in a lecture", "wasting a year". One feeling (guilty, behind). One cost (cannot help at home). Exit test met, so no more digging.

**P:** Sorry, why are you asking all this? I thought this was about ecom.

**S:** I'm not asking just for the sake of asking. The better I understand you, the better I can help, like a personal trainer would.

**P:** Okay. Fair.

> [RECAP]

**S:** Okay so basically your main goal is to earn enough to help your mum and dad and stop feeling like you're wasting a year, correct?

**P:** Yeah. That's it. Honestly.

> [TIMEFRAME] [TEST B]

**S:** Got it. Give me the bigger picture, like how long have you been looking into ecom?

**P:** Since about Christmas. Videos mostly.

**S:** Okay and within the last ten months… what have you tried in ecom?

**P:** Opened a Shopify trial. Put up a couple of products. Sold nothing.

**S:** What do you feel has been missing to get you there?

**P:** Knowing what to actually sell. The videos say you're making money in a week or two.

> [WHY THIS LINE] That is not how it usually goes, but arguing a wrong belief helps nobody. The setter lets it sit and moves on.

> [WGW]

**S:** What happened with that?

**P:** I ran out of time. Exams, then I picked up extra shifts. Never went back to it.

> [WHY NOW]

**S:** What made you start looking now?

**P:** Letters again over the summer. And the new term starts soon.

> [FIN]

**S:** What would you want to be making every month from ecom, to make it worth your time?

**P:** Couple of grand? Is that realistic?

**S:** It's a number. How much are you making right now? (just so I can understand how far away you are from that)

**P:** About five hundred a month, from the shifts. Plus the loan.

**S:** How much of that are you able to save?

**P:** Nothing, really. Maybe fifty quid some months.

**S:** I'm sure you know it takes money to make money. How much would you say you have saved up for investing into ecom?

**P:** About three hundred, from last summer. But my mum could lend me the rest.

> [WHY THIS LINE] He just told us the parents are out of work, and now the plan is to borrow from his mum. The setter must not build on it, and must not make it sound like a good idea.

> [RECOVERY] The setter starts to say "that could work", hears where it leads, and stops mid-sentence.

**S:** That could... no. I'm going to stop that sentence.

**S:** I wouldn't suggest borrowing from your mum for this, least of all with the letters on the mat. That money has a job already.

**P:** Yeah. I mean, she did offer.

**S:** I believe you. I still wouldn't take it.

**P:** Okay. So what does that mean for me?

**S:** Got it man. Right now it wouldn't make sense to book you. I'll send you some resources and we pick this up in a month or two.

**P:** No, I get it. I kind of knew, honestly.

**S:** Thanks for being straight with me. Sounds like you're carrying a lot for nineteen.

**P:** Ha. Yeah. A bit.

**S:** Go and catch your seminar. Take care, Sam.

**P:** Cheers. You too.

## Depth scorecard
Layers reached L5 · exit test met: yes ("letters on the mat", "sat in a lecture", "wasting a year") · echoes used: 3 ("skint", "fine", "wasting") · questions that assumed something: 2 ("Is that for rent, or for everything?", "What's that like, being at uni with that going on?") · minutes in CURRENT: ~4

## What to notice
- The full CURRENT was run on a call that was always going to be a DQ. It cost four minutes and it gives the follow-up in two months something real to start from.
- "My mum could lend me" is the trap. The setter stops his own sentence, says plainly he wouldn't take it, and does not offer a way around the number. No loans, no plans, no hope.
- The wrong belief ("money in a week or two") is left alone. The DQ is about the savings only, and the ending is kind without being soft on the decision.

## If they had said X instead

**X1. "It's fine, it's my student loan I'd use."**
**P:** I could use some of my student loan.
**S:** I wouldn't suggest that either. That money is for your rent and your course.
**P:** Yeah. Fair.
**S:** Got it man. Right now it wouldn't make sense to book you. I'll send you some resources and we pick this up in a month or two.

**X2. He says "how much is it?" before the ladder.**
**P:** What's the actual price, though?
**S:** It's a four-figure investment, depending on the program, but we can't even know if we can help you yet. That's what the call with $CLOSER is for.
(Taster once, then back to the question on the table.)

**X3. He says "mum and dad don't know I'm doing this".**
**S:** Okay. Thanks for telling me.
(Note it, do not probe. It does not change the DQ.)

## Worse version

Bad line: "Your mum offered, so why not take it? You could pay her back in a few months."
He answers: "Yeah, maybe. I'll ask her tonight."

Why it fails: it pushes a nineteen-year-old to borrow from a household already in debt, and it promises a payback nobody can back. The fix is the one used above: stop the sentence, say plainly you wouldn't take it, then the DQ line.
