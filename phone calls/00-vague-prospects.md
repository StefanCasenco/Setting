# 00 · Vague prospects: "I just want to make more money"

From the **JDS & Setter Syndicate** group call (4 Nov). You played a deliberately vague prospect for George. Scott broke down how to handle it, then finished the call himself. This file is that lesson, plus Scott's 7-step framework from the same call. All 100 calls in `phone calls 2/` are built on it.

---

## 1. What you did as the prospect (the vague playbook)

Learn these, because real prospects do exactly this:

| What you said | What it does to the setter |
|---|---|
| "Everyone wants to make more money, right?" | Throws the question back. No "why". |
| "It's kind of boring." → "It's repetitive." → "That's what repetitive means, right?" | Gives a word, then refuses to explain it. Every "how so?" gets the same word back. |
| "Just saw the ad." | Kills the "why now" before it starts. |
| "Six months, but I drew a line in the sand." | Sounds like a trigger, but there's nothing behind it. |
| "I changed my beliefs." | Vague, and it pulls the setter off the main thread. |
| "I don't know, I just want to make more money." | Back to the start. A loop. |

## 2. What George did

**Good:**
- Didn't accept "everyone wants more money": *"I guess not everyone, bro. What's specifically happening in your life…"*
- Grabbed the one real word, "boring".
- Spotted the gap between six months of feeling it and two weeks of acting on it.

**Where it got stuck (George said this himself afterwards):**
- **The same "why" in different words.** *How so? → How come? → Any examples? → What impact?* You'd already said "repetitive", so each one got the same non-answer. George also flagged this when he played the prospect earlier: *"the only reason I started being a bit of an arse was because you asked me the same question again."*
- **Fishing for a trigger.** *"Did anything happen two or three weeks ago?"* Nothing happened. "Just saw the ad."
- **Arguing.** *"You didn't see a single ad in the last six months?"* That's true, but now it's a debate. Scott: *"You might win the argument. But your goal isn't to argue with prospects."*

## 3. How Scott fixed it

Scott's point: **stop asking for the reason. Change the angle, then pin it to something concrete.**

**Step 1. Challenge the surface reason with a reframe, not another "why".**
> "I hate to break it to you, man, but if you're getting into high-ticket sales, it's still going to be the same thing every day. Eight hours of calls. The biggest difference is you can do it from anywhere in the world and scale your income. So, knowing it's still going to be repetitive, why do you actually want to make a change?"

They can't answer "boring" any more. Now it has to be **money or freedom**.

**Step 2. If they won't give a number, pull them down to their current income first.**
> "How much are you actually making right now? … Okay. And if you were to make the transition, how much would you need to be making each month for it to be worth the change?"
> → "Five grand."

**Step 3. Ask what the number changes. Then follow their words, one at a time.**
> "Five grand. What does that actually change for you?" → "Replace my income, work remotely, travel more."
> "Do you want to travel more, or live in the same country?" *(catching a contradiction, said curiously)*
> "Where do you actually want to go?" → "Spain." → "Why Spain?" → "Ibiza, Marbella." → "They sound like party spots, man." → "Have some fun, I'm still young." → **"What do you mean when you say you're still young?"** → "I'm 23, I want to see the world."

Each question comes from the last answer, and each one is about a *new* word. Not one of them is "why?" again.

**Step 4. Tie it to the job.**
> "Do you think you'll be able to travel the world anytime soon if you're stuck in your 9 to 5?" → "No." → "Why not?" → "I'm stuck in the job and not making enough."
> "How important is it to you to travel while you're still young?"

**Step 5. Future-pace the cost.**
> "Let's say you don't make a change. You stay comfortable. How would it feel in five years if you'd never got to travel?"

**Step 6. If they say "I'll settle", don't fight. Ask once, then let it go.**
> "Is that what you're willing to settle for?"
> If yes: "How come you're even on the call today, then?"
> Scott: *"There's no point going into a boxing match to convince them that they want it. They have to want it anyway. If they're happy to slave away at their 9 to 5, you just leave them to it."*

**Why this matters (Scott to Pippa):** in B2C, logic isn't enough. *"Things can make perfect sense, they can have logical certainty, but if they don't have emotional certainty, they won't pay on the back end."* You only need **one** pain point, the biggest lever, the "North Star". **"Dig straight down, not around."**

## 4. Two corrections to the earlier files

Scott said two things in this call that change some advice in `full calls/` and calls 01–50. Use his version from now on:

1. **Don't over-talk before the question, and don't hand them the answers.**
   To Jemma: *"You don't get points for knowing what George is thinking. You want to force him to say it. We end up giving binary questions, or asking a question and then giving three or four answers so he can just pick."*
   → **Options ("is it X or Y?") are for stuck or very shy prospects only.** By default, ask open questions and let them talk. Keep the lead-in short.
2. **Don't ask permission at the open, and don't farm yeses.**
   *"We don't need to ask, are you free for a quick 5–10 minutes? We can jump straight into what made you fill out the form."* Use a familiar tone ("Hey George, how you doing, man?"), not the unsure "Hey… is this George?". State who you are and the context, then ask an open question. If they don't have time, they'll tell you.

## 5. Scott's 7-step setting framework

| # | Step | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening** | Familiar tone. Name, company, context, open question. "Hey Dean, how you doing, man? It's Stefan from Marco's team. You filled out an application about trading. What made you interested in that in the first place?" |
| 2 | **Soft qualification** | "Where are you based? What do you do for work?" Are they working or unemployed? New to the model, or already doing it? Students, unemployed and low earners: be transparent early. "Our program is a mid four-figure investment. Is that viable for you right now, or completely out of the question?" (A binary question here is on purpose: you either DQ or move on.) Doctors, lawyers, people already doing the model full-time: prioritise them. |
| 3 | **Current situation: SIT** | **S**ituation: what's going on? **I**mpact: how is that affecting you? **T**imeline: how long have you felt that way? *"Do you enjoy being a bin man?" "Nah, sucks." "Why do you say it sucks?" "Long hours on my feet." "How long have you felt that way?" "The last year."* |
| 4 | **Goals** | "If you got into X, what would be the main goal over the next 6–12 months?" Then the transformation behind the number: **location, time or financial freedom**, and why that one matters to *them*. Same SIT again: how long have they wanted it? |
| 5 | **Roadblocks** | What's stopped them doing it alone? It's always **Knowledge** (don't know what to do), **Guidance** (know what but not how, or in what order) or **Accountability** (know exactly what, just don't do it). Get them to *say* it, because that's the bridge to the offer. Then: **"What's different now?"** |
| 6 | **Urgency: temp check** | "On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it for you to [their goal]?" **7+:** "7's a strong number. What makes it that important to you?" then "What's holding it back from a 9 or 10?" (this uncovers fear and limiting beliefs). **6 or below:** "How come it's only a 6?" Then challenge, or DQ. |
| 7 | **Booking** | Assumptive tone, no rising "…?" at the end. Sell the call, not the product, and personalise it. *"Next step would be to set up a Zoom consultation with one of our head coaches, where they can break down how we could help you overcome [their problem] so you can [their goal]. How does that sound?"* |

**Disqualify to protect the closer's calendar.** There are three types: a **fit** DQ (they don't match what the program needs), a **financial** DQ (no money to invest) and an **interest** DQ (not serious). *"Your job isn't to book as many calls as possible. It's to book qualified ones."* It respects your time, the closer's time and the prospect's time.

**"No time" as a roadblock:** don't challenge identity. Ask "What's changed now for you to have time to make this happen?" If the answer is "nothing", go to urgency ("Is this actually a priority?"). Leave time-commitment challenges to the closer.

**"I've got no money" as a roadblock:** this is the smoothest way into financial qualification, because they brought it up. "When you say you haven't had money to invest, what budget have you got set aside now to actually get started?"

## 6. Vague answer → what to say

| They say | Don't | Do |
|---|---|---|
| "I just want to make more money." | "Why?" (and again, and again) | "How much are you making right now?" … "How much would you need for it to be worth the change?" … "What does that change for you?" |
| "Everyone wants more money, right?" | "Haha yeah." | "Not everyone, man. What's specifically going on in your life that's got you wanting more?" |
| "It's boring / repetitive." | "How so? How come? Examples?" | Reframe: "Honestly, [the new thing] is repetitive too. So knowing that, why make the change?" |
| "Just saw the ad." | "Did anything happen?" | "Fair. And how long have you felt [their word] about the job?" Then: "What's different now?" |
| "I don't know." | Answer it for them. | Silence, two seconds. Then: "If you had to guess?" |
| "I want freedom." | "Freedom's great!" | "Freedom from what?" / "What would that look like day to day?" |
| "I'm still young / I want to see the world." | Move on. | "What do you mean when you say you're still young?" |
| A contradiction ("travel more" + "same country") | Ignore it. | Curious, not catching them out: "Do you want to travel more, or stay where you are? Which is it?" |
| "I'll just settle, it's fine." | Argue. | "Is that what you're willing to settle for?" If yes: "How come you're on the call today, then?" If still yes, let them go. |
| One-word answers the whole call | Keep digging forever. | Temp check: "On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is this to you?" A low number means an interest DQ, and that's okay. |

## 7. The George call, run again with all of this

> **S:** Hey Stefan, how you doing, man? It's George from Just Do Sales. You reached out about getting into high-ticket sales. What made you interested in that in the first place?
> **P:** I saw a new space, online, making money from sales. I'm in sales already, but I want to make more, I guess.
> **S:** Where are you based, and what's the sales job?
> **P:** Moldova. Travel agent, I send rich people on nice holidays. Good base, some commission. It's kind of boring.
> **S:** Boring. Do you enjoy it at all?
> **P:** It's repetitive, to be honest. Same thing every day.
> **S:** I'll be honest with you, man, high-ticket sales is repetitive too. Eight hours of calls a day. The difference is you can do it from anywhere and scale what you make. So knowing that, why do you actually want to make a change?
> **P:** …I guess the money. And remote.
> **S:** What are you bringing in at the moment?
> **P:** About three grand.
> **S:** And if you made the switch, how much would you need to be making for it to be worth it?
> **P:** Five grand.
> **S:** What does five grand actually change for you?
> **P:** Replace my income, work remotely, travel more.
> **S:** Where would you go?
> **P:** Spain. Ibiza, Marbella.
> **S:** Ha, party spots. What's the draw?
> **P:** Have some fun. I'm still young.
> **S:** What do you mean when you say you're still young?
> **P:** I'm 23. I want to see the world before I'm stuck.
> **S:** Do you think you'll get to see the world anytime soon, staying in the travel agency?
> **P:** No. Ironic, right? I book everyone else's holidays.
> **S:** *(laughs)* Yeah, that is ironic. How long have you felt that way?
> **P:** Six months.
> **S:** And what's different now, compared to six months ago?
> **P:** …My friend moved to Lisbon last month. Works remote. He sends me photos every day. *(This is the real trigger. It only comes out once the money and travel thread is concrete.)*
> **S:** On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it to you to be out there before you're 25?
> **P:** 8.
> **S:** 8's strong. What's stopping it being a 10?
> **P:** I don't know if my English is good enough for calls.
> **S:** That makes sense. So it's less about knowing what to do, more about having someone guide you through it so you're confident on the phone. Fair?
> **P:** Yeah.
> **S:** Next step would be to set up a Zoom with one of our head coaches. They can break down how we'd help you get confident on calls, so you can replace that three grand remotely and actually be out in Spain before 25. How does that sound?

(Financial qualification goes in between, as usual. It's left out here so the thread is easy to follow.)
