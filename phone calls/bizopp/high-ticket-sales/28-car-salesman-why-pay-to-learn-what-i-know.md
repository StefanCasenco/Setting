# 28 · High-ticket sales · New lead · Car salesman, 10 years: "Why would I pay to learn what I already do?"

**Prospect:** Steve, 36, car sales at a Ford dealership, UK · **Lead:** application from an ad · **Length:** ~12 min · **Ends:** booked
**What happens:** This is the prospect Kevin complained about in your calls ("I have 10 years experience, why pay you?"). The setter agrees he can sell, and finds what he doesn't have.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Steve?

**[ST]:** Yep, Steve.

**[S]:** Hey Steve, it's Stefan from Luke's team. You applied about getting into remote high-ticket sales. Does that ring a bell?

**[ST]:** Yeah, yeah. Look, I'll save you the pitch, mate. I've sold cars for ten years. I know objection handling, I know rapport, I know closing. So what would you actually teach me?

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Honestly, Steve, probably not how to sell. You've done ten years on a forecourt, that's harder than most of what we do. I'm not going to teach you rapport, you'd laugh at me. So let me flip it. If you already know how to sell, what had you applying?

**[ST]:** *(pause)* …Good question. *(laughs)* The remote bit, mostly.

**[S]:** Remote. What's wrong with the forecourt?

**[ST]:** Saturdays. Every Saturday for ten years. And Sundays half the time. And standing outside in January waiting for someone to walk in, then they want to "think about it". You know what I mean.

**[S]:** I can imagine. What does a normal week look like?

**[ST]:** Five, six days. 9 till 7. Plus the drive. And it's commission, so if it's a bad month, it's a bad month. The market's dead at the moment, everyone's waiting for interest rates.

**[S]:** What's a bad month look like?

**[ST]:** Last month I did 1,900. Best month this year was 4,200.

**[S]:** That's a big swing.

**[ST]:** It's mental. You can't plan anything.

**[S]:** And at home, how does that land?

**[ST]:** My partner's a nurse, so she works weekends too, but different ones. We see each other properly like two days a month. *(laughs)* We text more than we talk.

**[S]:** Two days a month.

**[ST]:** Yeah. And she wants to start a family. And I'm like, with what time? We'd be passing the baby between shifts.

**[S]:** Yeah. That's a real thing to think about.

**[ST]:** It is. It's why I clicked, if I'm honest.

**[S]:** So basically, it's not about learning to sell. You can sell. It's about using that from home, with your weekends back, before you start a family. Fair?

**[ST]:** *(pause)* Yeah. That's fair.

**[S]:** And how long have you been looking at remote sales?

**[ST]:** Couple of months. I applied to a few closing jobs. Got nothing.

**[S]:** What happened with those?

**[ST]:** They want "high-ticket experience". Or they want a Loom video. I don't know what a Loom is. *(laughs)* And half of them are commission-only for offers I've never heard of.

**[S]:** Yeah, so it's not the selling. It's knowing how that world works, what offers are good, how to get in.

**[ST]:** Yeah. I'm a fish out of water with that bit.

**[S]:** Makes sense. Money side, quick, just so I know where you're at.

**[ST]:** Go on.

**[S]:** If remote closing worked out, what would you want to be making?

**[ST]:** If I could do 4k steady, every month, from home, I'd be happy. No swings.

**[S]:** And on average now?

**[ST]:** About 2,800 averaged out.

**[S]:** And are you able to save?

**[ST]:** Good months I save. Bad months I eat it. Maybe 300 a month on average.

**[S]:** And in total?

**[ST]:** About 6k.

**[S]:** And is a decision like this something you'd make with your partner?

**[ST]:** Yeah. She'd want to know.

**[S]:** And Steve, honestly, if there was a way to get into the right offers, without starting from zero, would you be open to investing time, energy and resources into it?

**[ST]:** If it's not a course telling me how to build rapport, yes. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* I'll make sure of it. Next step is a call with Callum, one of our coaches. He was in car sales too, actually, Audi. He'll skip the basics and go through how to actually land a good remote offer. Sound good?

**[ST]:** He was in cars? Alright, yeah.

**[S]:** When are you off?

**[ST]:** Wednesdays.

**[S]:** Callum's got Wednesday at 11 or 2. Which one?

**[ST]:** 11.

**[S]:** Wednesday at 11. Best email?

**[ST]:** *(gives it)*

**[S]:** Sent. And Steve, I'll send you a link to what a Loom is. *(laughs)*

**[ST]:** *(laughs)* Cheers, mate.

## What to notice

- **"What would you actually teach me?"** The setter agreed: "Probably not how to sell… you'd laugh at me." Then flipped it: "If you already know how to sell, what had you applying?" He can't argue with his own reason.
- **Respect experience out loud.** "Ten years on a forecourt, that's harder than most of what we do." The ego relaxes.
- **"What's a bad month look like?"** Specific numbers (1,900 vs 4,200) say more than "it's up and down".
- **"We text more than we talk."** That was the pain. A starting-a-family deadline was underneath.
- **"So it's not the selling. It's knowing how that world works."** This names the real gap, which is different from what he thought you'd sell him.
- **Matching the closer** (ex-Audi) for a prospect with an ego. Same move as the prop trader call.
