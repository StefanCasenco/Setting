# 30 · High-ticket sales · Old lead, 2 months · Single mum who doubts herself: "I'm not a sales person"

**Prospect:** Leanne, 33, teaching assistant, single mum of two, UK · **Lead:** applied in September, booked, cancelled · **Length:** ~12 min · **Ends:** booked

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Leanne?

**[L]:** Hiya.

**[S]:** Hey Leanne, it's Stefan from Luke's team. You applied in September about remote sales, and you were booked in, but I think it got cancelled. I just wanted to check in. Does that ring a bell?

**[L]:** Oh. Yeah. Sorry, I cancelled it. I just… I panicked a bit, to be honest.

**[S]:** No need to be sorry at all. What did you panic about, if you don't mind me asking?

**[L]:** I just thought, who am I kidding? I'm not a sales person. I'm a teaching assistant. I'm not confident like that.

**[S]:** That's really honest, thank you. Can I ask, what made you apply in the first place, before the panic kicked in?

**[L]:** Money, honestly. I'm on like 1,300 a month. I've got two boys, eight and five. Their dad helps a bit, but not much. And I just saw the ad saying you can do it from home and I thought, that would be amazing. School run, and work from home.

**[S]:** 1,300 with two boys. How does that work?

**[L]:** *(laughs)* It doesn't, really. I do a cleaning job on Saturdays as well. And I go to my mum's for dinner twice a week so we save on food. Don't tell anyone. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Your secret's safe. That sounds like a lot of juggling.

**[L]:** It is. And the boys want stuff, like, the eldest wants to do football camp and it's 120 quid and I had to say no. He was gutted.

**[S]:** That must be hard, saying no to him.

**[L]:** *(quiet)* It's the worst bit. Every time.

**[S]:** Yeah.

**[S]:** Leanne, can I say something about the "I'm not a sales person" thing?

**[L]:** Go on.

**[S]:** You work with five-year-olds all day, yeah?

**[L]:** Year 1, yeah.

**[S]:** How do you get a five-year-old to sit down and do phonics when they don't want to?

**[L]:** *(laughs)* You make them think it's their idea. Or you make it a game.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* That's sales, Leanne. Honestly. Getting someone to want to do something, without forcing them. Most of what good sales is, is listening and being kind. Which, from this call, you're good at.

**[L]:** *(pause)* …Huh. I never thought of it like that.

**[S]:** I'm not saying it's easy. It's a skill and you'd have to learn it. But "I'm not a sales person" isn't really true. You just haven't done it for money.

**[L]:** *(laughs)* Okay. Maybe.

**[S]:** So it's not really about being a natural salesperson. It's about being able to say yes to football camp, and doing the school run without the Saturday cleaning. Fair?

**[L]:** Yeah. That's it.

**[S]:** Okay, couple of quick money ones, just so I know where you're at. No judgement, I know it's tight.

**[L]:** Very tight. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** If remote sales worked, what would make a difference?

**[L]:** Honestly, 2,000 a month would be life-changing. I could drop the cleaning.

**[S]:** And are you able to save anything at the moment?

**[L]:** No. Honestly, no. Some months I'm in my overdraft.

**[S]:** Thank you for being honest. Have you got anything put away at all?

**[L]:** I've got about 1,800. That's from my tax refund. It's for emergencies.

**[S]:** Okay. And is credit something you've got?

**[L]:** I've got a card with about 2,000 on it. From last Christmas.

**[S]:** Okay. Leanne, I'll be straight with you, because I don't want you to panic again. Things like this are an investment, and money's very tight for you. So the next call is going to be honest about whether it's the right time. If it isn't, the coach will say so, and that's okay. Does that feel alright?

**[L]:** *(pause)* Yeah. That actually makes me feel better.

**[S]:** Good. It's with Amara, she's a mum too, she started when her daughter was small. She'll go through it all, including whether it makes sense right now. No pressure to decide anything. Sound good?

**[L]:** Yeah. Okay.

**[S]:** When are the boys in bed?

**[L]:** 7:30, if I'm lucky.

**[S]:** Amara's got Tuesday at 8 or Wednesday at 8. Which one?

**[L]:** Tuesday.

**[S]:** Tuesday at 8. Best email?

**[L]:** *(gives it)*

**[S]:** Sent. And Leanne, if you feel the panic coming before Tuesday, text me first, before you cancel. Deal?

**[L]:** *(laughs)* Deal.

## What to notice

- **"What did you panic about, if you don't mind me asking?"** Ask about the cancellation gently. It's usually the real objection.
- **"I'm not a sales person"** was handled with *her own skill.* "How do you get a five-year-old to do phonics?" She answered with sales, without knowing it. A story beats an argument.
- **"I'm not saying it's easy."** Don't oversell after a reframe. It keeps it honest.
- **"Don't tell anyone" (dinner at her mum's).** "Your secret's safe." Protecting her pride keeps her talking.
- **Low money: be honest before the closer.** "The next call is going to be honest about whether it's the right time." That's right for her, and it's what made her feel safe enough to book.
- **"Text me first, before you cancel."** Name the pattern (panic, then cancel) with a laugh. It gives her a step before running.
