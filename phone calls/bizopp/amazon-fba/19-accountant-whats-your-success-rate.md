# 19 · Amazon FBA · New lead · Analytical accountant · "What's your success rate?"

**Prospect:** Wei, 39, accountant at a mid-size firm, Canada · **Lead:** application this morning · **Length:** ~13 min · **Ends:** booked
**What happens:** He asks for data, speaks flatly, and doesn't show feelings. The setter respects the logic and finds the feeling through numbers.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Wei?

**[W]:** Yes. Speaking.

**[S]:** Hey Wei, it's Stefan from Adam's team. You filled out an application this morning about Amazon FBA. Does that ring a bell?

**[W]:** Yes. Before we begin, what is your success rate? What percentage of students reach profitability?

**[S]:** Good question, and honestly, the kind I'd ask too. I don't have the official numbers in front of me, and I'm not going to guess them, because you'd catch me. *(laughs)* The coach on the next call can give you real data. My job is to see in ten minutes if that call even makes sense for you. Fair?

**[W]:** *(pause)* That's reasonable.

**[S]:** So it's ten minutes. What had you looking at FBA?

**[W]:** I've done the analysis. Private label FBA has better margins than dropshipping, more control than affiliate, and it's an asset that can be sold. Brands sell for three to four times annual profit.

**[S]:** You've done your homework. *(laughs)* And besides the analysis, what's the reason you want an asset like that?

**[W]:** Diversification. My income is 100% dependent on one employer.

**[S]:** Okay. And what's wrong with that, for you specifically?

**[W]:** *(pause)* It's risky.

**[S]:** Risky how? Has something happened?

**[W]:** …Our firm was acquired in the spring. By a larger firm. They've already let go of eleven people in my department. I'm still there. For now.

**[S]:** Eleven people. And you're waiting to see if you're twelve.

**[W]:** *(pause)* Yes. That's accurate.

**[S]:** How long have you been there?

**[W]:** Fourteen years.

**[S]:** Fourteen years, and it could be one email. How's that been, living with that since spring?

**[W]:** *(long pause)* I don't sleep well. I check my email at 6am to see if there's a meeting invite from HR. My wife has noticed.

**[S]:** What has she noticed?

**[W]:** That I'm… distracted. She said I'm not present. That I'm doing numbers in my head at dinner.

**[S]:** Doing numbers in your head at dinner. What numbers?

**[W]:** *(slight laugh)* How many months we'd last. Mortgage, two children, my mother lives with us.

**[S]:** And what's the answer, when you do the numbers?

**[W]:** Eight months. Maybe nine.

**[S]:** Yeah. That would keep anyone up.

**[S]:** So it's not really about FBA being a good model. It's about not having your family's whole life depend on one email from HR. Fair?

**[W]:** *(pause)* Yes. That's a better summary than mine.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* And how long have you been researching FBA?

**[W]:** Four months. I've built a spreadsheet of 340 potential products.

**[S]:** 340. *(laughs)* That's more than most people who've launched. Have you ordered anything?

**[W]:** No.

**[S]:** And what's stopped you, with 340 products picked?

**[W]:** I don't know which one is right. Every product has risk. I keep adding columns.

**[S]:** Keep adding columns. *(laughs)* That's a very accountant problem.

**[W]:** *(laughs)* Yes. My wife says the same.

**[S]:** Okay. Money side, quick, so I know what's realistic. And I know you like numbers, so this part should be easy. *(laughs)*

**[W]:** Go ahead.

**[S]:** Twelve months from now, what monthly profit would make you feel safe, independent of the firm?

**[W]:** $4,000 a month profit. That covers the mortgage and essentials.

**[S]:** And current take-home, roughly?

**[W]:** $7,800 a month.

**[S]:** And monthly savings, on a usual month?

**[W]:** $1,500.

**[S]:** And total savings, outside retirement accounts?

**[W]:** $55,000. Of which I'd consider $20,000 to $25,000 risk capital.

**[S]:** That's very clear, thank you. *(laughs)* And would you make the decision with your wife?

**[W]:** Yes. Jointly.

**[S]:** And if the model and the numbers made sense, would you both be open to investing time, energy and resources?

**[W]:** If the data supports it. Yes.

**[S]:** Okay, Wei. Next step is a call with Daniel, one of our coaches. He's a former financial analyst, actually. He'll go through your spreadsheet, help you narrow down the 340, and give you the real data on our results you asked for at the start. And your wife's welcome, since she's the one watching you do numbers at dinner. Sound good?

**[W]:** Yes. That's acceptable. *(pause)* Good, actually.

**[S]:** When suits you both?

**[W]:** Saturday morning. My mother takes the children to Chinese school.

**[S]:** Daniel's got Saturday at 10 or 11:30. Which one?

**[W]:** 10.

**[S]:** Saturday at 10. Best email? Personal, not work. *(laughs)*

**[W]:** *(slight laugh)* Yes. Definitely personal. *(gives it)*

**[S]:** Sent. Accept it for me? And bring the spreadsheet.

**[W]:** Accepted. I will.

**[S]:** Perfect. And Wei, try not to check the email at 6am tomorrow. *(laughs)*

**[W]:** *(pause)* I'll try. Thank you, Stefan.

## What to notice

- **Analytical prospects test you with data questions first.** "The kind I'd ask too… I'm not going to guess, because you'd catch me." Honest, with a little humour. He respects that.
- **His "logic" answer hides the feeling. Ask "what's wrong with that, for you specifically?"** A logical person will give you the real reason if you ask for specifics, not feelings.
- **"Risky how? Has something happened?"** It assumes there's a story. There was.
- **Use his language: numbers.** "Doing numbers in your head at dinner. What numbers?" … "Eight months." That's his pain, said as a number. That's how analytical people say how they feel.
- **"Keep adding columns. That's a very accountant problem."** A gentle joke that names his blocker without judging it.
- **Match the closer** ("former financial analyst") and promise him the data he asked for at the start. Close the loop.
