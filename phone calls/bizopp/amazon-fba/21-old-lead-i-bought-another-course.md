# 21 · Amazon FBA · Old lead, 3 months · "I already bought another course"

**Prospect:** Hannah, 29, marketing coordinator, UK · **Lead:** applied in August, booked, no-showed, went quiet · **Length:** ~10 min · **Ends:** booked
**What happens:** She bought a £297 course elsewhere and feels a bit defensive about it. The setter makes it okay and finds out where she's stuck.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Hannah?

**[H]:** Yes?

**[S]:** Hey Hannah, it's Stefan from Adam's team. You applied back in August about Amazon FBA. I'm just checking in to see where you ended up. Does that ring a bell?

**[H]:** Oh. Yes. Um, I actually bought a different course. Sorry.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Don't be sorry, that's great, you actually did something. Most people who apply just keep scrolling. Which one did you go with?

**[H]:** It's a £297 one. On Udemy-ish, like a smaller creator.

**[S]:** Nice. How's it going?

**[H]:** *(pause)* Good. Well. It's okay. I've done the modules.

**[S]:** All of them? That's more than most. And where are you at with it now? Have you picked a product?

**[H]:** I've got a shortlist. Three products. But I'm kind of stuck on which one.

**[S]:** Stuck how?

**[H]:** Like, the course says "validate with data", and I've done that, but they all look kind of the same. And I don't want to put £5,000 into stock and pick the wrong one.

**[S]:** £5,000 of stock on a guess. Yeah, that's scary.

**[H]:** It is. And there's no one to ask. The course has a Facebook group but it's like, everyone's posting "first sale!!" and I'm just sitting there.

**[S]:** Just sitting there. How long have you been at that point?

**[H]:** Like six weeks.

**[S]:** Six weeks on three products. And honestly, Hannah, I'm not here to slag off the course you bought. If you're happy, crack on. But can I ask, what was the reason you wanted to do FBA in the first place, back in August?

**[H]:** *(sighs)* Honestly, I'm a marketing coordinator, and I'm basically running campaigns for products for a company, and I'm good at it, and I'm on 28k. And my manager's on 55 and he does less than me. And I just thought, if I'm good at selling products, why am I doing it for someone else?

**[S]:** "Why am I doing it for someone else."

**[H]:** Yeah. And London's so expensive, I'm 29, I'm in a flat share with three people. I want my own place.

**[S]:** Three flatmates at 29. How's that?

**[H]:** *(laughs)* Someone keeps eating my yoghurts. *(pause)* No, honestly, it's fine, but I feel like I should be further. My sister's got a house and a baby and I've got a shelf in a fridge.

**[S]:** A shelf in a fridge. *(laughs)* Oof.

**[H]:** *(laughs)* Yeah.

**[S]:** So it's not about FBA being fun. It's about using what you're already good at for yourself, so you can stop feeling behind. Fair?

**[H]:** Yeah. That's fair.

**[S]:** And the six weeks stuck, what's that been like?

**[H]:** Frustrating. Like I've spent £297 and I've learned a lot, but I'm not actually any closer.

**[S]:** Yeah. And look, the course gave you the knowledge. It sounds like what's missing is someone to look at your three products and say, "this one, here's why." Is that fair?

**[H]:** *(pause)* Yeah. Literally that.

**[S]:** Okay. Couple of quick money ones, just so I know where you're at.

**[H]:** Okay.

**[S]:** What would FBA need to make a month for you to get your own place?

**[H]:** Like an extra £1,500.

**[S]:** And take-home now?

**[H]:** About £1,900.

**[S]:** And with London rent, are you able to save much?

**[H]:** About £300 a month.

**[S]:** And in total, saved up for the stock?

**[H]:** About £7,000. I've been saving for it.

**[S]:** That's solid. And is credit something you've got?

**[H]:** Yes, but I'd rather not use it.

**[S]:** Fair. And if there was a way to get unstuck, with someone who actually looks at your products, would you be open to investing time, energy and resources into that?

**[H]:** Yes. As long as it's not repeating the course I've done.

**[S]:** Totally fair, I wouldn't want you to pay for the same thing twice. So next step is a call with Priya, one of our coaches. She'll look at your three products, tell you what she'd do, and if what we offer is just the same as your course, she'll say so. Sound fair?

**[H]:** Yes, that's fair.

**[S]:** When are you free, after work?

**[H]:** After 6:30.

**[S]:** Priya's got Wednesday at 7 or Thursday at 6:30. Which one?

**[H]:** Wednesday.

**[S]:** Wednesday at 7. Best email?

**[H]:** *(gives it)*

**[S]:** Sent. Accept it? And this time, if anything comes up, just text me. *(laughs)*

**[H]:** *(laughs)* I will, I promise. Sorry about August.

**[S]:** Honestly, don't worry about it. Bring your three products. And label your yoghurts. *(laughs)*

**[H]:** *(laughs)* Thanks, Stefan.

## What to notice

- **"I bought a different course, sorry."** Praise it: "that's great, you actually did something." A prospect who feels judged for buying elsewhere hangs up.
- **"How's it going?" then "Where are you at with it now?"** No attack on the other course, just questions. She found the gap herself (stuck for six weeks).
- **"I'm not here to slag off the course you bought. If you're happy, crack on."** It takes the frame back by respecting her choice, then goes to the original why.
- **"A shelf in a fridge."** Repeat the image. A real phrase like that is worth more than any recap.
- **Name the gap in one line.** "The course gave you the knowledge. What's missing is someone to look at your three and say 'this one'." She said, "literally that."
- **"I wouldn't want you to pay for the same thing twice."** This handles her worry before the closer has to.
