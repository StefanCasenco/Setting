# 23 · Amazon FBA · New lead · 58-year-old wants to use his 401(k)

**Prospect:** Frank, 58, regional sales manager, US · **Lead:** application from a Facebook ad · **Length:** ~12 min · **Ends:** booked, with a clear "not the retirement money" line
**What happens:** He's keen and offers his retirement money himself. The setter slows him down. That protects him and builds trust.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Frank?

**[F]:** This is Frank!

**[S]:** Hey Frank, it's Stefan from Adam's team. You filled out an application about Amazon FBA. Does that ring a bell?

**[F]:** Sure does! I've been waiting for your call, son. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Well, here I am. So, quick ten minutes, just to see where you're at and if we can help. Sound fair?

**[F]:** Shoot.

**[S]:** So what had you looking at Amazon?

**[F]:** Well, I'm 58, I've been in sales thirty-four years. Industrial supplies. And they just told us they're "restructuring the regions". Which means they're gonna get rid of the old guys and hire kids at half the salary. I've seen it before. And I thought, Frank, you've sold stuff your whole life, why not sell your own stuff?

**[S]:** Thirty-four years. And they're restructuring around you.

**[F]:** Around me, yep. I've got maybe six months, I'd say.

**[S]:** How's that sitting with you?

**[F]:** *(pause)* You know, I put a brave face on. But it stings. I gave them thirty-four years. Missed a lot of my kids' stuff for that company. Hotels, airports. And now it's "thanks, Frank, here's your cake."

**[S]:** "Here's your cake." What did you miss, with the kids?

**[F]:** Oh, plenty. Baseball games. My daughter's prom, I was in Ohio. *(pause)* She's 30 now. She's got a little one. I don't want to miss my grandkid's stuff too.

**[S]:** Yeah. That makes sense.

**[S]:** So it's not just about replacing the salary. It's about having something that's yours, so you're not missing it all again with your grandkid. Fair?

**[F]:** That's it exactly. You're good, son.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* And have you done anything with Amazon yet?

**[F]:** Watched about a hundred hours of YouTube. *(laughs)* My wife says I'm obsessed. I've got some ideas. Industrial stuff, actually, like shop organisers. I know that market.

**[S]:** That's a real advantage, knowing a market from the inside.

**[F]:** That's what I figured.

**[S]:** Okay, Frank, a couple of money questions, just so I know what's realistic.

**[F]:** Sure. And look, I'll make it easy for you. I've got about $400,000 in my 401(k). I can pull from that. Money's not a problem.

**[S]:** *(pause)* Frank, I appreciate you being so open. Honestly, though, I'm going to stop you right there, because I'd say this to my own dad. Retirement money should stay retirement money. You're 58. If anything went wrong, there's no time to rebuild it. I wouldn't want you touching that for this. Is that okay?

**[F]:** *(pause)* Huh. Well. That's not what I expected you to say.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* I know. But I'd rather you trust me than have you regret it.

**[F]:** *(laughs)* Okay. I respect that. My wife'll love you.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* So outside of the 401(k), have you got savings, regular savings you could use?

**[F]:** Sure. We've got about $60,000 in a savings account. Emergency money and some extra.

**[S]:** And what's coming in each month right now?

**[F]:** About $8,500 after tax. My wife's retired, she was a teacher, so she's got her pension.

**[S]:** And are you able to put money aside each month?

**[F]:** About $2,000.

**[S]:** And if Amazon worked out, a year from now, what would you want it making?

**[F]:** If it made $4,000 a month, with my wife's pension, we'd be fine when they let me go. Could even retire a little early.

**[S]:** And would this be a decision with your wife?

**[F]:** Oh, absolutely. Forty years married. She'll want to look your guy in the eye. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Good. And if there was a way that fit, using savings, not retirement, would you both be open to investing time, energy and resources into it?

**[F]:** Yes. Absolutely.

**[S]:** Okay, Frank. The next step is a call with Steve, he's one of our coaches. He'll go through the shop organiser idea, your experience, and what's realistic with six months' runway. And your wife on the call, so she can look him in the eye. *(laughs)* And Frank, I'm going to put a note for him: retirement money stays retirement money. Sound good?

**[F]:** Sounds real good, son. You're alright.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* When are you both home?

**[F]:** I'm on the road Monday to Wednesday. Thursday or Friday.

**[S]:** Steve's got Thursday at 5 or Friday at 10. Which one?

**[F]:** Friday at 10. I'll work from home.

**[S]:** Friday at 10. Best email?

**[F]:** *(gives it)*

**[S]:** Sent. Can you check it on your phone and accept?

**[F]:** Give me a sec, I'm not great with these things… okay, there. Accepted!

## What to notice

- **When someone offers retirement money, stop them.** "I'd say this to my own dad. Retirement money should stay retirement money." It's the right thing to do, and it's the biggest trust-builder on the call. He said, "That's not what I expected you to say."
- **Put it in the notes for the closer.** "I'm going to put a note for him." The prospect knows you meant it.
- **"Here's your cake."** Repeat the bitter line, then ask what it cost him. Older prospects' pain is often about the years, not the money.
- **"That's a real advantage, knowing a market from the inside."** Turn his age and experience into a strength. Older leads often feel behind.
- **Small help at booking** ("check it on your phone"). Give him time. Don't rush older prospects through the email step.
