# 08 · Trading · Old lead, 5 months · Angry: "Stop calling me"

**Prospect:** Darren, 41, bus driver, UK · **Lead:** opted in for a free masterclass in June. Other companies have been spamming him since. · **Length:** ~10 min · **Ends:** booked
**What happens:** He opens angry. The setter takes the heat, doesn't defend, gives him the exit, and he stays.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Darren?

**[D]:** Who is this? If this is another trading company, I swear to God—

**[S]:** *(calm)* It is, I'm not gonna lie to you. It's Stefan from Ray's team. You signed up for a masterclass in June. But, Darren, if you want me to take you off, I'll do it right now, no problem.

**[D]:** I've had like twenty calls this month! Twenty! Some bloke rang me at 9pm on a Sunday. How'd you lot even get my number?

**[S]:** Twenty, that's mad, I'd be fuming too. Honestly, I've no idea who the others are. You put your number in on Ray's page in June, that's how I've got it. And I'm not gonna sell your number to anyone. But if you want off the list, say the word and you're off. I mean it.

**[D]:** *(pause, breathing)* …It's not you, mate. It's just. I signed up for one thing and now I'm on everyone's list.

**[S]:** Yeah, that's rubbish. Sorry you've had that.

**[D]:** *(sighs)* What did you even want?

**[S]:** Honestly, just a quick five minutes to see if you're still interested in trading at all. If not, I'll take you off and you won't hear from us again. If you are, I'll see if we can actually help. Fair?

**[D]:** …Go on then. Five minutes.

**[S]:** Five minutes. So back in June, what had you signing up for the masterclass?

**[D]:** I dunno. I was on a break, scrolling. It looked alright.

**[S]:** Fair. And are you still looking into trading, or has it gone on the back burner?

**[D]:** Back burner. I've got too much on.

**[S]:** Too much on how?

**[D]:** I drive buses. Split shifts. I'm out at 5:30 in the morning, back at 9 at night, with three hours in the middle where I can't really do anything. It's a joke.

**[S]:** Split shifts, that sounds brutal. Three hours in the middle where you can't go home?

**[D]:** I can, but by the time I'm home I've got to come back. So I sit in the canteen. Every day.

**[S]:** And how long have you been doing that?

**[D]:** Twelve years.

**[S]:** Twelve years of canteen in the middle. How's that been on you?

**[D]:** *(pause)* Honestly, it's not great. I've got two lads, eleven and nine, and I see them for like twenty minutes in the morning when they're half asleep. That's it, weekdays.

**[S]:** Twenty minutes.

**[D]:** And the eldest has started football. Saturday mornings. And I'm on Saturdays every other week. So I miss half of it.

**[S]:** Yeah. That's hard.

**[D]:** It's the job, innit.

**[S]:** So when you saw the masterclass in June, was it about that? Something that could give you some of that time back?

**[D]:** *(pause)* …Yeah. I suppose it was. I didn't think of it like that.

**[S]:** And have you tried any trading since then, or nothing?

**[D]:** Nothing. I downloaded an app on my break once. Didn't know what I was looking at.

**[S]:** Yeah, most people say that. So, can I ask a couple of quick money ones, just to see if it'd even make sense? No pressure, if it doesn't, it doesn't.

**[D]:** …Go on.

**[S]:** If trading could do something for you, what would it need to bring in a month so you could drop to straight shifts?

**[D]:** If I went to straight shifts I'd lose about four hundred. So four hundred.

**[S]:** So you'd basically need four hundred to get your evenings back. That's not crazy. And what are you on now, roughly?

**[D]:** About two-five after tax. Wife does part-time.

**[S]:** And are you able to save much, with two lads?

**[D]:** Hundred, maybe. Football boots ain't cheap. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Football boots are a scam, man. And have you got anything put away?

**[D]:** About four grand. That's for emergencies.

**[S]:** Yeah, of course. And if it was something that actually fit, would you and your wife be open to investing in it, time, energy and resources?

**[D]:** We'd have to talk. But… yeah. If it got me to his football.

**[S]:** Okay. Darren, here's what I'd suggest. One call with Pete, he's one of the coaches. No pressure, he'll look at whether trading even fits around split shifts, honestly, and what it would take to get that four hundred. Your wife on the call too, so you're deciding together. And if it's not for you, I'll take you off our list completely, and you'll never hear from us again. Fair?

**[D]:** *(pause)* …Yeah. That's fair.

**[S]:** When are you both home?

**[D]:** I'm off Sunday. This Sunday.

**[S]:** Pete's got Sunday at 11 or 3. Which one?

**[D]:** 3. After the football.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* After the football, perfect. Best email?

**[D]:** *(gives it)*

**[S]:** Sent. And Darren, sorry again about the other calls. That's not on.

**[D]:** Not your fault, mate. Cheers.

## What to notice

- **Never defend against anger.** "It is, I'm not gonna lie to you" and "I'd be fuming too" take the heat out. Defending ("but we're different!") adds heat.
- **Offer the exit early and mean it, twice.** "If you want off, say the word and you're off. I mean it." People stay when they feel free to leave.
- **"It's not you, mate."** That's the turn. Wait for it. Don't rush into questions before the anger has dropped.
- **Lower the ask.** "Five minutes." Then actually keep to it, or at least ask the important questions inside it.
- **"Was it about that? Something that could give you some of that time back?"** Linking his old opt-in to the pain he just told you. He hadn't connected them himself.
- **"Football boots are a scam, man."** A tiny shared joke in the money section, after a very tense start. It shows you're on his side.
