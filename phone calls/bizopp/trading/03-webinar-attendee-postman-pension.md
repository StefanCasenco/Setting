# 03 · Trading · Webinar attendee · Postman, 52, worried about his pension

**Prospect:** Gary, 52, postman, UK · **Lead:** attended the full webinar last night · **Length:** ~15 min · **Ends:** booked with his wife
**What happens:** A slow, careful talker who goes off on tangents (his knees, the Royal Mail changes, his son). The setter lets him talk and guides him back gently.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Gary?

**[G]:** Yes, Gary speaking.

**[S]:** Hi Gary, it's Stefan from Ray's team. You were on Ray's webinar last night, about trading. Does that ring a bell?

**[G]:** Oh, yes, yes. I watched the whole thing. Took notes, actually. My wife thought I'd gone mad. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Notes, I love that. Most people catch the middle bit and leave. So just so you know, Gary, it's a quick chat, ten minutes or so. I want to see where you're at, and if we can actually help. If we can, I'll explain the next step. If not, no problem at all. Is that alright?

**[G]:** Yes, that's fine. I'm on my lunch.

**[S]:** Perfect. So, you took notes. What stood out the most?

**[G]:** Well, the bit about risk, actually. He said most people lose because they risk too much on one trade. And I thought, well, that's sensible. Because, you see, I've always been careful with money. My father was a miner, and we didn't have anything, so I've always been… careful. I've been at Royal Mail thirty-one years. Thirty-one years. Started when I was twenty-one. And you know, things have changed a lot. They've changed the rounds, they've changed the hours, they've brought in all the parcel stuff, and it's not the job it was. My knees are shot. I've had one operation already. And now they're talking about changes to the pension, and I thought, well, Gary, you can't just sit and wait for it.

**[S]:** Thirty-one years, Gary. That's a career. And when you say the pension changes, what's worrying you most about it?

**[G]:** Well, that it won't be what they promised. I've worked it out. If I go at sixty, with the changes, I'll get… not a lot. Not what I planned for. And my wife's only part-time. And our son's still at home, he's twenty-four, he's… well, he's struggling a bit. Work-wise. So.

**[S]:** So it's a lot on your shoulders.

**[G]:** It is. It is. I don't talk about it much. *(pause)* Sorry, I'm going on.

**[S]:** Not at all, Gary, that's exactly what I need to hear. And the knees, are you still doing the full round?

**[G]:** Still doing it. Eleven miles a day, some days. The operation helped, but I know it won't last forever. The physio said, Gary, you've got maybe five, six years in those knees doing this.

**[S]:** Five or six years. And how does that sit with you, hearing that?

**[G]:** Well… it frightens me, if I'm honest. Because what else would I do? I've only ever been a postman.

**[S]:** Yeah. That's a real thing to sit with.

**[S]:** So if I'm hearing you right, it's not about getting rich. It's about having something for you and your wife that doesn't depend on the pension or the knees. Is that fair?

**[G]:** That's exactly it. That's exactly what I said to Linda.

**[S]:** And how long have you been looking at trading?

**[G]:** Only since the summer. My nephew does it. He showed me on his phone. I thought it was gambling, to be honest. But he explained it, and the webinar explained it, and it's not, is it? Not if you do it properly.

**[S]:** Not if you do it properly, no. And have you tried any of it yourself yet, or has it been more learning so far?

**[G]:** Oh, no, no. I haven't put a penny in. I wouldn't, not until I know what I'm doing.

**[S]:** Honestly, that's smarter than most people I speak to. And what's made you look into it now, rather than, say, in a couple of years?

**[G]:** The pension letter. It came in September. And I thought, well, Gary, if you start at sixty it's too late.

**[S]:** Yeah. That makes sense.

**[S]:** Gary, can I ask you a few questions about the money side? Just so I know what would actually be sensible for you. And I want to say up front, the last thing I'd want is anyone touching their pension for this. That's not what this is.

**[G]:** Oh, good. Because I wouldn't.

**[S]:** Good. So, a few years from now, when the knees are done, what would you want trading to be bringing in, to feel safe?

**[G]:** Oh, I don't know. Something to top up the pension. Five hundred, a thousand a month? I'm not greedy.

**[S]:** Not greedy at all. And just so I understand where you're at now, what's coming in for the house, roughly, with you and Linda?

**[G]:** Me, about two-two after tax. Linda, about nine hundred.

**[S]:** And with prices how they are now, and your son at home, are you able to put much aside each month?

**[G]:** About three hundred. We're careful.

**[S]:** And outside the pension, have you got savings put away?

**[G]:** Well… we've got about fifteen thousand. That's our rainy day. Linda's very firm about that.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Good on Linda. And is that something you'd decide together, if anything like this came up?

**[G]:** Oh, absolutely. Thirty years married, we decide everything together.

**[S]:** Thirty years, that's lovely. So, Gary, here's what I'd suggest. The next step is a call with Steve, he's one of our coaches. He's actually a bit older himself, he started in his fifties. He'll go through it all properly, what's realistic, what isn't, and what it would actually look like for someone in your position. And I'd really suggest Linda's on the call with you, so she hears it all directly and can ask her own questions. How does that sound?

**[G]:** That sounds very good. She'll have questions, believe me.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Good, that's what the call's for. When are you both home?

**[G]:** Evenings. After six. Or Saturdays.

**[S]:** Steve's got Thursday at 6:30 or Saturday at 10. Which is better?

**[G]:** Saturday at 10. Linda's not working Saturday.

**[S]:** Saturday at 10. What's the best email, Gary?

**[G]:** *(gives it slowly, spells it)*

**[S]:** Lovely. I've just sent it. Do you know how to check it on your phone, or would it be easier if I texted it to you as well?

**[G]:** Text it as well, please. I'm not the best with the emails. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** No problem at all, I'll do both. And Saturday at 10, nothing that might come up? No grandkids, no football?

**[G]:** *(laughs)* No grandkids yet. No, we'll be in.

**[S]:** Perfect. If anything changes, just text me back on this number. Have your notes from the webinar with you, Steve will love that. Enjoy the rest of your lunch, Gary.

**[G]:** Thank you, Stefan. You've been very kind.

## What to notice

- **Older prospects talk slower and wander. Let them.** "Not at all, Gary, that's exactly what I need to hear" when he apologised for going on. If you cut them off, they close up.
- **Pick one thread from the ramble.** Out of the father, Royal Mail, the knees, the pension and his son, the setter picked the pension (the fear) and the knees (the clock).
- **"Five or six years. How does that sit with you, hearing that?"** Repeat the number back, then ask. It's simple English and it works.
- **The money section was set up with safety first.** "The last thing I'd want is anyone touching their pension for this." That removes the biggest fear before the questions, so he answered all of them.
- **"Good on Linda"** is a small laugh that respects his wife's role, and it led straight to "do you decide together?". Then she goes on the call.
- **Practical help at the booking** ("text it as well?"). Older leads no-show when they can't find the email.
