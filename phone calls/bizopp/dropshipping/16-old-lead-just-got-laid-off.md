# 16 · Dropshipping · Old lead, 3 months · "I just got laid off"

**Prospect:** Ben, 34, graphic designer at a marketing agency, US · **Lead:** downloaded the "first product" guide in August, never booked · **Length:** ~12 min · **Ends:** booked
**What happens:** He's raw and a bit panicky. The setter slows things down and makes sure he doesn't decide from fear.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Ben?

**[B]:** Yeah, hey.

**[S]:** Hey Ben, it's Stefan from Nate's team. This is a bit of a random one. Back in August you grabbed our free guide on finding your first product. I'm just checking in on people who never got a call. Does that ring a bell?

**[B]:** Oh, man. *(laughs)* Funny timing.

**[S]:** Funny how?

**[B]:** I got laid off on Friday.

**[S]:** Oh, man. I'm sorry. That's rough.

**[B]:** Yeah. The whole design team. They're "using AI for assets now". *(laughs)* Six years.

**[S]:** Six years, and then "we're using AI now". How are you doing with it?

**[B]:** Honestly? I don't know. I'm kind of in shock. I've been on Indeed all weekend. There's like nothing. And everything that's there is half what I was making.

**[S]:** That sounds scary. Is it just you, or is there family relying on it?

**[B]:** My wife works, thank God. But we have a mortgage. And she's pregnant. Second one.

**[S]:** Okay. That's a lot all at once.

**[B]:** It's a lot. *(pause)* Sorry, I don't know why I'm telling you all this.

**[S]:** Don't apologise, man, honestly. It's been three days. Anyone would be like this.

**[B]:** Yeah.

**[S]:** Look, I'll be honest with you. The last thing I want is for you to jump into anything out of panic. That's never a good decision. So let's just talk for a few minutes, see where you're at, and if it makes sense, great. If not, I'll tell you. Cool?

**[B]:** Yeah. Cool. Thanks.

**[S]:** So back in August, before all this, what had you grabbing the guide?

**[B]:** I think I knew it was coming, honestly. They'd already cut two people. And I thought, I'm a designer, I can make a store look good, maybe I can sell something.

**[S]:** That's a real advantage. Most people's stores look terrible. *(laughs)*

**[B]:** *(laughs)* They really do. I look at them and I'm like, how does anyone buy from this?

**[S]:** Ha. And did you do anything with it after August?

**[B]:** I made a store. Like, I designed it. Home decor stuff. But I never launched it, because work got busy and I thought, I've got a job, it's fine.

**[S]:** "I've got a job, it's fine."

**[B]:** *(laughs)* Yeah. Famous last words.

**[S]:** So the store's just sitting there, built.

**[B]:** Built. Never launched.

**[S]:** And what stopped you launching it, other than work?

**[B]:** The ads part. I know design. I don't know ads, or how to find a product that actually sells. That's the bit that scares me.

**[S]:** Makes sense. Okay, let me ask a couple of money questions, just so I know what's realistic right now. And honestly, Ben, especially right now, I'm not going to push you into anything that puts the mortgage at risk. Okay?

**[B]:** Okay. I appreciate that.

**[S]:** If the store could do something, what would it need to make to take the pressure off?

**[B]:** If it made like $3,000 a month, plus my wife's income, we'd be okay.

**[S]:** And what were you making at the agency?

**[B]:** About $5,200 a month after tax.

**[S]:** Did you get any severance?

**[B]:** Three months. So I've got till February, basically.

**[S]:** Okay, so there's a bit of runway. That's good. And savings, outside of that?

**[B]:** About $18,000. That's our emergency fund. I guess this is the emergency. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Yeah, I guess it is. And would you make a decision like this with your wife?

**[B]:** Definitely. She's… she's more scared than me, honestly.

**[S]:** That makes sense. And if there was a way that used your design skills and taught you the part you don't know, would you both be open to investing time, energy and resources into it? Alongside job hunting, not instead of.

**[B]:** Yeah. Alongside, yeah. That makes sense.

**[S]:** Okay. So the next step is a call with Kyle, he's one of our coaches. He'll look at the store you've already built, and tell you honestly if it's worth launching, and what it'd take with your runway till February. And your wife should be on it, because it's both of your money and she's scared. She should hear it straight. And Ben, if he says "keep job hunting, don't do this," that's an answer too. Sound fair?

**[B]:** Yeah. That's really fair. Thank you.

**[S]:** When are you both home? I'm guessing your days are free now. *(laughs gently)*

**[B]:** *(laughs)* Yeah, very free. She's home after 5:30.

**[S]:** Kyle's got Wednesday at 6 or Thursday at 6:30. Which one?

**[B]:** Wednesday.

**[S]:** Wednesday at 6. Best email?

**[B]:** My personal one, obviously. *(laughs)* *(gives it)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Yeah, not the work one. Sent. Accept it for me?

**[B]:** Done.

**[S]:** Perfect. Have the store open on the call. And Ben, take the rest of the week easy if you can. Three days in, you're allowed to be in shock.

**[B]:** Thanks, man. Really.

## What to notice

- **"Funny timing" means something happened. Ask.** "Funny how?" opened the whole call.
- **Slow down for a raw prospect.** "It's been three days. Anyone would be like this." Then a clear frame: "The last thing I want is for you to jump into anything out of panic." That protects him, and it protects the business from a refund later.
- **"That's a real advantage. Most people's stores look terrible."** Turning his old job into a strength when he's feeling worthless.
- **"I've got a job, it's fine."** Repeating his own words back. He laughed: "famous last words". He sees the lesson himself.
- **"Alongside job hunting, not instead of."** That takes the pressure off, so it's easier to say yes.
- **"If he says don't do this, that's an answer too."** On a fragile call, permission to say no is what makes him show up.
