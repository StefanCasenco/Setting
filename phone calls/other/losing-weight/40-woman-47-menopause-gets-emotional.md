# 40 · Losing weight · New lead · Woman, 47, menopause, cries on the call

**Prospect:** Karen, 47, office manager, UK · **Lead:** application from a Facebook ad for women over 40 · **Length:** ~13 min · **Ends:** booked

> **Weight loss calls carry a lot of shame.** Go slower than on bizopp calls. Never comment on their body. Never promise a number of kilos. If health comes up (menopause, medication, diabetes), it's for their doctor and the coach. Your job is to listen.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Karen?

**[K]:** Yes, hello.

**[S]:** Hi Karen, it's Stefan from Claire's team. You filled out an application about getting some support with your weight. Does that ring a bell?

**[K]:** Oh. Yes. I did. *(nervous laugh)* I nearly didn't.

**[S]:** I'm really glad you did. So it's just a quick ten minutes, a chat, nothing scary. To see where you're at and if we can help. Sound okay?

**[K]:** Yes. Okay.

**[S]:** So what had you filling it in, in the end?

**[K]:** Well. I've always been, you know, a bit bigger. But it's been manageable. And then the last two years, with the menopause, it's just… everything's changed. I've put on two stone. And I'm doing the same things. I'm eating the same. And it just keeps going on.

**[S]:** Two stone, doing the same things. That must be so frustrating.

**[K]:** It is. It's like my body's not mine anymore.

**[S]:** "Not mine anymore." What do you mean by that?

**[K]:** *(pause)* I don't recognise myself. In photos. In the mirror. I avoid the mirror now, actually. I get dressed with my back to it.

**[S]:** With your back to it.

**[K]:** *(voice breaking)* Sorry. I didn't think I'd get upset. *(crying)*

**[S]:** *(gently)* Karen, please don't apologise. Take your time. There's no rush at all.

**[K]:** *(pause, sniffing)* Sorry. It's just. Nobody really asks, you know? My husband's lovely, but he just says "you look fine." And I'm not fine.

**[S]:** Yeah. "You look fine" when you don't feel fine. That's lonely.

**[K]:** *(quiet)* It is. Yes.

**[S]:** Thank you for telling me. Honestly.

**[K]:** *(laughs a little)* I've told you more than I've told him.

**[S]:** *(softly)* That's okay. Sometimes it's easier with someone you don't know.

**[K]:** It is.

**[S]:** Can I ask, is there something you're missing out on, because of how you feel?

**[K]:** My daughter's getting married in May. And I've been dreading the dress shopping. I haven't even started. She keeps asking. I keep saying "next month".

**[S]:** Next month.

**[K]:** I don't want to be the mother of the bride hiding at the back of the photos.

**[S]:** Yeah. That makes complete sense.

**[S]:** So it's not really about a number on the scales. It's about feeling like yourself again, and being in those wedding photos instead of hiding. Is that fair?

**[K]:** Yes. That's exactly it.

**[S]:** And over the last two years, what have you tried?

**[K]:** Slimming World. Twice. I lost half a stone, then it came back. I did the shakes for a bit. I walk the dog every day.

**[S]:** And has your GP said anything about the menopause side?

**[K]:** I'm on HRT now. She said it might help. It's early days.

**[S]:** Okay, good that you've got her on it. Our coaches aren't doctors, so anything medical stays with her. That okay?

**[K]:** Yes, of course.

**[S]:** Okay, Karen, a couple of quick questions about investing in this, just so I know what makes sense. Cool?

**[K]:** Yes.

**[S]:** What are you spending now, on Slimming World, the shakes, anything like that?

**[K]:** Slimming World was about £25 a month. The shakes were £60.

**[S]:** And work-wise, what's coming in?

**[K]:** About £2,300. My husband's about the same.

**[S]:** And are you able to put anything aside?

**[K]:** Yes, we're comfortable. Kids have left home. Well, nearly. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Nearly. And would you decide this yourself, or with him?

**[K]:** I'd tell him. But it's my decision. *(pause)* Actually, it's the first thing I've wanted just for me in a long time.

**[S]:** I love that. And if there was a coach who understood the menopause side, and worked with you week to week, would you be open to investing in yourself for that?

**[S]:** Okay. The next step is a call with Helen, she's one of our coaches. She's 52 and she went through it herself. She'll go through everything, the HRT, what you've tried, what's realistic before May, and honestly, she gets it. Sound good?

**[K]:** She's been through it? Oh, that's… yes. That would be good.

**[K]:** Evenings, after 6.

**[K]:** Monday. Before I talk myself out of it. *(laughs)*

**[K]:** *(gives it)*

**[S]:** Sent. And Karen, if you do talk yourself out of it, text me first. I'll talk you back in. *(laughs)*

## What to notice

- **"I nearly didn't." "I'm really glad you did."** It welcomes the courage it took to apply.
- **"Nothing scary."** One word that lowers the guard for anxious prospects.
- **When she cried, the setter got quieter and shorter.** "Please don't apologise. Take your time." No questions, no fixing, then silence. Let her come back.
- **"You look fine" when you don't feel fine. That's lonely.** That's naming the feeling she didn't say. It's not therapy, just one line.
- **No body comments, no number promises, a medical line.** "Anything medical stays with her [GP]." Simple and safe.
- **"Before I talk myself out of it."** She told you the risk. "Text me first. I'll talk you back in." It gives her a step before cancelling.
