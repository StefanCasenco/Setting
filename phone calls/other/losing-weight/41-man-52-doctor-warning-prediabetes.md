# 41 · Losing weight · Webinar attendee · Man, 52, doctor said "pre-diabetic"

**Prospect:** Phil, 52, HGV driver, UK · **Lead:** attended a "healthy after 50" webinar · **Length:** ~11 min · **Ends:** booked
**What happens:** He uses humour to hide being scared. The setter goes along with the jokes but keeps the door open.

---

**[S]:** Heyy… Phil?

**[P]:** Phil speaking. Unless you're selling double glazing. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* No double glazing, I promise. It's Stefan from Claire's team. You were on the webinar Tuesday night, the "healthy after 50" one. Does that ring a bell?

**[P]:** Ah, yes. The wife made me watch it. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Did she sit next to you to make sure?

**[P]:** With her arms crossed, mate. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Okay. Quick ten minutes, just to see where you're at. Sound fair?

**[P]:** Go on.

**[S]:** So why did your wife make you watch it? *(laughs)*

**[P]:** *(laughs)* Well. I had my check-up at the doctor's. And she said I'm "pre-diabetic". Whatever that means. And my blood pressure's up. And I need to lose three stone. So, you know. The usual. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** The usual. *(laughs)* And how did it feel, sitting there hearing that?

**[P]:** Ah, it's fine. Everyone's pre-something at my age. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Maybe. But your wife made you watch a webinar with her arms crossed. So I'm guessing she's not taking it as "fine".

**[P]:** *(pause)* No. She isn't. *(less laughing)* Her dad had diabetes. He lost a foot. She looked after him.

**[S]:** Oh. That's serious.

**[P]:** Yeah. So, when I came home and said "pre-diabetic", she went white. Didn't speak to me all evening.

**[S]:** And how about you, honestly? Not her. You.

**[P]:** *(long pause)* I suppose it frightened me a bit. I've got a granddaughter. She's two. I want to see her grow up. I don't want to be the grandad with the bad foot.

**[S]:** Yeah. That makes complete sense.

**[S]:** So the jokes are the jokes. But underneath it, it's about being around for your granddaughter, and not putting your wife through it again. Fair?

**[P]:** *(quiet)* Yeah. That's fair, mate.

**[S]:** And what does a normal day look like for you, eating-wise? HGV driver, I'm guessing that's not easy.

**[P]:** *(laughs)* It's a nightmare. Services, all day. Meal deals. Sausage rolls. Coffee with four sugars. I'm in the cab ten hours.

**[S]:** Four sugars. *(laughs)* Okay, that's a start right there. Have you tried anything before?

**[P]:** The wife put me on a diet once. Lasted a week. I was starving in the cab and caved at a Greggs.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Greggs always wins. So it's not willpower, it's that nothing's ever been built around life in a cab.

**[P]:** That's it, yeah. Nobody understands the cab.

**[S]:** And has the doctor given you a plan, medication, anything?

**[P]:** Metformin, maybe, if it doesn't come down in three months. She wants me to try first.

**[S]:** Okay. And just so you know, our coaches aren't doctors, so anything medical stays with her. Is that okay?

**[P]:** Yeah, course.

**[S]:** Okay, couple of quick ones about investing in this. What are you spending on food in the cab, roughly, a week?

**[P]:** *(laughs)* Don't tell the wife. About £70 a week.

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Secret's safe. And roughly what's coming in?

**[P]:** About £3,000 a month. The wife does part-time.

**[S]:** And are you comfortable enough to put some aside?

**[P]:** Yeah, we're alright. Mortgage is nearly paid.

**[S]:** And this would be a decision with your wife?

**[P]:** Oh, she'd want to be in on it. Arms crossed. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Bring the arms. And if there was a coach who built something around ten hours in a cab, and checked in every week, would you be open to investing in yourself for it?

**[P]:** Yeah. If it means no foot problems. Yeah.

**[S]:** Okay. Next step is a call with Gary, one of our coaches. He works with a lot of drivers and shift workers. He'll look at your day in the cab, what the doctor said, and what's realistic in three months. And your wife on the call, definitely. Sound good?

**[P]:** Yeah, she'll grill him. *(laughs)*

**[S]:** Good. When are you both home?

**[P]:** Saturday morning.

**[S]:** Gary's got Saturday at 9:30 or 11. Which one?

**[P]:** 9:30.

**[S]:** Saturday at 9:30. Best email?

**[P]:** The wife's. She manages all that. *(gives it)*

**[S]:** *(laughs)* Of course. Sent. And Phil, maybe two sugars this week instead of four. *(laughs)*

**[P]:** *(laughs)* Steady on.

## What to notice

- **Go along with the jokes at first. Don't kill the humour.** "Did she sit next to you to make sure?" matched his energy.
- **Then gently point at the gap between the joke and reality.** "Your wife made you watch with her arms crossed. So I'm guessing she's not taking it as 'fine'." It's still light, but it's true.
- **"And how about you, honestly? Not her. You."** Humorous people talk about others' feelings. Bring it back to him.
- **"So the jokes are the jokes. But underneath it…"** A summary that respects his humour and names the real thing.
- **"Greggs always wins."** A small joke that removes shame from failing a diet.
- **Medical line, said once, simply.** He has a doctor and possible medication. That's hers, not yours.
