## The moves that show up again and again

**Lubricant (context before a question)**
- *Their words:* "You said 'everything is you'. Is that the main thing?"
- *The reason:* "I'm asking so I don't book you into something that has you eating noodles."
- *Normalise:* "Most people I speak to have a story behind the number."
- *Options:* "Put money on a trade, or more watching so far?"
- *Real life:* "Football boots are a scam, man." / "The car always breaks at the worst time."

**Taking the frame back**
- Agree with the true part, then ask the real question. "Could be, 200 hours is a lot. So what had you filling in an application?"
- Trade with a yapper: "You answer mine, the coach answers yours, with real numbers. Deal?"
- Respect what they already chose: "If it's working for you, crack on. Can I ask…"
- Give the exit: "If it's not for you, say the word and you're off." People stay when they feel free to go.

**Defusing tension**
- Don't defend: "I'd be fuming too." / "You should be."
- Name the feeling lightly: "I can tell this is really bugging you."
- Laugh with them, never at them.
- When they cry or go quiet: say less. "Take your time."
- Couples arguing: "You're both right," with one specific reason each. Don't solve it, that's the closer's job.

**Setter, not closer**
- One pain, one feeling, one cost, then move on.
- No promises: results, scores, points, income, kilos.
- Health and credit: "Our coaches aren't doctors." / "Nobody honest can promise numbers."
- Protect them: retirement money, emergency funds and baby money stay where they are.

## How to use them

1. Pick a niche you're dialling, and read 2–3 calls out loud, setter side only.
2. Cover the setter lines. At each one, say what you'd ask before you look.
3. Read "What to notice". Find the lubricant, the frame-back and the defuse in the call.
4. Roleplay the situation with Kevin or Atefur, using the "Find a call by what happens" table. Ask them to *be* that prospect.
5. All names, people and companies are invented.
